# Why human code is noisy

Human code is noisy because every channel the medium gives a human author is a lossy workaround for something the medium cannot say: sequential text cannot state a partial order, so the author picks a total one; the language cannot name a plan, so the author copies its instances; nothing records intent, so the author smears it across identifiers that other authors would choose differently 93% of the time; nothing proves a check unnecessary, so nobody deletes it; and nothing makes an addition cost its proposer what it costs the field, so languages and organizations grow by a ratchet. The measured evidence sorts the noise into three layers with different fates. The artifact layer is dead weight for everyone: formatting is **13–15% of tokens in Java/C#/C++** and can be stripped with no significant accuracy loss for ten LLMs ([Hidden Cost of Readability, arXiv Aug 2025, ICSE 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.13666v1)), and layout showed no fMRI effect on humans either ([Peitek et al. 2017](https://www.se.cs.uni-saarland.de/publications/docs/SPP+17.pdf)). The structural layer is where the only measured human cost lives: textual size, vocabulary size and data-flow dependency reach correlate with brain activation at **τ ≈ 0.4**, while McCabe complexity correlates with nothing ([Peitek et al., ICSE 2021](https://www.se.cs.uni-saarland.de/publications/docs/PAP+21.pdf)), and **97% of real non-deadlock concurrency bugs** are unenforced atomicity or order assumptions ([Lu et al., ASPLOS 2008](https://www.cs.columbia.edu/~junfeng/10fa-e6998/papers/concurrency-bugs.pdf)). The intent layer is the one noise source that cannot be removed: two developers choose the same name with **median probability 6.9%** ([Feitelson et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.07487)), comments co-evolve with code **7–13%** of the time ([Wen et al. 2019](https://www.inf.usi.ch/faculty/lanza/PUBS/P/Wen2019a.pdf)), yet frontier models lose **11–29 points** of summarisation accuracy and up to 24 points of execution-prediction accuracy when identifiers are removed ([When Names Disappear, arXiv Oct 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.03178)). For the Reality Code Engine (RCE) this says: let the Form absorb order, locality, idiom and reachability (all decidable over state, events and a total step); let the projection absorb layout and idiom-per-community; keep intent as a structured, checked record from which the projection renders names; and govern the spec by a mechanical admission check, because the organizational evidence (org metrics predicting Vista failures at **86.2% precision / 84.0% recall**, beating every code metric ([Nagappan et al., ICSE 2008](https://www.cs.umd.edu/users/basili/publications/proceedings/P125.pdf)); Go declining **40.9%** of 1,095 proposals ([arXiv Oct 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06984v1)); WG21 at ~250 attendees and >500 papers a year with nothing removed ([Stroustrup, HOPL-IV 2020](https://www.stroustrup.com/hopl20main-p5-p-bfc9cd4--final.pdf))) shows that model authorship raises the generation rate without changing the rule. Three questions the design depends on have never been measured: whether a model reasons correctly from a nameless canonical Form, what fraction of statement pairs in ordinary code commute, and whether reversible deletion moves the **20%** rate at which developers actually delete code they have been told is dead ([Haas et al., TOSEM 2020](https://teamscale.com/hubfs/26978363/Publications/2020-unnecessary-code-tosem.pdf?hsLang=en)). RCE can run all three with tooling it already has.

Evidence grading throughout: **measured** means fetched and read with numbers; **canonical, not fetched** means a primary source cited from prior knowledge whose claims should be treated as attribution, not verified quotation; **vendor** means industry data with undisclosed sampling. Sample sizes in program-comprehension work are small (N = 6 to 63); effect directions are robust, effect sizes are not.

### Working memory holds four chunks, and only size, vocabulary and dependency reach are measured costs

The capacity figure that matters is Cowan's, not Miller's: when rehearsal and chunking are blocked, working memory holds **3–5 chunks, central estimate 4** ([Cowan, BBS 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11515286/)); Miller's 7±2 is the canonical-not-fetched figure that chunking inflates ([Miller 1956](http://psychclassics.yorku.ca/Miller/)). The one study that tested code metrics against brain activation directly (N = 19 students, 16 snippets, 41 metrics, fMRI) found that **McCabe cyclomatic complexity correlates with nothing (τ ≈ 0.09 with correctness, 0.04–0.09 with activation)**, while lines of code (τ = 0.43 with BA21 activation, −0.46 with correctness), Halstead vocabulary (τ = 0.32–0.40 across BA6/21/39, −0.45 with correctness) and the data-flow metric DepDegree (τ = 0.41 BA21, −0.41 correctness, "strongest correlation to brain activation") are the only quantities that track load; the authors' conclusion is that "a code's textual size drives programmers' attention" and "vocabulary size burdens programmers' working memory" ([Peitek et al., ICSE 2021](https://www.se.cs.uni-saarland.de/publications/docs/PAP+21.pdf)). The dependency-reach finding has a 1986 ancestor: Letovsky and Soloway's think-aloud study of six professionals on a 300-line Fortran program found that when a plan's lines are scattered, readers form plausible but wrong local interpretations (a counter increment copied without seeing it was a distant dirty-bit check; `IREC=0` misread as a not-found marker), and that the same readers **failed to notice existing redundancy**, two overflow checks duplicating each other and an assignment that made a prior step unnecessary ([Letovsky & Soloway 1986](https://www.cs.kent.edu/~jmaletic/cs69995-PC/papers/letovsky-1986-software.pdf)). Comprehension recruits the bilateral multiple-demand (working-memory and control) system, not the language system: Δβ = 2.17 for the MD system against 0.78 for language on Python, and swapping English identifiers for Japanese changed language-system response by Δβ = 0.03 (p = .84) ([Ivanova et al. 2020, via arXiv 2304.12373](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.12373)). The human-designed readability metrics do not predict any of this: across 121 metrics, 63 developers and 444 evaluations, no single metric reached **|τ| > 0.16** with measured understandability, the best combined model reached τ = 0.37, and the developers' own stated weighting of identifier quality was not supported by their data ([Scalabrino et al., TSE 2019](https://www.cs.wm.edu/~denys/pubs/TSE'19-Understandability.pdf)).

This is cognition, and it is self-defeating cognition: the noise humans add (extra names, defensive checks, duplicated plans) inflates exactly the two quantities, token count and vocabulary, that the measurement says cost them. For RCE the disposition follows directly. The Form already localises state transitions in a total step over explicit state, which attacks DepDegree, the one metric with a medium correlation to brain load; the surface must then present each plan's lines together and minimise distinct symbols per view, since Cowan's four chunks is the budget and Halstead vocabulary is the measured spend. Nesting-based complexity metrics can be dropped from any RCE readability claim; they measure nothing the brain pays for. The gap is that every number above is comprehension, not production: no fMRI or eye-tracking study measures the load of *writing* code, and no study measures load as a function of the number of concurrent state variables, which is the quantity a Form's state declaration makes explicit.

### Sequential text forces a total order; 97% of concurrency bugs are the bill

The largest real-world concurrency-bug study (105 bugs from MySQL, Apache, Mozilla, OpenOffice; 74 non-deadlock, 31 deadlock) found **atomicity violations in 69% and order violations in 32% of non-deadlock bugs, 97% in total**; **96% of all 105 bugs involve at most two threads**, 66% a single variable, roughly 90% at most four memory accesses; only 27% of non-deadlock fixes touched locks; and **17 of 57 Mozilla bugs had at least one buggy intermediate patch**. The authors' explicit conclusion: "most programmers think sequentially and therefore they make mistakes easily when writing concurrent programs"; programmers "assume small code regions run atomically, or assume an order between threads without enforcing it"; locks cannot express "A before B" ([Lu et al., ASPLOS 2008](https://www.cs.columbia.edu/~junfeng/10fa-e6998/papers/concurrency-bugs.pdf)). Eye-tracking gives the reading-side mirror: novices read code in story order about 75% of the time, experts about 60%, with experts making longer saccades (134 px vs 80 px) and fixating fewer elements (41.3% vs 52.4%); "the Execution Order model better explains the experts' reading approach than Story Order does" ([Busjahn et al. 2015](https://huang.isis.vanderbilt.edu/cs8395-f23/paper/linear-order-eye.pdf)). The cognitive-dimensions vocabulary names the mechanism without measuring it: *premature commitment* is a notation forcing "a decision before the proper information is available", *hidden dependencies* are links not visible in the text, and the framework warns that "relaxing order constraints may make notation diffuse or increase errors, and it requires a more sophisticated system" ([Blackwell & Green, CD tutorial](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/~afb21/CognitiveDimensions/CDtutorial.pdf)). Lee's "The Problem with Threads" is the canonical-not-fetched theory piece: threads discard determinism by default and the programmer prunes nondeterminism by hand ([Lee 2006](https://www2.eecs.berkeley.edu/Pubs/TechRpts/2006/EECS-2006-1.pdf)).

This is cognition induced by the medium, not a fixed trait: sequential text cannot carry a partial order, so the author picks a total one, and the reader cannot tell required order from incidental order. The ≤2-threads, ≤4-accesses profile means the failure is representational, not a scaling problem; a total step over explicit state with events as inputs removes the atomicity-assumption class by construction, which is what the earlier design document's partial-order log (Foata layers, deps-hashed entries, state hashes at cuts) already commits to. The new constraint from this evidence is Green's trade-off: a Form that makes dependencies explicit will look *more diffuse* to a human, so the surface must re-hide that diffuseness, and the right hiding is an execution-order view, because that is how the readers who matter actually read. What nobody has measured is the number RCE most wants: the fraction of statement pairs in ordinary sequential code that commute. The parallelisation literature holds this implicitly and was not searched; it is the second untested question below.

### Copying is mostly rational, and the cognitive part is plan transfer the language cannot name

The clone literature splits into two populations that the RCE paper's "70% of GitHub is clones" figure conflates. The GitHub-scale number is real: across 4.5M non-fork projects and 482M files, only ~85M were unique, so a given file has an 82% chance of a duplicate; **file-level exact duplication is 94% for JavaScript, 73% C++, 71% Python, 40% Java**; but the dominant causes are committed `node_modules` (6% of projects, ~70% of all JS files, "the single biggest cause"), vendored libraries, and generated code (Axis, JAXB, Django, Angular scaffolds), which dominated the sampled near-clone pairs ([Lopes et al., OOPSLA 2017, via Morning Paper](https://blog.acolyer.org/2017/11/20/dejavu-a-map-of-code-duplicates-on-github/)). That population is organizational and tooling-driven, not cognitive. The cognitive population is the one Kapser and Godfrey taxonomised in Apache httpd 2.2.4 (312 kLOC) and Gnumeric 1.6.3 (327 kLOC): eleven clone patterns in four groups, **forking** (platform variation), **templating** (boilerplate the language cannot abstract, API call protocols, idioms), **customization** (bug workarounds, replicate-and-specialise when modifying shared code is risky or owned by others) and **exact match**; **71.2% of 59 Apache clone groups at a 30-token threshold were rated beneficial**, 13.6% harmful, but the other samples varied widely (Apache/60-token 42% good/39% harmful; Gnumeric/30-token 33% good/57% harmful) ([Kapser & Godfrey, EMSE 2008](https://cormack.uwaterloo.ca/~migod/papers/2008/emse08-ClonePatterns.pdf)). Stack Overflow copying adds a provenance layer: 3.3–11.9% of GitHub projects contain files referencing SO, at most a quarter of copied snippets are attributed, and almost half of surveyed developers admit copying without attribution ([Baltes & Diehl, EMSE 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.02938)). The oldest observation is still the sharpest: a professional in 1986 copied a counter increment into a new routine because he could not see its distant purpose, copy substituting for understanding of a delocalised plan ([Letovsky & Soloway 1986](https://www.cs.kent.edu/~jmaletic/cs69995-PC/papers/letovsky-1986-software.pdf)), and Soloway and Ehrlich's plan theory explains why: experts carry stereotyped solution fragments, and a plan the language cannot express as a construct must be re-instantiated by hand every time ([Soloway & Ehrlich 1984, summary](https://research.cs.queensu.ca/home/cordy/cisc860/Biblio/hurd/misc/soloway84.pdf)).

Three origins, three dispositions. The vendoring and generation population is organizational and tooling noise; content-addressed definitions (the Unison move the earlier design document adopted) make it vanish, because an identical body has one key. The templating population is a language failure: the Form should hold plans as parametrised, content-addressed definitions so the surface can render the community's idiom per instance without the Form holding the instance. The customization population is loss aversion in the organizational setting ("owned by others", "too costly to test"): copy-and-specialise is the reversible, low-blame move, and a system where reversibility is cheap removes the motive. The gap: no experiment measures the cognitive cost saved by copy-paste against re-derivation; "copying as cognitive economy" is an inference from the motivational taxonomies, and Roy and Cordy's commonly quoted 7–23% clone prevalence was not verified.

### Names are the only intent channel, chosen at 6.9% agreement and decoded at 97%

Naming is a low-agreement, high-dependency channel. Across 47 naming instances and 334 participants, the **median probability that two developers choose the same name is 6.9%** (range 2.5–64.6%), yet given names are interpreted consistently (96.4–97.8% read `arrangeFilesByName(files)` as returning an ordered list) except on genuine technical ambiguities; the authors' three-step model (choose concepts, choose words, choose order) multiplies variability at each step, and experienced developers' names are 1.75 characters longer with more concepts ([Feitelson et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.07487)). The free-text intent channel decays measurably: across 1,500 Java projects and 3.3M commits, **code and comments co-evolve in 7% of method-comment cases and 13% of class-comment cases**, and only 13–20% of code changes trigger any comment change at all ([Wen et al. 2019](https://www.inf.usi.ch/faculty/lanza/PUBS/P/Wen2019a.pdf)). Models depend on the channel more than humans do. Four semantics-preserving obfuscations (alpha-renaming, `llllIII`, cross-domain terms, misleading names) dropped class-level summarisation from **87.3 to 58.7 for GPT-4o** and 87.7 to 76.7 for DeepSeek V3, and execution prediction fell too (GPT-4o ClassEval 76.6→70.2 under alpha-renaming, Llama 4 Maverick on LiveCodeBench 80.2→56.4 under cross-domain names); the authors call names "semantic anchors" and read the execution drop as "identifier leakage", names used as retrieval cues for memorised patterns rather than reasoning from structure ([When Names Disappear, arXiv 2510.03178, Oct 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.03178)). Humans, by contrast, do not process identifiers as language at the neural level (Ivanova's Δβ = 0.03 for the Japanese swap). The canonical-not-fetched frame is Naur's: the program text is not the program; the theory lives in the programmer and is not recoverable from the text ([Naur 1985](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~remzi/Naur.pdf)); Zave and Jackson locate what the text drops, the designated terminology and environment assumptions that give a specification meaning ([Zave & Jackson, TOSEM 1997](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/237432.237434)).

This is cognition overlaid with culture. Names are a lossy, idiosyncratic encoding that readers nevertheless decode reliably, so they are overloaded with every kind of intent the language gives no other place for; comments are the same channel without even the decoding guarantee, which is why they drift to 87–93% non-co-evolution. The earlier design document put names in an unhashed side table and positions in the hash. This evidence sharpens that: the side table should hold the *concept* (Feitelson's step 1) as a structured record bound to the content-addressed definition, and the projection should perform steps 2 and 3, word choice and ordering, per reader and per language. A checked intent channel (the logged event vocabulary, invariants, `intent` plus `example` at the gate) is the only one that survives, because free text decouples within a few commits when nothing checks it. The evidence also cuts the other way: current models need names, so a machine-facing projection cannot simply alpha-rename; the Form must either render names from the concept record or demonstrate that a model can reason from the nameless Form, which is the first untested question. One cited figure is not verified: the commonly reported ~19% faster defect finding with full-word identifiers versus abbreviations ([Hofmeister et al. 2017, via mapping study](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.11102)) was not fetched from the primary source.

### Nobody deletes because proving a check unnecessary needs knowledge nobody has

The retention numbers are stark. In three production systems with usage recording, **files executed at least once over 6–16 months were 42%, 46% and 40%**, so 54–60% of files were never run; earlier work found ~25% of methods unused over two years and 28% of features unused over five months; 34% of 50 recommended chunks were confirmed unnecessary by their owners; and **developers deleted only 10 of 50 (20%) shortly after being shown them**. The stated reasons are a loss-aversion profile verbatim: "no one was familiar with" old code, "not confident enough", migration code kept "to avoid any risk", Jenkins would remove nothing because of unknown plug-in dependents. The authors' hypothesis is that "the risk of code deletion was bigger than the benefit of it" because deletion earns no recognition; and the fear is rational, since the UCDetector dead-code warnings were **32% correct (24/76)** and 29% of recommended classes were still reachable ([Haas et al., TOSEM 2020](https://teamscale.com/hubfs/26978363/Publications/2020-unnecessary-code-tosem.pdf?hsLang=en)). Defensive accretion has its own trace in the concurrency data: 19 of 74 non-deadlock fixes were added condition checks rather than removal of the race, and fixes layered on buggy fixes in 17 of 57 Mozilla cases ([Lu et al. 2008](https://www.cs.columbia.edu/~junfeng/10fa-e6998/papers/concurrency-bugs.pdf)). Safety regimes institutionalise the same checks as ceremony: JPL's Power of 10 mandates fixed loop bounds, no recursion, at least two assertions per function, all return values checked, zero warnings ([Holzmann 2006, Wikipedia summary; primary not fetched](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Power_of_10:_Rules_for_Developing_Safety-Critical_Code)). Prospect theory (losses weigh roughly twice gains) and status-quo bias are canonical-not-fetched, and no study applies them quantitatively to deletion decisions ([Kahneman & Tversky 1979](https://www.jstor.org/stable/1914185)); the Haas interview data is qualitative.

This is cognition (loss aversion) amplified by organization (no recognition for deletion, unknown external dependents, blame). The structure is an asymmetry of evidence: proving a check unnecessary needs global reachability knowledge, while keeping it costs nothing visible. In a Form with a total step and an explicit event set, unreachability is a decidable property of the Form, and the defensive check `if (x != null)` is a locally provable fragment of a globally unknown invariant that the totality proof makes the object. Two dispositions: defensive checks that re-prove locally what the total step proves globally are let go as text, with their *function*, evidence for a certifier, preserved as attached proof artefacts (the safety-culture ceremony is a substitute for proof, and auditors will need the proof in a reviewable shape); and deletion becomes reversible by construction, since the content-addressed history keeps the deleted body under its key. This is the direct test of the design invariant that reversibility replaces approval, and it is the third untested question: whether reversible, checked deletion moves the 20% rate. The gap worth naming is that rates of *redundant* checks, as opposed to unreachable code, were not found anywhere; Letovsky's two duplicated overflow checks remain the only concrete instance.

### Readability is familiarity, and familiarity is a loop with the training corpus

The readability literature decomposes into three layers once the measurements are placed side by side. Layout and whitespace: no significant fMRI difference between pretty-printed and disrupted layout, nor for beacons (N = 11, low power, "no evidence of benefit" rather than "proven useless") ([Peitek et al. 2017](https://www.se.cs.uni-saarland.de/publications/docs/SPP+17.pdf)); 13–15% of tokens in brace languages, 4% in Python, strippable with ≤ 4.2% Pass@1 change ([Hidden Cost 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.13666v1)). Perceived readability: Buse and Weimer's learned model from 120 annotators tracks line length, identifier count and indentation and correlates with defects and churn ([Buse & Weimer, TSE 2010, numbers from prior knowledge](https://web.eecs.umich.edu/~weimerw/p/weimer-tse2010-readability-preprint.pdf)), but Scalabrino's result is that perceived readability is not measured understandability (no metric above τ = 0.16). Familiarity: in top-starred Java and Python projects, original code was assigned lower surprisal than meaning-preserving rewrites (Java arithmetic swaps roughly 1.68× more probable under a global n-gram model, ~8× under a cache model), and 50 human raters agreed with the language model's preference **61.9% of the time (65.6% by majority vote)**, ~80% on relational swaps, with humans over-preferring extra parentheses; the authors' core claim is that developers "prefer it to be coded in more familiar forms" ([Casalnuovo et al. 2019/2020](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1910.03704)). Novices given Perl or Java were not significantly more accurate than with "Randomo", a placebo language whose keywords were drawn at random, while Quorum, Python and Ruby beat the placebo; `for`, `while` and `foreach` were among the least intuitive tokens ([Stefik & Siebert, TOCE 2013](https://neverworkintheory.org/2014/01/29/stefik-siebert-syntax.html)). The expertise side confirms that recognition, not capacity, is what expertise buys: experts' advantage is confined to plan-like programs and collapses on unplan-like ones ([Soloway & Ehrlich 1984](https://research.cs.queensu.ca/home/cordy/cisc860/Biblio/hurd/misc/soloway84.pdf)), Chase and Simon's chess masters recall real positions and not random ones (canonical, not fetched; [Chase & Simon 1973](https://doi.org/10.1016/0010-0285(73)90004-2)), and developer background explained more variance in understandability than any snippet property ([Scalabrino 2019](https://www.cs.wm.edu/~denys/pubs/TSE'19-Understandability.pdf)).

This is culture, with a cognitive substrate: what reads as "natural" is what the reader has seen most, and since models were trained on human code, human readability and low model surprisal are today largely the same quantity, which the geometry atlas's naturalness discussion (Hindle, Rahman, Casalnuovo as corrections to RCE §2.1) already noted from the entropy side. A canonical Form changes the training distribution and therefore moves the equilibrium; it does not escape it. Disposition: layout is let go entirely (the earlier design document's "indentation trade" is confirmed and the Form JSON remains the compact serialisation); community-canonical idioms are kept in the surface, because Soloway's collapse result means a projection that emits merely correct code will read as unplan-like to every expert and cost them their only advantage. A projection must emit the target community's idiom, not an equivalent. Expertise does not compress the text; it compresses the reader's model, and that is the fact a per-community projection exploits. The folklore that experts write less code has no traceable source (flagged below).

### Organizations ship their boundaries; org metrics beat every code metric at predicting failure

Conway's 1968 claim that organizations "are constrained to produce designs which are copies of the communication structures of these organizations" is canonical, not fetched ([Conway](http://www.melconway.com/Home/Conways_Law.html)); the two strongest tests are measured. On Windows Vista (3,404 binaries, >50 MLOC, several thousand engineers, six months of post-release failures, 50 random splits), eight organizational metrics (number of engineers, ex-engineers, edit frequency, depth of master ownership, organizational intersection factor, and others) predicted failure-prone binaries at **86.2% precision and 84.0% recall**, against 78.6/79.9 for code churn, 79.3/66.0 for complexity, 74.4/69.9 for dependencies, 83.8/54.4 for coverage and 73.8/62.9 for pre-release bugs ([Nagappan, Murphy & Basili, ICSE 2008](https://www.cs.umd.edu/users/basili/publications/proceedings/P125.pdf)). Across five matched open-source versus commercial product pairs holding function constant, the commercially built product had higher propagation cost (mean share of files affected by a change to a random file) in every pair, **7.74% vs 47.14%** for financial management, 8.25% vs 41.77% word processing, 23.62% vs 54.31% spreadsheet, 7.18% vs 22.59% Linux vs Solaris, 11.30% vs 43.23% database, most at p < 0.001, a factor of 2.3 to 6.1 from the table (the authors' "up to eight" uses a different measure) ([MacCormack, Baldwin & Rusnak, Research Policy 2012](https://dash.harvard.edu/bitstream/handle/1/34403525/maccormack,baldwin,rusnak_exploring-the-duality.pdf)). Technical debt concentrates where boundaries are drawn: of 536 completing respondents across three organizations, **54% ranked bad architecture choices in their top three debt sources** and 73% of free-text examples were architectural; 65% had no debt-management practice and 75% agreed debt had consumed "a painful chunk" of resources ([Ernst et al., ESEC/FSE 2015](https://cs.unibg.it/esecfse_proceedings/fse15/p50-ernst.pdf)). Growth in nine long-lived C programs over 705 releases was sub-linear in release count on maintenance branches (exponents 0.25–0.56) and super-linear only on development branches (Bison 1.65, Samba dev 2.34), with defect density falling, so Lehman's continuing-change and continuing-growth laws hold and declining-quality does not ([Neamtiu, Xie & Chen 2013](https://www.cs.kent.edu/~jmaletic/cs63902/Papers/jsme11neamtiu.pdf)). The interface economics are stated, not measured: an interface "is a public good with private costs", so whoever documents units, ranges and versions pays privately for everyone's benefit and the check is under-provided ([RCE interface-economics essay](https://research.openie.dev/papers/interface-economics/)); the dollar figures in circulation (CISQ's $2.41T for 2022, Stripe's $85B/year) are model estimates the essay itself refuses to total. Parnas's prescription, decompose around what is likely to change and hide it so change does not cross a handoff, is canonical, not fetched ([Parnas 1972](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/361598.361623)).

This is organization, and the striking result is that interface noise is partly exogenous to the technical problem: matched pairs hold function constant, and the org that built the code sets the blast radius. The Vista metrics (org intersection factor, depth of master ownership) are proxies for how many organizational boundaries a binary crosses, which is the RCE three-body claim that failures sit at handoffs, now with a measurement behind it. Disposition: the Form moves interface assumptions (units, ranges, timing, versions) into checked events in the log, so a crossing is a logged, replayable fact rather than an unwritten assumption; and a boundary that mirrors the org chart is let go, since nothing in the Form should know which team wrote a region. Growth is driven by the admission rate of new intent, not by time, which is the organizational statement of RCE §6.0's "new intent is what a supplier provides once". The causality direction (org shapes code, or code shapes org) is unresolved by either study, and no replication of the Vista result at a second company was fetched.

### Languages accrete by enthusiasm asymmetry and stay small by rule, never by taste

The accretion regime is documented by its own chair. WG21 grew from ~25 people at the founding meeting in December 1989 to "as many as 250 people" per face-to-face meeting for C++17/20, from a membership about twice that, with the paper collection growing by "more than 500 papers a year" and a 2018 pre-meeting mailing about three times the words of Shakespeare's complete works; "the feature set grows contiguously", nothing significant is removed; "most committee members like to see improvement; that is, to see features added"; "it is easier to summon enthusiasm for something new than for opposing it" ([Stroustrup, HOPL-IV 2020](https://www.stroustrup.com/hopl20main-p5-p-bfc9cd4--final.pdf)). His 2018 "Remember the Vasa!" letter said it directly: "individually, many proposals make sense. Together they are insanity to the point of endangering the future of C++" ([The Register, June 2018](https://www.theregister.com/2018/06/18/bjarne_stroustrup_c_plus_plus/)). Hoare's 1980 Turing lecture is the canonical warning, read here through a summary: the price of reliability "is the pursuit of the utmost simplicity", "no committee will ever do this until it is too late", and a technically unsound project can still be "doomed to succeed" ([Hoare 1980, via Morning Paper](https://blog.acolyer.org/2016/09/07/the-emperors-old-clothes/)). Wirth's 1995 plea names "customers' ignorance of features that are essential versus nice-to-have" and the mistaking of complexity for sophistication ([Wirth 1995, via Wikipedia summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wirth%27s_law)). The decline regime is measured. Go's process is explicitly "slanted towards declining proposals", requires a change to help roughly ten times as many developers as it hurts, and selected 3 of ~120 open Go 2 proposals for Go 1.13 ([Go blog, Nov 2018](https://go.dev/blog/go2-here-we-come)); over 1,095 proposals with 785 final decisions, **448 (40.9%) were declined and 337 (30.8%) accepted**, median resolution 31 days declined and 37 accepted, 14.7% resubmitted of which 53.3% were declined again, with coded reasons of poor feasibility 22.8%, deprecated 18.1%, limited use cases 13.2%, breaking Go's principles 11.4%, existing alternatives 10.3% ([arXiv 2510.06984, Oct 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06984v1)). Lua's composition regime kept the implementation at **~25,000 lines of C, a ~200 KB binary and a ~100-page manual** by the rule "mechanisms instead of policies": one mechanism per aspect (tables, functions, coroutines), modules, objects and exceptions built from them rather than added, with the acknowledged cost of "many incompatible object systems" ([Ierusalimschy et al., CACM 2018](https://www.lua.org/doc/cacm2018.pdf)). Elm's freeze regime: stable release 0.19.1 dated October 2019 with no later release, kernel-code access removed from non-core packages, compiler-enforced semantic versioning ([Elm, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elm_(programming_language))). The market does not prune any of this: adoption is driven by libraries and existing code, with intrinsic features ranking far lower and growth proceeding "niche by niche" ([Meyerovich & Rabkin, OOPSLA 2013, via summary; primary unreachable](https://vivekhaldar.com/articles/empirical-analysis-of-programming-language-adoption)), so bloat is not selected against on the timescale at which it accrues, and the Liebowitz–Margolis critique of QWERTY means "worse languages win" should be stated as "languages with more existing code win" ([Liebowitz & Margolis 1990, abstract](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=1069950)).

This is organization, with an incentive asymmetry at its core: feature benefit is private and immediate (proposer, promotion, product), cost is diffuse and deferred (every implementer, teacher and reader), which is the mirror image of the interface public-good problem. Every small language replaced reviewer taste with a rule about what counts as admissible. The earlier design document adopted a Go-style decline-biased proposal rule and a dated declined list; this evidence supplies its baseline metrics and its rubric, and the governance section below turns it into spec rules. The gaps: no source quantifies the defect or complexity effect of freeze versus decline-bias versus committee, so the comparison is structural; C++ standard page counts by edition were not retrieved from a primary source; and the "Glasgow 2014" manual page-count study could not be found at all (flagged below).

### Tribes run on belief, and English is a conformity tax on the non-anglophone majority

The clearest documented case of language culture operating on belief is the Ray-to-Berger episode. The FSE 2014 study reporting eleven languages significantly associated with defects was reproduced and reanalysed: 27,450 duplicate commits, a ~36% false-positive rate in keyword-based bug labelling, only 16 of 41 "TypeScript" projects containing TypeScript, sixteen p-values against one uncorrected cutoff; after correction **four languages survived with an "exceedingly small" effect**, yet of thirty citing papers, **26 had already read the result as causal** ([Berger et al., TOPLAS 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.10220); [Rebuttal², arXiv 1911.11894](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1911.11894)). The original authors dispute the false-positive estimate and the correction choice ([Ray et al. rebuttal](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.07393)). The weak Whorfian claim is measured: of 450 cross-language Stack Overflow posts across 18 languages, **276 (61%) carried incorrect assumptions from a prior language**, and interviewed professionals described having to "suppress old habits" ([Shrestha, Barik & Parnin, ICSE 2020 / CACM 2022](https://cacmb4.acm.org/magazines/2022/3/258915-here-we-go-again)); Dijkstra's strong form ("mentally mutilated beyond hope of regeneration") is canonical rhetoric, not fetched ([EWD498](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/users/EWD/transcriptions/EWD04xx/EWD498.html)). English dominance is quantified from both sides. Of 840 non-native learners from 86 countries, the barriers were instructional materials, technical communication, and learning English and programming simultaneously ([Guo, CHI 2018](https://pg.ucsd.edu/publications/non-native-english-speakers-learning-programming_CHI-2018.txt)); of 1.1M GitHub users, 12.7% wrote non-English commit messages, but **93% of Chinese-speaker projects use ASCII-only identifiers**, non-Latin-script users use English identifiers in 82% of projects against 12% for Latin-alphabet users, and only 0.6% of right-to-left-country repositories contain Arabic or Hebrew identifiers or comments ([Piech & Abu-El-Haija 2019](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1909.04556)); non-English identifiers and comments appeared in 5 of 10 industrial systems and none of 13 open-source ones, with non-English identifiers at 28–45% in three systems, a survivorship effect rather than a preference ([Pawelka & Juergens, ICSME 2015](https://teamscale.com/hubfs/26978363/Publications/2015-is-this-code-written-in-english-a-study-of-the-natural-language-of-comments-and-identifiers-in-practice.pdf?hsLang=en)). The formatting tribes have one precedent for disarmament: "gofmt's style is no one's favorite, yet gofmt is everyone's favorite" ([Go proverbs, not fetched](https://go-proverbs.github.io/)). The history of removing the human from the surface is a social-failure record: a 450-respondent, 22-interview study of model-driven engineering found productivity gains mostly of 20–30% that "don't justify the training costs and organizational change", "most projects fail at scale-up", code gurus resist because they fear losing importance, and generated code that humans cannot read raises certification costs; the successes were narrow DSLs built bottom-up by domain teams ([Hutchinson et al., ICSE 2011, via the authors' InfoQ summary](https://www.infoq.com/articles/the-state-of-practice-in-model-driven-engineering)).

This is culture. The cost of surface multiplicity is a measured 61% interference rate, which is an argument for one Form with derived projections rather than independently learned surfaces; the ASCII-transliteration behaviour (93%, 99.4%) is conformity to tooling, not preference, which is an argument for content-addressed identity with names surfaced in the reader's language; and the MDE record is a warning that a projected surface humans cannot audit will be resisted by status actors and priced by certifiers, so the Form must be *more* auditable than the surface, with proofs attached, not less. Disposition: keep a localised, usability-tested projection per reader (Stefik–Siebert's method, Guo's and Piech's localisation); move domain vocabulary (the insurance terms of industrial code) to metadata bound to definitions, never to keyword tokens; let go the keyword language, the language tribe as a quality signal, and the identity argument, redirected to projections the way gofmt made style irrelevant. No peer-reviewed study of tribalism per se exists; the genre is talks and essays. No study measures whether non-native naming raises defect rates, and no 2024–2026 study tests model comprehension of non-English identifiers.

### Model authorship inverts the artefact layer, not the reasoning layer, and inherits the tribe

Under frontier models, human formatting is already pure cost: across ten LLMs on fill-in-the-middle tasks in Java, C#, C++ and Python, removing formatting cut input tokens **24.5% on average (Java 34.9%, C++ 31.1%, C# 25.3%, Python 6.5%)** with no statistically significant Pass@1 change (largest drop 4.2%, Phi-3.5 on C++; DeepSeek-V3 79.1→80.0; GPT-4o on Python rose 66.4→71.5); output tokens fell only ~2.5% unprompted because "LLMs tend to maintain formatting conventions in their outputs regardless of input formatting", prompting cut GPT-4o output 27.2%, fine-tuning on fifty unformatted Java samples cut output 35.9% (Gemini) and 24.8% (GPT-4o) at unchanged Pass@1, and one prompt collapsed Gemini-1.5 on C++ from 67.2 to 11.1; the authors released a bidirectional formatter with 100% AST equivalence ([Hidden Cost of Readability, arXiv 2508.13666, ICSE 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.13666v1)). The reasoning layer does not invert: forcing JSON output during reasoning dropped GSM8K from **76.6 to 49.3 for GPT-3.5 Turbo, 86.5 to 23.4 for Claude 3 Haiku** (60% parsing errors), 74.7 to 48.9 for Llama 3 8B, with the mechanism traced to "100% of GPT 3.5 Turbo JSON-mode responses placed the 'answer' key before the 'reason' key", and removing the schema from the prompt restored Claude from 23.4 to 87.0 ([Tam et al., EMNLP Industry 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.02442v1)). The inherited tribe is measurable: eight models used Python for **90–97% of problems when the language was unspecified**, 58% of project-initialisation tasks where Python is unsuitable, never Rust for any of five high-performance tasks, and contradicted their own language recommendations in 83% of cases ([LLMs Love Python, arXiv Mar 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.17181v1)). The vendor signal on volume is directionally consistent but unverifiable: GitClear reports cloned code rising from **8.3% to 12.3% of changed lines (2021→2024)** and moved (refactored) code falling from 25% to under 10%, with churn rising from 3–4% to 5.5% in 2023, but the sampling is undisclosed, duplication is measured only within a commit, the "4x cloning" headline is not reconciled with 8.3→12.3, and how AI assistance was identified is not described ([GitClear 2025, vendor](https://www.gitclear.com/ai_assistant_code_quality_2025_research); [GitClear 2024 via Arc, vendor](https://arc.dev/developer-blog/impact-of-ai-on-code/)). The one large academic study (37,623 provenance-labelled PRs, 2,807 repositories, December 2024 to July 2025) finds effects are vendor-specific: revert rates of 6.1% for Codex and 14.5% for Devin against 11.5% human, pooled agent code *less* likely to carry a security smell (OR 0.63), and a median 12.6-hour wait for first human review on one vendor's PRs ([arXiv 2609.17598, 2026, abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17598)). A circulating "every 25% AI adoption → 8% more defects" figure attributed to Google has no source and should not be used. Independent designers converged in 2026 on the Form's premises, AST-as-source, content-addressed definitions, one canonical way, explicit effects (Tacit, Codong, X07, Magpie, Vera at 418 stars), with Faber Romanus staking the counter-position "for agents to write and humans to read" ([agentlanguages.dev, as of 19 Oct 2026](https://agentlanguages.dev/)).

This is culture inherited at the weight level, plus one cognitive-analogue fact about models. Three conclusions. First, the "written for people to read" premise ([Abelson & Sussman, SICP, not fetched](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262510875/structure-and-interpretation-of-computer-programs/)) inverts at the artefact layer only; readability-for-models does not invert, it *narrows* to intent anchors and structure. Second, the reconciling design is "reason freely, commit canonically": generation in free text, then a deterministic transform into the Form, which is what the Hidden-Cost bidirectional tool prototypes and what the earlier design document's gate (model proposes, check admits) already requires; a Form that constrains the model's *reasoning* trace to its schema will pay the Tam penalty. Third, a Form-first ecosystem does not escape culture by removing humans; it must counter the Python prior and the retained-formatting prior explicitly, with fine-tuning on the Form's own test-validated translation pairs (the `rce emit` plus `rce test` corpus) being the cheapest demonstrated lever at fifty samples. The vendor duplication trend, if it holds, is Lehman's Law II at higher gain, and under RCE's design a human reviewer whose attention does not scale is exactly the admission check that fails. No study tests model comprehension or editing on a JSON-AST surface against text at scale; the X07 and Tacit designs are unevaluated; and nobody has measured whether AI-authored code raises propagation cost or org-intersection.

## Governance rules for the RCE spec, drawn from the organizational record

The organizational note's rules are restated here as spec obligations, each tied to the measurement that motivates it. They extend, and do not contradict, the earlier design document's "Go-style decline-biased surface-proposal rule, per-version removed-features line and `rce migrate`"; RCE §7 could not be read in this research cycle (the page truncated inside §6.2 at ~110k characters), so the author should reconcile these against whatever §7 already states.

**Admission is a mechanical check, not a vote.** Every small-language case replaced enthusiasm with a rule: Go's 10:1 helped-to-hurt ratio and two decline-slanted feedback rounds, Lua's one-mechanism-per-aspect, Elm's freeze. RCE §5.5 already says a model "may propose equivalences. It may not admit them" ([RCE paper](https://research.openie.dev/papers/rce/)); the same must hold for spec changes. A construct is admitted only if it passes a stated criterion that a reviewer applies but does not author: not expressible as a composition of existing mechanisms, measured benefit above a stated harm threshold, no hidden control flow, an error is an output in λ and an event in D. This inverts Stroustrup's asymmetry by making opposition the default and enthusiasm the thing that must clear a bar.

**Composition before addition, with the cost named.** Lua's rule held the core at 25 kLOC and produced incompatible user-level object systems; Go's "existing alternatives" plus "breaking principles" together account for ~22% of declines. The spec should state where incompatibility is permitted to live (outside the gate, in projections and libraries) and where it is not (in the Form, the log format, the canonical JSON).

**Symmetric removal.** "Nothing significant goes away" plus "don't break existing code" is the accretion ratchet, and Lehman II holds unless work reduces complexity. Every admission must ship a removal path (`rce migrate` with the repair), or the surface freezes in the Elm regime and evolution moves to the ecosystem. The per-version removed-features line is the observable; a version with no entry is a version that only grew.

**One decision locus per interface.** The Vista result says binaries touched by many org units fail; Conway says every org boundary becomes an interface. A spec governed by a committee whose membership boundary runs through the language is a predicted defect site. Keep the set of bodies that can write to the Form, the log format and the canonical JSON at one or a tiny fixed set; route everyone else through a proposal process with published reasons, reusing Go's decline taxonomy (feasibility, deprecated, limited use, breaking principles, existing alternatives, duplication, no consensus) as the rubric.

**Published gate metrics.** Go's record gives a baseline: 41% declined, 31% accepted, median 31–37 days, 15% resubmission with 25% eventually accepted. RCE should publish the same series so the decline bias is observable rather than asserted, and compute propagation cost (MacCormack) and org-intersection (Nagappan) on its own repositories as the falsifier for the three-body claim that failures sit at handoffs. A gate without published metrics is a committee with better rhetoric.

**Do not rely on the market to prune.** Adoption tracks existing code and libraries, not language quality; bloat is not punished on the timescale it accrues. Smallness is a governance property, enforced by the rule above, never an expected outcome of competition. The observability-barrier corollary is that RCE's benefits (energy, provability) are invisible at the point of authoring, so the adoption path is per-domain projections and libraries, niche by niche, the pattern of every MDE success and every Meyerovich–Rabkin growth case.

**AI changes the rate, not the rule.** The vendor trend (duplication up, refactoring down) is what a human-reviewed gate does at a higher generation rate. The admission check must be mechanical to scale with model output; otherwise the model becomes the dominant source of Lehman-II growth, and the 12.6-hour review wait becomes the new handoff.

## Three questions no one has measured, and how RCE runs them

**Can a model reason from a nameless canonical Form?** The obfuscation study replaced names with nothing, or with misleading names, and found 11–29-point summarisation drops and execution-prediction drops that the authors read as identifier leakage. It never replaced names with a schema. The experiment RCE can run: take the L0–L7 benchmark Forms, produce four projections per Form (named surface; alpha-renamed surface; Form JSON with the concept record attached; Form JSON with the concept record stripped), and measure summarisation, execution prediction and edit correctness per projection on held-out, multi-language tasks with the contamination falsifier the earlier design document specified. The hypothesis the design needs true is that the concept record recovers the named-surface score; if it does not, names must be rendered into every machine-facing projection and the "nameless Form" is a storage property only. Fine-tuning on the `rce emit` plus `rce test` corpus is the follow-on: fifty samples moved formatting behaviour in the Hidden-Cost study, so the cost of the test is small.

**What fraction of ordering in ordinary sequential code is semantically required?** The commutation rate is the single number that prices the over-specified-order mechanism, and no study reports it; Lu's 97% is the cost of the mistakes, not the size of the slack. RCE's lift is the instrument: for each lifted program, the Form's step dependency graph gives the partial order, and the source gives the total order; the ratio of statement pairs that commute under the Form's deps to all adjacent pairs is the commutation rate, reported per corpus and per language. The geometry atlas asked a neighbouring question, whether the "code is low-entropy" literature measures a small behaviour space or a redundant chart; the commutation rate is one of the two components of that redundancy (the other is the Nerode index of the finite-control projection), and both are tool runs over the same corpus.

**Does reversible, checked deletion change the 20% deletion rate?** Haas's 25 developers deleted 10 of 50 confirmed-unnecessary chunks, citing fear and unknown dependents, against a 32%-precise detector. The design invariant that reversibility replaces approval predicts that when deletion is a content-addressed, replay-checked, one-command-reversible operation whose reachability verdict is a decided property of the Form rather than a 32%-precise heuristic, the rate rises. The experiment: instrument `rce` so that unreachable definitions (decidable over the explicit event set) are reported with a replay-backed certificate, deletion keeps the body under its key with a restore path, and the fraction of reported chunks actually deleted within a fixed window is logged across the stdlib and any user repositories that opt in. Pair it with a willingness-to-delete versus willingness-to-add comparison under matched uncertainty, which is the loss-aversion experiment nobody has run on code. If the rate does not move, fear was never the binding constraint and recognition (the organizational half of Haas's hypothesis) is, which points at making energy and correctness deltas of a deletion first-class and attributable on the receipt.

## Claims that do not survive the source check

Two claims the author's working material leans on have no traceable support and should be removed or restated. The **"Glasgow 2014 study of language manual page counts"** could not be located; searches on specification page-count growth returned only forum material, and no peer-reviewed page-count study was found. Unless the RCE bibliography supplies a primary source, the manual-growth argument should rest on Stroustrup's HOPL-IV paper-volume figures (>500 papers a year, mailing at three times Shakespeare) and Lua's 100-page manual, which are verified, and not on a page-count series. The **"experienced programmers write less code"** claim traces to a 2008 blog post that attributes it to "a study I heard of recently" with no citation, sample or numbers ([Cook 2008](https://www.johndcook.com/blog/2008/06/03/experienced-programmers-and-lines-of-code/)); the measured evidence runs the other way on the lexical axis (experienced developers' names are 1.75 characters longer with more concepts) and is silent on produced code size, Halstead volume or compression ratio, so the claim should be stated as untested and the expertise argument should rest on recognition (Soloway, Busjahn, Chase and Simon), not on volume. Three further items are quoted from memory and should be verified before any quotation appears in normative text: Buse and Weimer's 2010 feature weights, Holzmann's Power of 10 rule list, and the Hofmeister 2017 "19% faster with full words" figure. The CISQ and Stripe cost figures are model estimates; the GitClear series is vendor data with undisclosed sampling; the Google "8% more defects" figure has no source at all.

## Conclusion

The ten mechanisms collapse to one statement: human code is noisy where the medium gives the author no channel for what they mean, and the noise is each author's private workaround, idiosyncratic (6.9% naming agreement), unchecked (7–13% comment co-evolution), and retained out of rational fear (32% detector precision, 20% deletion). Cognition sets the budget (four chunks, vocabulary and dependency reach as the measured spend); organization sets the asymmetries (private benefit of addition, public cost of checking, no recognition for deletion); culture sets the defaults everyone inherits, including the models. The Form is the first representation that gives those channels a home that is checked rather than read: order as a partial order, locality as the step, plans as content-addressed definitions, intent as a logged vocabulary with examples at the gate, reachability as a decidable property. What changes from the two earlier reports is the status of names: the earlier design document put them in an unhashed side table as spelling; the evidence here says the side table must carry the concept, because both the only human readers who matter (experts reading plan-like code) and the only authors who will matter (models that lose 29 points without anchors) read through names, and the Form has not yet shown either can read without them.

The second-order implication is for how the author spends effort. Every mechanism above has a measured ceiling on what a surface can achieve (no readability metric above τ = 0.16, no fMRI effect of layout, 62–66% human–model agreement on what is natural) and an unmeasured floor on what the Form can claim (the commutation rate, the nameless-Form reasoning score, the deletion rate under reversibility). The three experiments are cheap relative to a proof, each is a tool run over corpora RCE already generates, and each converts a design premise into a number that would either justify the Form's existence or tell the author which premise to drop. Run them before the next spec revision; the governance rules above only matter for a language whose premises have survived its own gate.
