# Dualities, correspondences and invariants between program representations ("the geometry of code"), for the Reality Code Engine Form

Frame checked against the source: the RCE paper (research.openie.dev/papers/rce, sections 2.1, 3, 3.6) defines the Form as P = (S, E, δ, D, λ) with δ: S × E → S × O* total, deterministic and pure; Definition 3 makes two fits equivalent when they produce the same output sequence for every event sequence; Definition 6 defines the fit e-graph (e-classes = Definition-3 equivalence under sound rewrites); Proposition 3 says extraction with the joule cost returns the least-energy term of an e-class; section 3.6 frames the round trip as a lens with GetPut/PutGet, and claims universality via Gurevich's theorem plus propositions U1–U4 (capture, faithful projection, lift by construction, replacement). The paper does not itself use the words manifold, chart, gauge, holonomy or geodesic — those are the working geometry to be tested here — [RCE paper](https://research.openie.dev/papers/rce/). The repo README confirms the Form is total: every program terminates (bounded loops, structural recursion), which matters for every "universality" verdict below — [RCE README](https://github.com/openIE-dev/rce).

## Q1. Defunctionalization / refunctionalization, the functional correspondence, CPS / direct style: the maps from higher-order charts to the Form chart

### Takeaway
These are the one family of translations that is genuinely a theorem-grade "transition map" into a first-order state-machine chart: Reynolds' pipeline (closure conversion → CPS → defunctionalization) is total and meaning-preserving in one direction for any higher-order program, and the Danvy school proved the inverses exist exactly when the first-order program is already "in defunctionalized form" (one dispatch function per sum type). What is preserved is observable behaviour (and, for CPS, evaluation order made explicit); what is free is the choice of continuation representation, the evaluation-order axioms, and the closure representation.

### Cited Findings
- Reynolds (1972) introduced definitional interpreters, defunctionalization and the observation that a meta-circular interpreter inherits the evaluation order of its defining language unless the interpreter is written in continuation-passing style; CPS plus defunctionalization yields a first-order interpreter — the original "lower a higher-order chart to a first-order chart" — [Reynolds, Definitional interpreters for higher-order programming languages, ACM 1972; reprinted HOSC 1998](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1010027404223).
- Danvy and Nielsen (2001) show defunctionalization is a general program transformation: each function space becomes a sum type plus an apply function; it preserves meaning and is the mechanical route from closures to first-order data — [Danvy & Nielsen, Defunctionalization at work, PPDP 2001 / BRICS RS-01-23](https://www.brics.dk/RS/01/23/).
- Danvy and Millikin present refunctionalization as "the left inverse of Reynolds's defunctionalization" and give two methods for transforming a program that is "almost in defunctionalized form" into one that actually is, illustrated with a Dyck-word recognizer and Dijkstra's shunting-yard algorithm — [Danvy & Millikin, Refunctionalization at work, BRICS RS-08-4 (2008); journal version Science of Computer Programming 74(8), 2009](https://www.brics.dk/RS/08/4/). The precondition (a first-order data type consumed by exactly one dispatch function) is the "representation choice" that makes the inverse exist; a first-order program whose constructors are consumed in several places has no direct higher-order preimage.
- Ager, Biernacki, Danvy and Midtgaard (2003) established the "functional correspondence": closure conversion, CPS transformation and defunctionalization applied to a standard evaluator mechanically derive the CEK machine, Krivine's machine, the SECD machine and others; the derivation works in both directions, so an abstract machine (a state machine) and a higher-order evaluator are two charts of one object — [Ager et al., A functional correspondence between evaluators and abstract machines, PPDP 2003](https://doi.org/10.1145/888251.888254).
- Koppel's Compose 2019 talk "The best refactoring you've never heard of" popularised the recipe "CPS-convert, then defunctionalize the continuation" as the mechanical way to turn recursive functions into explicit stack machines (and back) — [Koppel, pathsensitive.com (2019)](https://www.pathsensitive.com/2019/07/the-best-refactoring-youve-never-heard.html); the UPenn PL Club wrote up the same recipe — [PLClub blog, Defunctionalize the continuation (2020)](https://cis.upenn.edu/~plclub/blog/2020-05-15-Defunctionalize-the-Continuation). Hutton and Bahr (2016) give a calculational version that goes directly from a direct-style evaluator to a compiler without introducing continuations explicitly — [Hutton & Bahr, Cutting out continuations (2016)](https://eprints.nottingham.ac.uk/32703).
- Plotkin (1975) proved the CPS simulation theorems: the call-by-value and call-by-name CPS transforms are correct (they simulate the source evaluation) and the transform is not complete — equality in the CPS target is strictly coarser than equality in the source calculus — [Plotkin, Call-by-name, call-by-value and the λ-calculus, TCS 1975](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3975(75)90017-1).
- Sabry and Felleisen (1993) found the exact set of axioms A such that direct-style terms are equal under λv + A iff their CPS images are βη-equal: the CPS "transition map" is lossless on equational theories only after enriching the source theory — [Sabry & Felleisen, Reasoning about programs in continuation-passing style, LISP and Symbolic Computation 1993](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01019462).
- Danvy's direct-style transformation is the inverse of CPS on the image of CPS (terms whose continuations are used linearly); terms that use continuations non-linearly need first-class control (call/cc) in the direct-style target — [Danvy, Back to direct style, Science of Computer Programming 1994](https://doi.org/10.1016/0167-6423(94)90003-5).
- Hatcliff and Danvy (1994) factor every known CPS transform through Moggi's computational metalanguage: CPS = (monadic translation) ∘ (continuation monad instantiation); the "free representation choices" are exactly the monad — [Hatcliff & Danvy, A generic account of continuation-passing styles, POPL 1994](https://doi.org/10.1145/174675.178053).
- Filinski (1994) proved any monadic effect is expressible in a language with first-class continuations plus one mutable cell ("representing monads"); i.e. continuations + state is a universal coordinate system for effects — [Filinski, Representing monads, POPL 1994](https://doi.org/10.1145/174675.178047).

### Inferences
- The Form's δ: S × E → S × O* is literally the shape of a defunctionalized, CPS-converted evaluator with the continuation reified into S (Σc = control regions is exactly the defunctionalized continuation). So "any higher-order language lifts to the Form" is backed by a theorem *for the lowering direction* (Reynolds/Danvy), not an analogy.
- The lift is not canonical: closure representation, which sub-terms become control states, and evaluation order (λv vs λn CPS) are free choices; two different lifts of the same program land in the same e-class only under Definition 3 (same outputs for all event sequences), which is where the "gauge" lives (Q4).
- The inverse (Form → higher-order chart) exists only when the Form is "in defunctionalized form" (Danvy–Millikin); a Form whose events are consumed by many steps has no single refunctionalized preimage. This is the first concrete sense in which the Form chart is strictly coarser than a higher-order chart: the lift is a surjection onto its image, not a bijection.
- Felleisen's and Danvy's results show that a target without first-class control cannot host the direct-style inverse of a non-linear CPS term: this is the one place the "compactified coordinate" picture (Q8) is precise — the continuation coordinate is explicit in the Form and implicit (integrated out) in direct-style languages.

### Gaps
- I did not find a published proof that refunctionalization composes with direct-style transformation to give a total inverse of the full Reynolds pipeline on arbitrary first-order state machines; the Danvy-school results are stated on evaluators and specific programs.

## Q2. Futamura projections, partial evaluation, staging, binding time as a coordinate

### Takeaway
The Futamura projections are theorems (given a self-applicable specializer) and binding-time analysis is a genuine two-valued coordinate (static/dynamic) on every sub-expression; staging (MetaML) is the typed version. They show that "compiler" and "interpreter" are not different kinds of object but the same object viewed at different binding times — but they say nothing about losslessness; specialisation deliberately discards the dynamic information.

### Cited Findings
- Futamura (1971) showed that partially evaluating an interpreter with respect to a program yields a compiled program (first projection), partially evaluating the specializer w.r.t. the interpreter yields a compiler (second), and w.r.t. itself yields a compiler generator (third) — [Futamura, Partial evaluation of computation process — an approach to a compiler-compiler (1971), reprinted HOSC 12(4), 1999](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1010095604496).
- Jones, Gomard and Sestoft (1993) is the canonical treatment: offline partial evaluation assigns each expression a binding time (S or D) by a monovariant/polyvariant analysis; the Mix equation `[[mix]](p, s) = p_s` with `[[p_s]](d) = [[p]](s, d)`; "Jones-optimality" is the condition that self-application removes all interpretive overhead — [Jones, Gomard & Sestoft, Partial Evaluation and Automatic Program Generation, Prentice Hall 1993 (free online)](https://www.itu.dk/people/sestoft/pebook/).
- Taha and Sheard's MetaML gives binding time a type: code of type `<t>` is a staged value; the type system guarantees generated code is well-typed, i.e. the binding-time coordinate is a typed index on the term — [Taha & Sheard, MetaML and multi-stage programming with explicit annotations, TCS 2000](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0304-3975(00)00053-0).
- Würthinger et al. (Truffle/Graal) realise the first Futamura projection in production by partially evaluating AST interpreters: "one VM to rule them all" — [Würthinger et al., One VM to rule them all, Onward! 2013](https://doi.org/10.1145/2509578.2509581).

### Inferences
- The RCE pipeline is a Futamura object: the checker/interpreter defines meaning and `rce emit` is a (hand-written) first projection. Zig-style `comptime` in SURFACE.md is binding-time annotation; "evaluated during lowering, results become constants in the Form IR" is exactly offline partial evaluation.
- Binding time is a coordinate on the *representation*, not on behaviour: two fits that differ only in what is static are in the same e-class. This is a correct example of a "gauge direction" that is decidable (binding-time analysis is a static analysis).

### Gaps
- No work was found that treats binding time as a continuous or metric coordinate; it is discrete (two or finitely many stages).

## Q3. Curry–Howard–Lambek, propositions as sessions, encodings, data/codata, the expression problem

### Takeaway
These are the genuine dualities and three-way correspondences in PL: proofs/programs/arrows (theorem), data/codata as initial/final algebras (theorem), Church/Böhm–Berarducci encodings as data-as-functions (theorem with a stated range), sessions as linear propositions (theorem), and the expression problem as the statement that the ADT chart and the object/codata chart have complementary blind spots (a precise, old observation of Reynolds 1975).

### Cited Findings
- Wadler's survey states the three-way correspondence: propositions as types, proofs as programs, and (Lambek) both as morphisms in a cartesian closed category; it documents Howard (1969/1980) and Lambek (1980) — [Wadler, Propositions as types, CACM 2015](https://doi.org/10.1145/2699407).
- Lawvere (1963) showed an algebraic theory is a category with finite products, and its models are product-preserving functors — the categorical statement that a signature-and-equations (an algebraic "language") and its semantics are a functor, not a set of strings — [Lawvere, Functorial semantics of algebraic theories, PNAS 1963](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.50.5.869).
- Caires and Pfenning (2010) and Wadler (2012) show session types are linear-logic propositions, processes are proofs, and cut elimination is communication; Wadler gives a translation from a session-typed functional language into the proof system and back — [Caires & Pfenning, Session types as intuitionistic linear propositions, CONCUR 2010](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-15375-4_16); [Wadler, Propositions as sessions, ICFP 2012](https://doi.org/10.1145/2364527.2364568).
- Böhm and Berarducci (1985) proved every term of a free algebra (any ADT) is representable in System F as a polymorphic function (iterators), and that every term of the encoding type that is "closed" corresponds to a term of the data type: data and its fold are the same object — [Böhm & Berarducci, Automatic synthesis of typed Λ-programs on term algebras, TCS 1985](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3975(85)90135-5).
- Carette, Kiselyov and Shan (2009) give the "tagless final" encoding: an object language as a type class / signature of its denotations; interpreters are instances; it solves the expression problem and makes "initial (data) vs final (functions) encoding" a choice of chart with identical expressiveness for first-order terms — [Carette, Kiselyov & Shan, Finally tagless, partially evaluated, JFP 2009](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0956796809007205).
- Hagino (1987) introduced categorical data types with initial algebras (data) and final coalgebras (codata) as dual constructions — [Hagino, A categorical programming language, PhD thesis Edinburgh 1987](https://www.lfcs.inf.ed.ac.uk/reports/87/ECS-LFCS-87-38/). Downen, Sullivan, Ariola and Peyton Jones (2019) show codata is dual to data, that the visitor pattern is the Böhm–Berarducci encoding, and give a compilation of codata to data and vice versa — [Downen et al., Codata in action, ESOP 2019](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-17184-1_5).
- Wadler's 1998 note defines the expression problem: extend a datatype with both new cases and new functions without recompiling and with static type safety — [Wadler, The expression problem (1998)](https://homepages.inf.ed.ac.uk/wadler/papers/expression/expression.txt). Cook (2009), following Reynolds (1975), argues that ADTs and objects are two complementary approaches to data abstraction: ADTs make adding operations easy and objects make adding representations easy — [Cook, On understanding data abstraction, revisited, OOPSLA 2009](https://doi.org/10.1145/1640089.1640133). Oliveira and Cook (2012) show object algebras (= tagless final) solve the problem in plain Java — [Oliveira & Cook, Extensibility for the masses, ECOOP 2012](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-31057-7_2).
- Curien–Herbelin (2000) and Wadler (2003) show call-by-value and call-by-name are dual under a classical sequent-calculus syntax (a literal involution on programs) — [Curien & Herbelin, The duality of computation, ICFP 2000](https://doi.org/10.1145/351240.351262); [Wadler, Call-by-value is dual to call-by-name, ICFP 2003](https://doi.org/10.1145/944705.944723).

### Inferences
- The Form picks the data/initial side everywhere: states and events are sums ("kind", total match), δ is a fold over D ("state as a fold of the log", Definition 2). By the Hagino/Downen duality the Form is a chart on the data side; codata-style programs (servers, streams, objects with unbounded protocols) enter only via Böhm–Berarducci encoding — which is a theorem that they can enter, but with the expression-problem cost: adding a new event (case) is cheap, adding a new operation across all states requires touching every step.
- "Charts have complementary blind spots" is precisely the Reynolds 1975 / Cook 2009 observation, with 50 years of literature; it is a theorem-level statement about *extensibility* (modular change), not about expressiveness (both encodings denote the same functions).

### Gaps
- No source found that frames the expression problem in explicitly geometric (chart/atlas) terms; the mapping is the author's.

## Q4. Program equivalence as gauge symmetry; where the orbit is decidable

### Takeaway
Sound rewrites generate a congruence, and the e-class is a connected component of the proof groupoid, so "gauge orbit" is a fair analogy for equality-saturation e-classes — but it is a groupoid, not a Lie group, and the orbit is decidable only when the equivalence is: finite-state Mealy equivalence (decidable, n log n), KAT (PSPACE-complete), DPDA equivalence (decidable, Sénizergues), and nothing beyond — contextual equivalence of Turing-complete programs is undecidable (Rice). For RCE's Definition 3 this bites: it is Mealy-machine equivalence, decidable for finite Σd and undecidable once data is unbounded, even though every RCE program terminates.

### Cited Findings
- Plotkin (1977) formalised contextual (observational) equivalence and full abstraction: a semantic model is fully abstract iff its equality coincides with contextual equivalence; the Scott model of PCF is not fully abstract — [Plotkin, LCF considered as a programming language, TCS 1977](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3975(77)90044-5).
- Park (1981) introduced bisimulation; for deterministic systems, bisimilarity coincides with trace (output-sequence) equivalence — [Park, Concurrency and automata on infinite sequences, 1981](https://doi.org/10.1007/BFb0017309).
- Rutten (2000) established universal coalgebra: a Mealy machine is a coalgebra for F(X) = (O × X)^E; the final coalgebra of this functor is the set of causal stream functions E^ω → O^ω, and every machine has a unique behaviour map into it; two states are equivalent iff they map to the same point — [Rutten, Universal coalgebra: a theory of systems, TCS 2000](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0304-3975(00)00056-6); the Mealy-specific statement and the synthesis of minimal Mealy machines by derivatives is in [Rutten, Algebraic specification and coalgebraic synthesis of Mealy automata, ENTCS 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.entcs.2006.06.006) and [Bonsangue, Rutten & Silva, Coalgebraic logic and synthesis of Mealy machines, FoSSaCS 2008](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-540-78499-9_17).
- Nerode (1958) showed the minimal automaton is unique up to isomorphism, its state count equal to the index of the Nerode right-congruence; Hopcroft (1971) gave the n log n minimisation — [Nerode, Linear automaton transformations, Proc. AMS 1958](https://doi.org/10.2307/2033204); [Hopcroft, An n log n algorithm for minimizing states in a finite automaton, 1971](https://doi.org/10.1016/B978-0-12-417750-5.50022-1).
- Kozen (1997): Kleene algebra with tests axiomatises while-program equivalence modulo the Boolean conditions; its equational theory is decidable and PSPACE-complete (Cohen, Kozen, Smith 1996) — [Kozen, Kleene algebra with tests, TOPLAS 1997](https://doi.org/10.1145/256167.256195); [Cohen, Kozen & Smith, The complexity of Kleene algebra with tests, Cornell TR 1996](https://ecommons.cornell.edu/handle/1813/7253). Regular-expression equivalence is PSPACE-complete — [Stockmeyer & Meyer, Word problems requiring exponential time, STOC 1973](https://doi.org/10.1145/800125.804029).
- Sénizergues proved equivalence of deterministic pushdown automata decidable (Gödel Prize 2002) — [Sénizergues, L(A)=L(B)? decidability results from complete formal systems, TCS 2001](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0304-3975(00)00285-1).
- Church (1936) proved β-equality of λ-terms undecidable; Rice (1953) that every non-trivial semantic property of programs is undecidable, so contextual equivalence of Turing-complete programs is undecidable — [Church, An unsolvable problem of elementary number theory, AJM 1936](https://doi.org/10.2307/2371045); [Rice, Classes of recursively enumerable sets and their decision problems, Trans. AMS 1953](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-1953-0053041-6).
- Equality saturation (Tate et al. 2009) and egg (Willsey et al. 2021): an e-graph holds a congruence relation over terms; rewrites add equalities, never destroy; saturation may not terminate and is run to a bound; extraction picks a representative minimising a cost function — [Tate et al., Equality saturation: a new approach to optimization, POPL 2009](https://doi.org/10.1145/1480881.1480915); [Willsey et al., egg: Fast and extensible equality saturation, POPL 2021](https://doi.org/10.1145/3434304).
- Rovelli's reading of gauge: gauge variables are redundancies that describe relations to other systems, not nothing — [Rovelli, Why gauge?, Found. Phys. 2014](https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.5599); a philosophical overview of the same question is [Why gauge? Conceptual aspects of gauge theories (arXiv 2022)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.05339).

### Inferences
- The right mathematical object for "gauge orbit" is the connected component of a term in the groupoid whose arrows are rewrite proofs (an e-class is exactly that, by construction of congruence closure). A group acts freely and transitively on an orbit; rewrite groupoids do not (different proofs of the same equality, non-invertible directed rewrites). So: analogy, with a precise replacement (groupoid / HoTT path-space, see Q5).
- RCE Definition 3 is Mealy equivalence = final-coalgebra equality. Theorem: for finite S this is decidable and yields a unique minimal Form (Nerode/Hopcroft/Rutten), i.e. a canonical representative per e-class *independent of any cost* — a stronger property than "least joule extraction". For S containing unbounded data (u64 fields, text) the equivalence is undecidable in general even for terminating programs (Rice applies to total functions on unbounded domains via reduction from any Π1 property). The e-graph therefore under-approximates the orbit; this is the same situation as egg's bounded saturation.
- "Gauge" in the Rovelli sense is apt: the representation-level freedom (which language, which closure representation) is relational redundancy w.r.t. the host/observer. That is a conceptual match, not a theorem.

### Gaps
- No paper was found that uses the term "gauge" for program equivalence in a technical (non-decorative) way; searches for gauge + program equivalence return physics philosophy, Moggi monads and metric-enriched categories.

## Q5. Categorical and geometric semantics: Lambek, string diagrams, game semantics, GoI, differential/linear logic, HoTT, wp, refinement, Kolmogorov/MDL

### Takeaway
"Geometry" has three literal homes in PL: (i) HoTT, where equalities are paths and the e-class is a path-connected component (theorem-grade); (ii) differential categories / AD, where programs are literally smooth maps and have derivatives; (iii) Girard's geometry of interaction and game semantics, where higher-order programs become first-order token-passing automata — this is the deepest support for a state-machine Form, because Danos–Herbelin–Regnier proved game-semantic strategies and abstract machines coincide. Metric structure exists only in quantitative equational logic (distances between behaviours) and Kolmogorov information distance (a true metric on descriptions), never as a Riemannian metric with geodesics.

### Cited Findings
- Abramsky–Jagadeesan–Malacaria and Hyland–Ong gave the first fully abstract models of PCF via games (strategies as sets of plays with history-free / innocence conditions) — [AJM, Full abstraction for PCF, Inf. & Comp. 2000](https://doi.org/10.1006/inco.2000.2930); [Hyland & Ong, On full abstraction for PCF, Inf. & Comp. 2000](https://doi.org/10.1006/inco.2000.2917).
- Danos, Herbelin and Regnier (LICS 1996) proved that the game-semantic interpretation of λ-terms (plays/strategies) is the same object as the execution of an environment/abstract machine (the PAM / Krivine-style machine): "Game semantics and abstract machines" — [LICS 1996 record](https://lics.siglog.org/1996/DanosHerbelinRegnie-Gamesemanticsandabs.html).
- Girard's Geometry of Interaction interprets cut-elimination as a dynamical system of token paths through proof nets; Mackie implemented it as compilation ("the geometry of implementation") and Ghica used it to compile programs to hardware circuits ("geometry of synthesis") — [Girard, Geometry of interaction I, Logic Colloquium '88 (1989)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0049-237X(08)70271-4); [Mackie, The geometry of interaction machine, POPL 1995](https://doi.org/10.1145/199448.199483); [Ghica, Geometry of synthesis: a structured approach to VLSI design, POPL 2007](https://doi.org/10.1145/1190216.1190269). Fredriksson and Ghica revisited the abstract-machine reading — [Abstract machines for game semantics, revisited (2013)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1304.4159).
- Coecke and Kissinger's string-diagram calculus and Abramsky–Coecke categorical quantum mechanics give compositional, diagrammatic semantics for processes with sound and complete equational rewriting — [Coecke & Kissinger, Picturing Quantum Processes, CUP 2017](https://doi.org/10.1017/9781316219317); [Abramsky & Coecke, A categorical semantics of quantum protocols, LICS 2004](https://doi.org/10.1109/LICS.2004.1319636). Bonchi, Sobociński and Zanasi proved full abstraction for signal flow graphs: the diagrammatic equational theory exactly captures behavioural (stream) equivalence of linear dynamical systems — [Bonchi, Sobociński & Zanasi, Full abstraction for signal flow graphs, POPL 2015](https://doi.org/10.1145/2676726.2676993).
- Ehrhard–Regnier's differential λ-calculus and Blute–Cockett–Seely's Cartesian differential categories make "derivative of a program" an axiomatised operation; Elliott shows automatic differentiation is a functor from programs to derivative programs — [Ehrhard & Regnier, The differential lambda-calculus, TCS 2003](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0304-3975(03)00392-X); [Blute, Cockett & Seely, Cartesian differential categories, TAC 2009](http://www.tac.mta.ca/tac/volumes/22/23/22-23abs.html); [Elliott, The simple essence of automatic differentiation, ICFP 2018](https://doi.org/10.1145/3236765).
- Homotopy type theory: identity types are path spaces, types are ∞-groupoids, and univalence makes equivalence of types a path; Awodey–Warren gave the homotopy-theoretic model of identity types — [The HoTT Book, Univalent Foundations Program 2013](https://homotopytypetheory.org/book/); [Awodey & Warren, Homotopy theoretic models of identity types, Math. Proc. Camb. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0305004108001783).
- Dijkstra's weakest-precondition predicate transformers (1975) and Back–von Wright's refinement calculus organise programs as a lattice ordered by refinement, with wp as a monotone transformer on the predicate lattice — [Dijkstra, Guarded commands, nondeterminacy and formal derivation of programs, CACM 1975](https://doi.org/10.1145/360933.360975); [Back & von Wright, Refinement Calculus, Springer 1998](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4612-1674-2).
- Mardare, Panangaden and Plotkin introduced quantitative equational logic (equations x =ε y, completeness for metric-enriched algebras); Dahlqvist and Neves give a V-equational deductive system for the linear λ-calculus that is sound and complete for autonomous categories enriched over partial orders or (ultra)metric spaces — distances are between behaviours, graded equivalence — [Mardare, Panangaden & Plotkin, Quantitative algebraic reasoning, LICS 2016](https://doi.org/10.1145/2933575.2934518); [Dahlqvist & Neves, An internal language for categories enriched over generalised metric spaces, CSL 2022](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.CSL.2022.16).
- Metric semantics of processes: de Bakker–Zucker and America–Rutten built process domains as complete ultrametric spaces (distance 2^-n for the longest agreeing prefix), with semantics as unique fixed points by Banach — [de Bakker & Zucker, Processes and the denotational semantics of concurrency, Inf. & Control 1982](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0019-9958(82)91013-8); [America & Rutten, Solving reflexive domain equations in a category of complete metric spaces, JCSS 1989](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-0000(89)90027-5).
- Kolmogorov complexity is not computable; Bennett, Gács, Li, Vitányi and Zurek defined information distance E(x,y) = max{K(x|y), K(y|x)}, a true metric up to additive constants, universal among admissible distances; Rissanen's MDL replaces K with a computable code length under a model class — [Bennett et al., Information distance, IEEE Trans. IT 1998](https://doi.org/10.1109/18.681318); [Rissanen, Modeling by shortest data description, Automatica 1978](https://doi.org/10.1016/0005-1098(78)90005-5).

### Inferences
- The strongest literal geometry for a Form is the final-coalgebra / ultrametric picture: behaviour space of Forms is the final (O × X)^E coalgebra, carrying the prefix ultrametric. It is complete, totally disconnected and of topological dimension zero — the opposite of a manifold. "Behaviour space is a manifold" is therefore false as stated; "behaviour space is a complete ultrametric space with a universal property" is a theorem (Rutten, de Bakker–Zucker).
- HoTT supplies the precise "paths as equalities": an e-class with its rewrite proofs is a groupoid; "the gauge group" becomes the fundamental groupoid of the e-graph. Different proofs of one equality = non-trivial π1, which egg discards (it stores one proof per equality for explanation). This is the honest analogy for holonomy (Q8).
- Game semantics / GoI / Danos–Herbelin–Regnier give a theorem-grade route from any typed higher-order program to a first-order interaction automaton — a second, language-independent lift into state-machine form, complementary to defunctionalization, and fully abstract for PCF. That is the closest existing "global chart" claim and it is for a terminating-or-partial typed functional core, not for arbitrary languages.
- Cost as a metric is false: a joule cost is a function on representations (a potential), and extraction is argmin over a finite set, not a geodesic. The one true metric in sight is information distance (Bennett et al.), which is between *descriptions*, so "least-joule extraction = MDL with joules in place of bits" is an analogy with a defensible formal core (MDL is argmin of a potential, exactly what extraction does) — but no connection, no transport, no geodesic.

### Gaps
- No PL or CS-theory paper was found that defines a Riemannian or Finsler metric on program or behaviour space, or a geodesic interpretation of optimisation; the closest are the ultrametric and quantale-enriched settings above.

## Q6. Information-theoretic structure of code; a measured "dimension" of code

### Takeaway
Code is measurably lower-entropy than natural language (Hindle 2012), buggy lines are measurably higher-entropy (Ray 2016), and this signal survives into the LLM era (Yang et al. 2024: entropy re-ranking improves fault localisation 50% Top-5). But the "naturalness" gap shrinks substantially once syntax tokens are removed (Rahman 2019) and part of it is a vocabulary/syntax effect (Casalnuovo 2019). No published measurement of an intrinsic *dimension* of code (embedding manifold dimension or Nerode state count across corpora) was found.

### Cited Findings
- Hindle et al. (2012) showed n-gram models give far lower cross-entropy on code than on English and that code is "very repetitive, and in fact even more so than natural languages" — [Hindle et al., On the naturalness of software, ICSE 2012](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICSE.2012.6227135).
- Ray et al. (2016) showed buggy lines have higher entropy under language models and that entropy is competitive with static bug finders for ranking — [Ray et al., On the "naturalness" of buggy code, ICSE 2016](https://doi.org/10.1145/2884781.2884848).
- Allamanis, Barr, Devanbu and Sutton surveyed the field — [A survey of machine learning for big code and naturalness, ACM CSUR 2018](https://doi.org/10.1145/3212695).
- Rahman, Palani and Rigby (2019) found that much of the code/English entropy gap is due to syntax tokens; after removing them the gap shrinks substantially (code remains more repetitive but much less dramatically) — [Rahman et al., Natural software revisited, ICSE 2019](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICSE.2019.00022).
- Casalnuovo, Sagae and Devanbu (2019) controlled for vocabulary and syntax and found code stays more predictable than English, attributing part to programmers' deliberate choices; Casalnuovo et al. (2020) found programmers prefer the more predictable of semantically equivalent expressions — [Casalnuovo et al., Studying the difference between natural and programming language corpora, EMSE 2019](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10664-018-9669-7); [Casalnuovo et al., Do programmers prefer predictable expressions?, Cognitive Science 2020](https://doi.org/10.1111/cogs.12921).
- Yang, Kolak, Hellendoorn, Martins and Le Goues (2024; ICSE 2025) show LLM token entropy re-ranking gives a 50% Top-5 improvement over spectrum-based fault localisation and their "entropy-delta" patch ranking a 49% Top-1 improvement — [Revisiting unnaturalness for automated program repair in the era of LLMs, arXiv 2404.15236](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.15236).
- Rabin, Mukherjee, Gnawali and Alipour (2020) studied code2vec embedding dimensions: information gain is spread evenly across dimensions and hand-crafted features nearly match them; no intrinsic-dimension estimate is given — [Towards demystifying dimensions of source code embeddings, arXiv 2008.13064](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.13064).
- Lee, Weber, Viégas and Wattenberg (2025) measure a simple intrinsic dimension of LLM token embeddings (not code) and find embeddings lie on a lower-dimensional manifold with low-ID tokens forming semantic clusters — [Shared global and local geometry of language model embeddings, arXiv 2503.21073](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.21073).

### Inferences
- Section 2.1's "programming is coding theory" is supported for the *entropy* claims (Hindle, Ray, Yang) and for the MDL reading, but the paper should cite Rahman 2019 and Casalnuovo 2019 as the corrections: the raw gap is partly syntactic.
- A Form has a natural, exact "dimension": for finite control, the Nerode/Mealy index of its minimal machine (number of distinguishable states), which is language-independent. That is the only "intrinsic dimension of code" that is a theorem; embedding-based ID numbers for code do not exist in the literature found.

### Gaps
- No 2024–2026 paper measuring intrinsic dimension of code embeddings or code-model hidden states was found (searches returned token-embedding ID for NL models and code2vec dimension-importance studies only).
- No corpus study of minimal-state-machine sizes of real programs (a Nerode-style "dimension") was found.

## Q7. Universal forms claimed before: ASM, UNCOL, LLVM, Wasm, Truffle, Flanagan, SSA↔FP, RVSDG, sea of nodes, MLIR

### Takeaway
Every successful "universal" representation is universal in one direction only (source → IR, deliberately lossy on source structure) and makes no lift claim; UNCOL failed precisely because it promised both directions for n languages × m machines. Gurevich's ASM thesis is the one universality result that is a theorem-from-postulates, and it is the one RCE cites — but it characterises *step-by-step behaviour of sequential algorithms*, and its parallel extension is contested. RCE's Form is additionally total (every program terminates), so it is a chart on a strict sub-space of behaviour regardless of ASM.

### Cited Findings
- Gurevich (2000) proved that every algorithm satisfying the Sequential Time, Abstract State and Bounded Exploration postulates is step-for-step simulated by a sequential ASM — [Gurevich, Sequential abstract-state machines capture sequential algorithms, ACM TOCL 2000](https://doi.org/10.1145/343369.343384). Dershowitz and Gurevich derived Church's thesis from these postulates — [Dershowitz & Gurevich, A natural axiomatization of computability and proof of Church's thesis, BSL 2008](https://doi.org/10.2178/bsl/1231081370).
- The parallel extension (Blass–Gurevich 2003) replaces bounded exploration with three postulates (proclets, bounded sequentiality, update) that "have not fully convinced the ASM community"; Ferrarotti, Schewe, Tec and Wang propose a simplified single postulate and note the debate is ongoing — [Blass & Gurevich, Abstract state machines capture parallel algorithms, TOCL 2003](https://doi.org/10.1145/937555.937561); [Ferrarotti et al., A new thesis concerning synchronised parallel computing, arXiv 1504.06203](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.06203).
- UNCOL (1958): Strong et al. proposed a universal intermediate language so that n front-ends and m back-ends replace n×m translators; Conway's "Proposal for an UNCOL" the same year; a first version was reported in 1961; the project never produced a working universal language — [Strong et al., The problem of programming communication with changing machines: a proposed solution, CACM 1958](https://doi.org/10.1145/368892.368915); [Conway, Proposal for an UNCOL, CACM 1958](https://doi.org/10.1145/368924.368928); [Steel, A first version of UNCOL, 1961](https://doi.org/10.1145/1460690.1460733).
- Landin's ISWIM (1966) was the first "one core, 700 surface languages" claim — [Landin, The next 700 programming languages, CACM 1966](https://doi.org/10.1145/365230.365257).
- Felleisen (1991) gave a formal expressiveness order on languages: a feature is macro-expressible if it can be eliminated by local (homomorphic) translation; some features (e.g. first-class continuations, assignment) are not, and their removal requires global restructuring — [Felleisen, On the expressive power of programming languages, Science of Computer Programming 1991](https://doi.org/10.1016/0167-6423(91)90036-W).
- Flanagan, Sabry, Duba and Felleisen (1993) showed CPS compilation is equivalent to A-normal form: the CPS "chart" adds nothing a direct-style IR with named intermediates lacks — [The essence of compiling with continuations, PLDI 1993](https://doi.org/10.1145/155090.155113). Kelsey (1995) and Appel (1998) showed SSA and CPS/functional programs are the same representation (φ-nodes = block parameters) — [Kelsey, A correspondence between continuation passing style and static single assignment form, IR'95](https://doi.org/10.1145/202529.202532); [Appel, SSA is functional programming, SIGPLAN Notices 1998](https://doi.org/10.1145/278283.278285).
- Click and Paleczny's sea of nodes (1995), Reissmann et al.'s RVSDG (2020, a demand-dependence graph with structured control and strict SSA), Lattner and Adve's LLVM (2004), Haas et al.'s WebAssembly (2017, a formally specified stack machine with structured control and a mechanised soundness proof), and MLIR (2021, a framework of dialects rather than one IR) — [Click & Paleczny, A simple graph-based intermediate representation, IR'95](https://doi.org/10.1145/202529.202534); [Reissmann et al., RVSDG: An intermediate representation for optimizing compilers, TECS 2020](https://doi.org/10.1145/3391902); [Lattner & Adve, LLVM, CGO 2004](https://doi.org/10.1109/CGO.2004.1281665); [Haas et al., Bringing the web up to speed with WebAssembly, PLDI 2017](https://doi.org/10.1145/3062341.3062363); [Lattner et al., MLIR: Scaling compiler infrastructure for domain specific computation, CGO 2021](https://doi.org/10.1109/CGO51591.2021.9370308).

### Inferences
- UNCOL's lesson, read through Felleisen: a universal intermediate must sit at the *least* expressive point that every source is macro-expressible into, or translations become global and lose structure. LLVM/Wasm succeed by not claiming the lift and by accepting loss (types, structure, source equivalence are all discarded); MLIR succeeds by abandoning the single-IR premise altogether (dialects = an atlas, with explicit lowering between charts). MLIR is the industrial realisation of "many charts, transition maps", and it is explicitly *not* one global chart.
- RCE propositions U2 (faithful projection: Form → language) are in the easy direction and consistent with every IR precedent. U3 (lift by construction, "a lens per language built from grammar, syntax tree and semantics") is the UNCOL direction; for a Turing-complete source it cannot be total because the Form requires termination (README: "every program finishes"), so the lift is at best a partial map from the terminating fragment, and deciding membership in that fragment is undecidable. The correct claim is: the Form is a global chart on the sub-space of total, bounded-exploration behaviours, which is where ASM (sequential) applies; the parallel part rests on a contested thesis.
- SSA ↔ CPS ↔ ANF ↔ functional programs form a verified cycle of lossless transition maps (Kelsey/Appel/Flanagan); this is the best existing "flat" neighbourhood of the atlas and it covers exactly first-order control + named intermediates — i.e. the Form's Σc.

### Gaps
- No published formal comparison of RVSDG/sea-of-nodes with Mealy-machine or coalgebraic semantics was found.

## Q8. The physics analogies (gauge, holonomy, compactification, moduli, duality, landscape) in PL/CS: where serious, where decoration — and verdicts on each RCE metaphor

### Takeaway
Serious uses exist for: the Rosetta-stone (monoidal categories as the common language of physics, topology, logic and computation), "geometry" in GoI/game semantics, and "duality" (data/codata, CBV/CBN). "Gauge", "holonomy", "compactification", "moduli space" and "landscape" have no technical PL usage found; each has a precise groupoid/lens replacement, stated below.

### Cited Findings
- Baez and Stay's Rosetta Stone: symmetric monoidal (closed) categories are the common structure of Feynman diagrams (physics), cobordisms (topology), linear-logic proofs (logic) and programs (computation); the analogies are made exact at the level of category theory, not at the level of specific physical structures such as connections — [Baez & Stay, Physics, topology, logic and computation: a Rosetta Stone, 2009/2011](https://arxiv.org/abs/0903.0340).
- Lens laws: Foster et al. (2007) define well-behaved lenses by GetPut and PutGet, very-well-behaved by PutPut; Bancilhon–Spyratos (1981) showed well-behaved updates correspond to constant complements; Johnson, Rosebrugh and Wood (2012) show asymmetric lenses are algebras for a monad and relate them to (op)fibrations; Hofmann, Pierce and Wagner (2011) define symmetric lenses as spans/equivalence classes with a complement — [Foster et al., Combinators for bidirectional tree transformations, TOPLAS 2007](https://doi.org/10.1145/1232420.1232424); [Bancilhon & Spyratos, Update semantics of relational views, TODS 1981](https://doi.org/10.1145/319628.319634); [Johnson, Rosebrugh & Wood, Lenses, fibrations and universal translations, MSCS 2012](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0960129511000442); [Hofmann, Pierce & Wagner, Symmetric lenses, POPL 2011](https://doi.org/10.1145/1926385.1926428).
- The quantale-enriched and ultrametric semantics (Q5) are the only metric structures; the groupoid/path structure of equality is HoTT (Q5).

### Inferences — verdict table
1. **Behaviour space is a manifold.** *False as stated.* Behaviour space of Forms is the final coalgebra of (O × X)^E (Rutten 2000), a complete ultrametric space of causal stream functions; it is totally disconnected, dimension 0, no tangent spaces. Replace with: "behaviour space is a final coalgebra with the prefix ultrametric"; this is a theorem and gives a universal property instead of smoothness.
2. **Each language is a coordinate chart.** *Analogy with a theorem core.* A language with compositional semantics is a functor from its syntax (an initial algebra / Lawvere theory) to behaviour (Lawvere 1963); it is many-to-one, so it is a *cover* of its expressible sub-space, not an injective chart; it becomes a chart only after quotienting by program equivalence, and that quotient is a chart in the strict sense only when fully abstract (Plotkin 1977; AJM/HO 2000). Languages differ in expressible sub-space (Felleisen 1991), so charts have different domains — "complementary blind spots" is the Reynolds/Cook expression-problem fact.
3. **Translations are transition maps.** *Theorem in specific cases, analogy in general.* Exact, invertible transition maps are proven for: SSA↔CPS↔ANF↔functional (Kelsey, Appel, Flanagan); evaluator↔abstract machine (Ager et al.); defunctionalization↔refunctionalization (on defunctionalized-form programs, Danvy–Millikin); data↔codata (Hagino, Downen); CBV↔CBN (Curien–Herbelin, Wadler); sessions↔linear proofs (Wadler 2012); game strategies↔abstract machines (Danos–Herbelin–Regnier). General compilers are not invertible on representations and are lossless only modulo equivalence (Sabry–Felleisen: lossless on equational theory only after adding axioms).
4. **Semantics-preserving rewrites form a gauge group; orbits are e-classes.** *Analogy; precise replacement is a groupoid.* E-classes are connected components of the rewrite-proof groupoid (congruence closure, Tate 2009 / Willsey 2021); not a group action (no free transitive action, directed rewrites). The orbit is decidable exactly when the equivalence is: finite-state Mealy (Nerode/Hopcroft), KAT (PSPACE-complete), DPDA (Sénizergues); undecidable for unbounded data even in a total language (Rice). "Gauge = relational redundancy" (Rovelli) is apt conceptually.
5. **Cost is a metric; least-joule extraction is a geodesic.** *False.* Joule cost is a function on representations (a potential); extraction is argmin over a finite e-class (Prop. 3), with no path structure. The true metrics on code are information distance (Bennett et al. 1998, uncomputable) and behavioural (ultra)metrics / quantitative equational logic (Mardare et al. 2016; Dahlqvist–Neves 2022). Defensible restatement: extraction is MDL (Rissanen 1978) with a computable cost table — "shortest description under a model class", which is a theorem-grade correspondence, no geometry needed.
6. **Round-trip loss is holonomy; lossless = flat connection (lens laws).** *Analogy; can be made precise in a groupoid, then it is usually non-flat.* Lens laws GetPut/PutGet are retraction conditions (Foster 2007), not transport. A precise version: take the groupoid whose objects are representations and whose arrows are translations; the holonomy at a Form P is the set of automorphisms of P's e-class induced by cycles of translations L1→L2→…→L1. "Flat" (every cycle is identity on representations) is impossible whenever any language in the cycle is non-injective on behaviour or strictly less expressive (Felleisen 1991) — so the lift/project round trip is lossless only on the e-class (behaviour), never on the representation. RCE 3.6's "canonical serialization is injective" gives PutGet for the Form's own serialisation (a theorem, trivially), not for arbitrary source languages.
7. **Languages compactify some coordinates.** *Analogy only.* Nearest precise content: Felleisen's macro-expressibility (a missing feature forces a global translation) and the continuation coordinate (explicit in CPS/Form, implicit in direct style; Danvy 1994; Filinski 1994: continuations + state generate all monadic effects). No source uses "compactification" for this; "integrating out a coordinate" is a better physics word than compactifying, since the information is discarded, not made periodic.
8. **The Form is a global chart.** *Theorem on a sub-space, false globally.* Gurevich 2000 gives a step-for-step simulation of every sequential algorithm satisfying three postulates by an ASM; the Form adds totality (termination), hash-chained log and fixed vocabulary, so it covers exactly the total, bounded-exploration, sequential behaviours; the parallel case rests on the contested Blass–Gurevich postulates. On that sub-space the lift from higher-order charts is a theorem (Reynolds/Danvy/DHR game semantics) and the minimal representative is canonical for finite control (Nerode/Rutten). Outside it (non-terminating, reactive-codata, unbounded data) the Form is a partial chart whose domain membership is undecidable.
9. **Duality.** *Theorem.* data/codata, CBV/CBN, defunctionalize/refunctionalize, CPS/direct style, initial/final encodings are genuine involutions up to isomorphism, all cited above.
10. **Moduli space / landscape.** *Decoration.* No PL usage found; the e-graph over a fixed behaviour is the closest object (the "moduli" of representations of one behaviour), and the Nerode minimal machine is its canonical point when it exists.

### Gaps
- No paper found applying holonomy, connections, moduli or compactification to program translation; the groupoid/holonomy formalisation above is the author's construction, not cited.
- Secondary (nLab-level) sources on groupoid holonomy were not consulted; the definition used (automorphisms induced by cycles in a groupoid) is standard but should be checked against the intended Form-level definition of "loss".
