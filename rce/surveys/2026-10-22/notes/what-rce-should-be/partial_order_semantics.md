## KQ1. Event structures, Mazurkiewicz traces, pomsets, happens-before, vector clocks, Dilworth width; what a partial-order log looks like and how replay works

### Takeaway
A Mazurkiewicz trace is a total-order word *modulo commutation of independent events*; any linearization of a configuration of the corresponding event structure reads off the same trace, so a log that records one linearization plus the independence relation already *is* a partial-order log. RCE's W-PAR-1 footprint disjointness is exactly a Mazurkiewicz independence relation, and its per-tick "rank order then name-free key" is a canonical normal form (a Foata-like layering) that picks one representative deterministically. The question is not "sequence vs DAG" but "is the hash chain and the state hash invariant across representatives of the same trace" — today it is not (prev_hash chains the chosen linearization).

### Cited Findings
- Prime event structure: "A prime event structure is a triple ES = (E, ≤, #)" (events, causality, conflict); a configuration is a conflict-free, downward-closed set ("c = ↓c and (c × c) ∩ # = ∅"); linearizations of a configuration are linearizations of the poset (c, ≤c), and the traces represented are read off those linearizations; Proposition 4.5: the poset of configurations under inclusion is isomorphic to the trace structure under prefix order — [Thiagarajan, "Regular Trace Event Structures", BRICS RS-96-32, Sept 1996](https://tidsskrift.dk/brics/article/download/20012/17645).
- Mazurkiewicz traces are equivalence classes of words under an irreflexive symmetric independence relation I; trace event structures "constitute a common generalization of trees and traces"; maps `tes`/`est` are inverse up to isomorphism (Prop. 3.2) — [Thiagarajan 1996](https://tidsskrift.dk/brics/article/download/20012/17645).
- Confusion-freeness (the class where choices are local, i.e. determinate branching) is noted by Thiagarajan as "a much too drastic restriction" for his regularity purposes, but it is the class Huhn and Niebert proposed as well-behaved — [Thiagarajan 1996](https://tidsskrift.dk/brics/article/download/20012/17645).
- Vector clocks characterize happens-before in both directions (Mattern 1988 model: events are (process, index); a → b iff V(a) < V(b); concurrent iff clocks incomparable) — `spec/lean/RCE/VClock.lean` (`vc_iff`, `concurrent_iff`), which cites Mattern 1988 in its header; `TickClock.lean` proves the tick rules compute exactly such a counting clock (`count`, `HB_po`, `HB_trans`).
- RCE's `par` confluence is derived from a diamond on steps whose write set is disjoint from the other's free variables (`exec_comm`, `diamond`, `par_det` in `spec/lean/RCE/ParSem.lean`); Appendix C states W-PAR-1 "is the weakest footprint condition under which the diamond holds for every store" — `14-draft06-concurrency.md` lines 109-121.
- Elle (Jepsen) shows a partial order can be *recovered from observations* rather than logged: with append-only "traceable" objects every read reveals a prefix of the version order, giving wr/ww/rw edges; anomalies are cycles (Adya G0/G1c/G2) found by Tarjan SCC in O(V+E), versus NP-complete linearizability/serializability checking — [Kingsbury & Alvaro, "Elle", arXiv:2003.10554 (PVLDB 14)](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2003.10554).
- Kahn process networks: processes communicate over unbounded FIFO channels with blocking reads and non-blocking writes; the result is determinate (independent of scheduling) because each process is a continuous function on streams (Kahn 1974) — [Wikipedia, Kahn process networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kahn_process_networks); [Lee & Matsikoudis, Ptolemy "Process Networks and Rendezvous" chapter](https://ptolemy.berkeley.edu/books/Systems/chapters/ProcessNetworksandRendezvous.pdf).

### Inferences
- **What a partial-order log looks like.** Each entry carries `deps`: the hashes (or vector clock) of its immediate predecessors, and `hash = H(canonical(event), deps)`. The log is then a Merkle DAG; its hash is linearization-independent, which the current `prev_hash` chain is not. A *configuration* (consistent cut) is where a `state_hash` is meaningful; a per-entry `state_hash` only makes sense along one linearization.
- **How replay works.** Replay = pick a linearization of the DAG by a canonical rule, fold δ. RCE already has the canonical rule: rank order then name-free content-hash key (§4.1), and HC-15's (priority, source, seq) key. The theorem to prove is trace-invariance: for any two linearizations of the same configuration, fold gives the same state. For `par` this is T16; for replicated monotone regions it is CALM (KQ2); for everything else (select, read-modify-write, non-monotone rules) it is false and those events must be *ordered in the log* (they have deps on every event they could have observed).
- **Ticks are Foata layers.** A tick (6.8: every runnable task takes one step, all deliveries staged) is a layer of pairwise-independent events. Foata normal form of a trace is exactly the sequence of maximal independent layers; so the tick semantics is a canonical representative, not an extra assumption, *provided* the ops within a tick really commute. They do for channel ops (staged delivery) and `par`; they do not for `select` on two channels whose readiness is decided by the staging order of *previous* ticks (KQ4).
- **Dilworth width.** The minimum number of chains covering the happens-before poset is the number of sequential "sources"; a vector clock needs one component per such chain. In RCE those chains are the per-source sequences of HC-15 (source id, seq) plus per-actor mailbox order. So the natural shape is: one hash chain per source/actor (chains), plus cross-chain deps (the DAG edges) carried by messages. I found no fetched primary source for Dilworth/Charron-Bost in this run (see Gaps); the design inference does not depend on it.
- Elle's method implies RCE could keep D as a total order on the admitter but *check* trace-equivalence of a second replica's order by recovering the dependency graph from state hashes at cuts, i.e. "D is checked, not trusted" (E.2c law 1) extends to partial orders cheaply (linear-time SCC).

### Gaps
- No fetched source for Winskel's event-structure notes themselves, Lamport 1978, Mattern 1988, Dilworth's theorem or Charron-Bost's result that vector clock size must equal process count; the Lean file header cites Mattern 1988 and is the only in-hand reference. The writer should treat those as standard results, verify citations before publication.

## KQ2. CALM, Bloom, Dedalus, Hydro/DFIR, Flo, LVars, CRDTs; monotonicity inference in practice

### Takeaway
CALM (Hellerstein & Alvaro) says monotone ⇔ consistent coordination-free; "Keep CALM and CRDT On" extends it to *reads*: CRDT updates commute but only monotone *queries* are safe without coordination. Flo (POPL 2025) generalizes monotonicity to "eager execution" (results independent of when deltas arrive) and makes it a per-operator proof obligation that composes. RCE's Prop. 6 tag is the Bloom-style syntactic check; the practical gaps are (a) reads/queries of replicated state are not tagged, (b) `freeze`, `select`, `atomic`, `resolve` are the non-monotone coordination points and should be named as such in the host contract.

### Cited Findings
- CALM Theorem (Theorem 1): "A program has a consistent, coordination-free distributed implementation if and only if it is monotonic." Monotone: S ⊆ T ⇒ P(S) ⊆ P(T). Confluent: "produces the same set of outputs for any non-deterministic ordering and batching of a set of inputs"; confluent operations compose. "Coordination-freeness is equivalent to availability under partition." A program contains coordination if it requires messages under all data partitionings — [Hellerstein & Alvaro, "Keeping CALM", arXiv:1901.01930 (CACM 2020)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1901.01930).
- Bloom/Dedalus monotonicity analysis is syntactic: non-monotone ops (anti-join, set minus) "stand out in the language's syntax"; programs using only monotone syntax are conservatively certified deterministic; it misses "clever exceptions" that are semantically monotone; coordination can be added by program transformation (Blazes) and should be kept off the critical path — [Keeping CALM](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1901.01930).
- "Keep CALM and CRDT On": "CRDT guarantees extend only to data updates; observations of CRDT state are unconstrained and unsafe"; "monotone queries over CRDTs are exactly the queries that only need a local view of the system to be correct"; "with CRDTs, only non-monotone queries need to be ordered"; threshold queries over grow-only sets are sequentially consistent without coordination; no quantitative coordination-cost measurement in the paper — [Laddad, Power, Milano, Cheung, Crooks, Hellerstein, PVLDB 16(4), 2022](https://www.vldb.org/pvldb/vol16/p856-power.pdf).
- Flo (Laddad, Cheung, Hellerstein, Milano; POPL 2025 per the program page): parameterized dataflow over collection types (sequences, multisets, sets, lattice values, Z-sets); *streaming progress* ("will not block on a stream that may never terminate") and *eager execution* ("deterministic results even if we eagerly execute operators on partial inputs", Def. 3.1/4.1: any delta insertion point reaches the same stuck state); "we do not require there to be a unique small step"; confluence proofs rely on commutativity of processing order; LVar threshold reads must be monotone or "there could be non-determinism due to when the threshold is evaluated"; converting an LVar to a sequence is illegal; eager execution is strictly more general than monotonicity (covers DBSP retractions); fold is valid only over bounded inputs — [arXiv:2411.08274](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.08274); [POPL 2025 listing](https://popl25.sigplan.org/details/POPL-2025-popl-research-papers/9/Flo-a-Semantic-Foundation-for-Progressive-Stream-Processing).
- Hydro/Suki (Laddad, Cheung, Hellerstein, extended abstract, most recent ref 2024): choreographic staged programming; phase one runs the program to build a global dataflow graph, phase two slices by location and emits Rust; "Suki has been used to implement foundational protocols like Paxos with performance exceeding 50 kops/s out of the box"; no ordering/nondeterminism treatment in the abstract — [arXiv:2406.14733](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.14733v1). The runtime crate is `dfir_rs` (formerly Hydroflow) — [lib.rs/crates/dfir_rs](https://lib.rs/crates/dfir_rs).
- Anna KVS (Wu et al., ICDE 2018): wait-free actors, one thread per core, lattice (ACI merge) state, no shared state, gossip in epochs (~100 ms) off the critical path; "up to 10x" over Cassandra at 32 cores/node — [the morning paper, 27 Mar 2018](https://blog.acolyer.org/2018/03/27/anna-a-kvs-for-any-scale/). Keeping CALM reports that multiprocessor KVSs "can spend 90% of their time waiting for coordination" and Anna ran "over two orders of magnitude faster" — [Keeping CALM](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1901.01930).
- RCE's own LVar design follows Kuper et al. POPL 2014 (frozen bit, put-after-freeze error) and FHPC 2013 (threshold sets with pairwise incompatibility), proves quasi-determinism (T29q: "with freeze, whether a run errs can depend on the schedule"), and refuses racing freezes statically (W-LVAR-1) — `14-draft06-concurrency.md` lines 521-580, C.13 rows T29q/T29s/T29a.
- RCE's Merge laws are discharged by certificate over proven combinators (max/min, or/and, union/inter, pointwise map, lex on integer key) — never by sampling (W-CRDT-1, `MergeCert.lean`) — `14-draft06-concurrency.md` lines 455-494.

### Inferences
- RCE's Prop. 6 tag is the Bloom syntactic check and inherits its incompleteness; that is acceptable (sound), but the tag currently classifies *rules*, not *reads of replicated state*. Per "Keep CALM and CRDT On", a replica-local read in a replicated region is safe only if the read is monotone (threshold-shaped). Recommend: extend the monotone tag to reads: a read of `replicated state` is monotone iff it is an LVar-style threshold (`wait(Q)` with pairwise-incompatible Q) or a `Set.contains`/counter `>=` test; any other read of a replicated region is a coordination point and must carry a dep on a `host.Window` cut.
- Flo's eager-execution criterion is the right *definition* for "which RCE constructs survive a partial-order log": a construct survives iff its result is independent of the point at which inputs are delivered. By that test: `par`, `for…par`, `pscan`, workgroups, LVar `put`/`wait`, monotone rules, session channels (FIFO per pair) survive; `select` (rotating or nondet), `freeze`, `atomic`, `resolve`, supervisor restart decisions, deadlines do not and are the events that must be *ordered* (logged with full deps or stamped by a cut).
- Flo's "fold only over bounded inputs" maps onto RCE's `freeze`: a freeze is the bounding/sealing point. Under a partial-order log a `freeze` must be a log entry with deps on every `put` it seals (i.e. it is the Foata layer boundary for that LVar); W-LVAR-1 already forces this structurally inside `par` (freeze after join).
- Hydro's choreographic approach (one program, sliced by location, Rust emitted) is structurally what RCE's `node N {A, B}` + emitters do; the difference is that Hydro types streams by boundedness/ordering so the compiler knows where coordination is needed. RCE could reuse the idea: a `chan` whose element type is a `Merge` lattice is an unordered stream (coordination-free merge), a plain `chan` is an ordered stream (FIFO, single source).

### Gaps
- No primary Hydro 2025 paper fetched beyond the Suki abstract; the DFIR crate page gives no semantics. The claim that Hydro compiles monotone fragments to coordination-free code is not verified from a fetched source in this run.
- No published measurement of monotonicity-inference precision (how often the syntactic tag refuses a semantically monotone program) was found.

## KQ3. Deterministic parallelism: Kahn networks, DPJ, Cilk, ParaSail, Rust Send/Sync + rayon, Concurrent Revisions

### Takeaway
All deterministic-parallelism systems reduce to one of three disciplines: (1) non-interference by effects/regions (DPJ, RCE `par` footprints, Rust ownership), (2) determinate dataflow by blocking FIFO reads (Kahn), (3) deterministic *merge* at join (Concurrent Revisions, LVars, CRDTs). RCE already has all three; the result to import is Concurrent Revisions' determinacy theorem as the model for `atomic`/fork-join over *conflicting* state, and Overbeek's finding that merge functions must not depend on identifiers (RCE's name-free rule, independently derived).

### Cited Findings
- DPJ (Bocchino, Adve, Adve, Snir; the Cornell summary cites the HotPar 2009 position paper; the full system is OOPSLA 2009 — verify): regions, region path lists, per-method effect summaries, `cobegin`/`foreach`; the compiler statically infers non-interference; determinism "enforced at compile time"; proved sound on Core DPJ; `commuteswith` lets interfering methods run in parallel but correctness is on the programmer — [Cornell CS 6120 blog, 2020](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/courses/cs6120/2020fa/blog/parallel-java/); [Bocchino dissertation award page](https://sigplan.org/Awards/Dissertation/2010_bocchino.pdf).
- Kahn networks: determinate because each process is a continuous function from input histories to output histories under blocking reads; the merge (non-deterministic select) is what breaks it — [Ptolemy chapter](https://ptolemy.berkeley.edu/books/Systems/chapters/ProcessNetworksandRendezvous.pdf); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kahn_process_networks).
- Concurrent Revisions (Burckhardt, Baldassin, Leijen, OOPSLA 2010; Burckhardt & Leijen, ESOP 2011): fork snapshots the parent store; join merges with a per-location isolation type (versioned: joinee wins; cumulative: v2 + v3 − v1); every pair of states has a unique greatest common ancestor (merge base). Determinacy theorem: outcomes equal up to renaming of identifiers regardless of schedule, provided no nondeterministic primitives and each revision joined at most once; merge functions "must not depend on nondeterministic details such as the particular identifiers appearing in values. Otherwise determinacy fails." Contrast: transactional memory outcome depends on which transaction commits first. Mechanized by Overbeek (CPP 2020, AFP 2018), who found the fork side condition had to be strengthened — [Overbeek, arXiv:1912.09741](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.09741); [Burckhardt OOPSLA 2010 PDF](https://microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/revisions-oopsla2010.pdf).
- RCE's `par` emitters run branches on threads/workers where the target allows, and "in every case the store, the outputs and the trap point are those of source order"; a `par` waiting on LVars is run by restart, sound because joins are idempotent — `14-draft06-concurrency.md` lines 743-756.
- RCE reductions and `pscan` use one fixed power-of-two tree so float results are bit-identical across fabrics (G13 closed; HC-12) — `14-draft06-concurrency.md` lines 290-317, 495-520.

### Inferences
- RCE's W-PAR-1 is DPJ's non-interference without the annotation burden (footprints inferred from place paths); the Lean core covers only variables, and C.13 states the link to the full evaluator is vectors, not proof. Partial-order logging does not change this: `par` produces no log entries (W-PAR-2 forbids effects), so it is invisible to D and already linearization-free.
- Concurrent Revisions is the missing theory for RCE `atomic` (C.11.10) and for `with budget … else` (which already restores entry state): both are fork/join with a merge. CR's "joined at most once" and "merge base = unique greatest common ancestor" correspond to a DAG log where an `atomic` batch is one entry with deps on every actor's last entry — the merge base is the join of those vector clocks.
- Cilk/rayon/ParaSail: not fetched (see Gaps). What matters for RCE is already settled: rayon-style ordered reductions require a fixed tree for floats, which RCE has (HC-12 derivation, law 6 of E.2c).

### Gaps
- Cilk determinacy-race detection (Feng & Leiserson), ParaSail, and rayon ordered reductions were not fetched; no citations in hand.

## KQ4. Deterministic simulation and replay: FoundationDB, Antithesis, TigerBeetle, Jepsen/Elle, rr, Temporal/Restate/Durable Objects; nondeterminism and step-function versioning (G7)

### Takeaway
Every production replay system logs *exactly the nondeterministic choices* and re-derives the rest, which is RCE's E.2c law 1. The two things RCE should import are (a) rr's and Temporal's treatment of "the schedule is an input" — rr records context-switch points by retired-branch count, Temporal records nondeterministic markers (`GetVersion`) in history and *pins* executions that passed the point to the old code path, and (b) the durable-execution convention that code versioning is a logged marker with "already passed ⇒ old behaviour", which is what G7's RESOLVE stamp does. Under a partial-order log, a δ change is a *cut*, i.e. an entry every later entry depends on: it is inherently a coordination point, consistent with CALM (changing δ is non-monotone).

### Cited Findings
- Antithesis: runs "regular non-deterministic software inside a deterministic hypervisor"; DST makes "some or all layers of the testing stack deterministic"; controls clocks, thread interleaving and system randomness; FoundationDB's simulation-first framework (every nondeterministic component pluggable) "became the basis" of Antithesis; practical DST pioneered at FoundationDB and AWS around 2010; "execution can be rolled back and inspected at multiple points in time" — [Antithesis docs, DST](https://antithesis.com/docs/resources/deterministic_simulation_testing/).
- TigerBeetle VOPR: "a simulated environment where an entire cluster, running real code, is subjected to all kinds of network, storage and process faults, at 1000x speed", running "24/7 on 1024 cores"; "All transfers are executed one-by-one, on a single core" — [TigerBeetle safety docs](https://docs.tigerbeetle.com/concepts/safety/).
- rr (O'Callahan et al., May 2017): records syscall results, signal timing, RDTSC; only one recorded thread runs at a time; context switches placed by the retired-conditional-branch counter plus register state; cannot reproduce weak-memory data races or shared memory with unrecorded processes; ARM unsupported (LL/SC nondeterministic); record overhead under 2× except `make` (7.85×); replay often cheaper than record — [arXiv:1705.05937](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1705.05937). Chaos mode (randomized scheduling to expose races) is described in [O'Callahan's 2016 post](https://robert.ocallahan.org/2016/02/introducing-rr-chaos-mode.html) (not fetched).
- Temporal: "The Temporal Platform requires that Workflow code is deterministic"; non-deterministic work goes in Activities; "When `workflow.GetVersion()` is run for the new Workflow Execution, it records a marker in the Event History"; later calls for the same change ID "will always return the given version number"; "A Workflow that has already passed this `GetVersion()` call before it was introduced returns `DefaultVersion`"; a history/code conflict "would cause the Workflow to fail with a nondeterminism error"; Worker Versioning tags Workers in Deployment Versions so old Workers run old code; the pre-2025 experimental scheme was slated for removal March 2026; the SDK runtime check "does not perform a thorough check" — [Temporal Go versioning docs](https://docs.temporal.io/develop/go/versioning).
- Restate: "Restate tracks every step of your code execution in a journal"; "Each step is recorded in the journal and won't repeat on retry"; on failure it "replays the journal, skipping completed steps and resuming from exactly where it left off"; Virtual Objects: "Only one handler can modify state at a time"; "State updates are recorded alongside execution steps" — [Restate concepts](https://docs.restate.dev/concepts/durable_execution). Journaling of explicit nondeterministic results (`ctx.run`) is documented separately — [Restate journaling results](https://docs.restate.dev/develop/ts/journaling-results) (not fetched).
- Elle recovers the dependency graph from observations with append-only objects and finds anomalies as cycles in O(V+E) — [Elle](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2003.10554).
- RCE already: "Replay re-runs the scheduler on the logged inputs and refuses an entry unless the step it logs is the next step the scheduler takes" (HC-6); "an input is also refused if re-running on it would change a step already in D, so an input never rewrites the past"; `select nondet` arms are logged in `host.Step` — `16-host-contract.md` lines 86-130. Restart decisions are inputs (`RestartIn`), replayed "even after the strategy, bound or RESTART_WINDOW in the source has changed", with positional ids and a tree-schema migration rule — `14-draft06-concurrency.md` lines 613-680.
- G7 closed by a RESOLVE stamp (height, δ version, spec and fit digests) written by commit and checked by the fold; "commit refused inside a tick" — `conc-gap-register.md` row G7.

### Inferences
- RCE's HC-6 rule is stricter than Temporal's: Temporal tolerates a code change that preserves the command sequence; RCE refuses any input that would change a logged step. That is the right default for a proof-carrying engine but means every δ change needs the RESOLVE-stamp mechanism, never silent redeploy.
- **G7 under a partial order.** With a DAG log, "a recorded log height" is not well defined; the equivalent is a *cut*: the RESOLVE entry must list as deps the frontier (last entry of every chain) and every later entry must depend on it. Equivalently the admitter closes a window (`host.Window`) and RESOLVE is its first entry. This makes "commit refused inside a tick" a theorem rather than a rule: an in-flight step is one that is not below the cut, and it folds under the old δ (Temporal's `DefaultVersion` behaviour).
- rr's lesson for RCE emitters that run `par` branches on OS threads: determinism of the *result* (source-order merge) is enough for D, but a divergence in a thread-run branch cannot be replayed step-wise unless the schedule is recorded. Since `par` branches have no effects (W-PAR-2), recording is unnecessary; this is exactly why the construct survives.
- TigerBeetle's "one core, one-by-one" and Restate's single-writer Virtual Objects are the per-actor chain of KQ1; both get determinism per chain and push cross-chain order into the message log, which is the shape recommended in KQ6.
- Elle suggests a cheap conformance check for a partial-order D: give replicated-state regions append-only (list/set-union) fields; replay on another host, then verify that the recovered dependency graph is acyclic against the recorded deps. This is linear-time and needs no consensus.

## KQ5. Actors vs channels vs session types; Erlang/OTP supervision as library vs language; Pony; structured concurrency

### Takeaway
Actors with causal delivery (Pony) are data-race free but *not* deterministic: "message ordering stays non-deterministic where no causal relationship exists". Channels in Kahn form (single reader, blocking) are deterministic; `select` is the non-determinate merge. Session types give deterministic per-channel protocols and, with priorities or tree-shaped graphs, deadlock freedom (RCE T28d/T28p/T28g/T28a already mechanize this). RCE's supervision-as-language-feature with restart-as-logged-input is stronger than OTP's library form because the restart decision is in D.

### Cited Findings
- Pony (Clebsch PhD, Imperial, Oct 2017): six reference capabilities (iso, trn, ref, val, box, tag) defined by which aliases they deny, locally and globally; rule: if one unit of execution can write to a structure no other can read it, enforced statically so the runtime has no locks; within one node messages are causally delivered (a message is delivered only after all its causes, transitively); across nodes only pairwise FIFO, causality layered via tree networks at O(log n) latency; "the thesis does not claim Pony is deterministic ... This can produce logical race conditions even when the program is data-race free" — [Clebsch, "Pony: Co-designing a Type System and a Runtime"](https://www.ponylang.io/media/papers/codesigning.pdf).
- RCE actors: one message per tick in mailbox order, delivered in actor-id order, ids are name-free positional paths (G20), supervisors are static declarations whose restart decisions are logged inputs — `08-steps-loops-concurrency.md` lines 187-190; `14-draft06-concurrency.md` lines 588-680.
- RCE sessions: linear endpoints (T28l), duality, deadlock freedom on tree-shaped session graphs (T28d) or acyclic priority orders (T28p, after Kobayashi 2006 and Padovani 2014), multiparty global types with plain merge (T28g) and full merge over per-pair FIFO queues (T28a, after Honda-Yoshida-Carbone 2008 and Hou-Yoshida-Kuhn 2024); open: both ends delivered separately to a third process, priorities for recursive/branching global types — `14-draft06-concurrency.md` lines 323-454.
- Kahn: determinism needs blocking reads and single-reader FIFOs; the merge is the non-determinate operator — [Ptolemy chapter](https://ptolemy.berkeley.edu/books/Systems/chapters/ProcessNetworksandRendezvous.pdf).
- RCE's rotating `select` replaced "always minimal arm" (Go solves starvation with a random choice; "the rotation gives the same guarantee without randomness") and the choice "is a function of ids, payloads and per-select counters" — `08-steps-loops-concurrency.md` lines 192-204.

### Inferences
- Compositional deterministic semantics ranking for RCE: sessions (per-pair FIFO, protocol fixed, deadlock-free fragment proved) > Kahn-shaped channels (single reader, no select) > actors (deterministic only because the global tick totally orders deliveries) > `select` over independent sources (deterministic only given the tick's total order).
- Under a partial-order log, actor determinism must come from the per-actor mailbox chain: a mailbox is an ordered merge of several senders' FIFOs, so the *merge order* is a nondeterministic choice and must be logged (as HC-15 already does for host inputs via the window key). T28a's one-FIFO-per-ordered-pair is exactly the partial-order delivery model; the actor mailbox should be defined as the canonical merge (HC-15 key) of those per-pair FIFOs at each cut, which makes T27 (actor answers as its sequential specification) hold without a global tick.
- Rotating `select` is deterministic only relative to a total order of readiness. Keep it, but under a partial-order log every plain `select` with more than one arm on channels from different chains must log its taken arm (today only `select nondet` does, C.5/T21). Alternatively refuse multi-source `select` outside the admitter's window discipline. The former is cheaper and keeps programs.
- OTP supervision as a library would have the restart policy outside D; RCE's language-level supervisor with `RestartIn` entries and the tree-as-schema migration rule is the Temporal-marker pattern applied to supervision and should be kept.
- Structured concurrency (Trio/Kotlin/Swift 6): RCE's `scope` with "a join handle cannot leave its scope" (W-SCOPE-1, T26) is already structured; no change needed for a partial-order log, since a scope end is a join with deps on every child's last entry.

### Gaps
- Trio/Kotlin/Swift 6 structured concurrency and Erlang/OTP sources were not fetched in this run; the comparisons above rest on the RCE spec text and Pony/Kahn sources only.

## KQ6. Energy and coordination; where coordination-free gives measurable savings; recommendations for D, δ, ticks, the monotone tag, Appendix C constructs and HC rules

### Takeaway
No source gives joules per coordination round; the measurable evidence is time: up to 90% of a contended KVS's time waiting on coordination and two orders of magnitude from removing it (Anna), and one quorum round per window in RCE's own E.2c law 5. The strongest design conclusion: keep a *canonical* linearization (do not let targets choose it for non-monotone events), make the hash of D linearization-invariant via a DAG of per-chain hashes, and let only CALM-monotone replicated regions apply entries in local order.

### Cited Findings
- "state-of-the-art multiprocessor key-value stores can spend 90% of their time waiting for coordination"; Anna "ran over two orders of magnitude faster by eliminating that coordination"; "coordination can dramatically slow down computation, or stop it altogether" — [Keeping CALM](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1901.01930).
- Anna gossips in time-based epochs, off the critical path, bounding GET staleness by the epoch — [the morning paper 2018](https://blog.acolyer.org/2018/03/27/anna-a-kvs-for-any-scale/).
- RCE E.2c law 5: "a minority side cannot commit, so it cannot admit (HC-20). Without a partition each window costs one quorum round; windows batch inputs to amortise it, the same trade Calvin makes with its epochs" — `16-host-contract.md` lines 337-341.
- RCE cost semantics: budgets count operations, "not a measurement of time or energy (est_j is separate)"; `c_chan` = 8, `c_spawn` = 32, `c_fork` = 16; LVar threshold tests charge no fuel or budget "so the cost of a wait does not depend on how often a schedule rechecks it" — `14-draft06-concurrency.md` lines 207-244, 529-532.
- Energy-oriented consensus work exists (e.g. "EESMR: Energy Efficient BFT-SMR for the masses", arXiv 2304.04998; "Blizzard", arXiv 2201.02002) — found by search, not fetched — [EESMR](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.04998); [Blizzard](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.02002).

### Inferences (recommendations in RCE terms)
1. **Log shape D (adopt, modified hypothesis).** Keep D as the admitter's canonical total order for *admission* (HC-1, HC-15), but change the entry format so the hash is linearization-independent: `entry = {chain, seq_in_chain, event, deps: [hash…], hash}` with `hash = SHA-256(canonical{chain, seq, event, deps})`, one chain per source/actor/task (Dilworth chains), `deps` = the hashes the event causally observed (the message it consumed, the LVar puts a threshold read saw, the cut for a window). `prev_hash` becomes the chain-local dep; `state_hash` is recorded only at cuts (`host.Window`, `Tick`, `host.Term`, RESOLVE), where a configuration is well defined. Prop. 4 then becomes: two replays of one D in *any* linearization consistent with deps give the same state hash at every cut — provable from T16/T17-style commutation for events with disjoint deps and from CALM for monotone rules.
2. **Targets choosing linearization (refuse in general, adopt for monotone replicated regions).** For non-monotone events (select, resolve, read-modify-write, atomic, freeze, restart) the admitter's canonical key (priority, source, seq) stays normative and such entries get deps on the whole current frontier (a cut). For `replicated state` regions whose every rule is monotone (Prop. 6), replicas may apply entries in arrival order; T31 should be replaced by a CALM/CRDT statement: the state at any configuration is the join of the entries' contributions, independent of order (this is what makes T31 non-trivial). The existing `Merge` certificate machinery (W-CRDT-1) is the proof carrier.
3. **Ticks (keep, reinterpret).** A tick is a Foata layer / consistent cut, not a global instant. Deadlines (`with deadline n`, `step … deadline n`, `RESTART_WINDOW`) count window cuts, not entries; this keeps HC-2 ("only the admitter reads clocks") and T22 unchanged while removing the dependence on a single total order inside a window.
4. **Monotone tag (extend).** Tag reads as well as rules: monotone read = LVar threshold `wait(Q)` or lattice-threshold test; non-monotone read = anything else on a replicated region or an LVar `freeze`. Flo's eager-execution criterion is the definition to state in §3.4: an operation is monotone iff its result is independent of the delivery point of any delta. Keep the tag sound-not-complete; refuse a stored tag that differs from the derived one (already the rule).
5. **Appendix C constructs, survive/refuse under a partial-order log:**
   - `par`, `for … par`, `pscan`, workgroups/`barrier`: survive unchanged; no log entries (W-PAR-2), fixed trees (HC-12). Barrier = layer boundary.
   - `spawn`/`chan`: survive; a spawn is a chain creation with a dep on the spawner's entry (positional id, G20); a channel send/receive is a cross-chain dep. Single-reader channels are Kahn-determinate and need no choice logging.
   - `select` (rotating): survives but every taken arm of a multi-chain select must be a logged entry (today only `nondet` is, T21); the rotation counter stays as the canonical choice rule so the entry is derivable and checked on replay, as HC-6 does for `host.Step`.
   - actors: survive; the mailbox is the HC-15 canonical merge of per-sender FIFOs at each cut (T28a's queue model); one message per tick stays.
   - sessions: survive as-is; they are the best-behaved construct (per-pair FIFO = partial order; deadlock freedom proved on the tree/priority fragments).
   - LVars: survive and become the primary coordination-free construct; `put` entries commute (T29), `wait` entries depend on the puts they observed, `freeze` is a cut on that LVar.
   - `atomic`: survives as a *coordination point*: one entry with deps on every participating actor's chain head (Concurrent Revisions' merge base); W-ATOM-2 (no cross-node atomic) is consistent with keeping coordination inside one admitter term.
   - supervisors: survive; `RestartIn` is an entry on the actor's chain with a dep on the `Refused` entry; tree-as-schema migration rule unchanged.
   - `replicated state`: survives only in the monotone fragment (recommendation 2); a non-monotone rule in a replicated region is admitted but its entries must sit at cuts, which is where the one quorum round of law 5 is paid.
   - budgets/deadlines/priorities: survive; budgets are per-input and schedule-independent (HC-7a, LVar waits free); deadlines count cuts (recommendation 3); priority admission is part of the canonical key (HC-9, HC-15).
6. **Host contract.** Add HC rule: *deps are logged facts*; replay refuses an entry whose deps are not all in D before it (the DAG analogue of "an input never rewrites the past", HC-6) and refuses D if the recovered dependency graph has a cycle (Elle's O(V+E) check). RESOLVE/InstallHandler/RetireHandler (G7/G8) must be cut entries (deps = full frontier), and an entry not below the cut folds under the old δ version: Temporal's `DefaultVersion` rule, made a theorem.
7. **Energy claim to make, honestly.** RCE can claim a *structural* saving (coordination-free monotone regions pay zero quorum rounds, non-monotone ones pay one per window) and cite Anna's 90%/100× time figures as evidence that the saving is large where contention is; it should not claim joule figures until `est_j` receipts measure a monotone vs non-monotone workload on the same fabric.

### Gaps
- No source quantifying energy (J) per consensus round or per coordination barrier on commodity hardware; EESMR/Blizzard were found but not read.
- No source compares hash-chain vs Merkle-DAG log verification cost; the recommendation to hash per chain plus deps is inferred from the Mazurkiewicz/event-structure results, not from a measured system.
- Whether RCE's T16 core (variables only, no loops/calls/data) can be extended to prove linearization-invariance of a DAG fold over the full evaluator is open in the spec itself (C.13 note) and unchanged by this research.
