# Proof-engineering practice for keeping spec, checker and mechanized proofs in sync (design input for the Reality Code Engine)

Grounding read before searching (2026-10-22): the RCE paper at research.openie.dev/papers/rce/ (sections 3.7, 5.3, 6), and in the local clone `spec/book/00-overview.md`, `10-theorems.md`, `INDEX.md`, `READ-PROTOCOL.md`, `spec/proposals/soundness.md`, `spec/proposals/closing-the-loop.md`, `spec/lean/` (89 `.lean` files, 23,134 lines, toolchain `leanprover/lean4:v4.12.0`), `spec/checker/tests/core_rules.rs`, `scripts/claim-counts.py`, `.github/workflows/{ci,pages,tree-sitter-sync,xarch-parity}.yml`.

Summary of RCE's current sync machinery, as found in the repo (used as the baseline for every recommendation below):

- One normative text (`spec/book/*.md`); `rce-book build` rewrites per-chapter `<!-- body-sha256 -->` footers, `INDEX.md` and the joined `RCE-SPEC.md`; `rce-spec-check` fails if book and single file diverge (`spec/book/READ-PROTOCOL.md`).
- The grammar is the EBNF of chapter 3 read from the Markdown, LALR(1)-built (0 conflicts; 974 / 1152 / 1214 states for base / +C.2 / +F.13), and the examples and rejects are run through the LALR tables, the Rust parser and the tree-sitter port alike (`00-overview.md`).
- `scripts/claim-counts.py --check` fails when a figure quoted in the prose disagrees with the source; it is explicitly "a count, not a pass" and only runs `rce-spec-check` itself.
- Checker → Lean generation exists in two places: `checker/tests/core_rules.rs` generates `RCE/Core/Rules.lean` (one `#check` per rule in `core_rules::CORE_TY_RULES`) and "fails when book 5.3 names a rule the table lacks"; `LVarRules.lean` is generated from `conc::LVAR_RULES` (`soundness.md`, S2).
- Vectors: generated `*Vectors.lean` files are checked `by decide` (17 Lean files use `by decide`; `ModelVectors.lean` alone has 40 theorem declarations); the S6 adapter `rce-conform-lean` runs Lean's `runMain` under the conformance harness: 122 pass / 1 fail / 1,867 unsupported, later 1,208 / 17 / 766 after statements were added; "Anything outside the subset is `unsupported`, never a pass" (`soundness.md`).
- Theorem status vocabulary in the paper: Proved (P), Proved on a semantics (P*), on a model (M, M+ with kernel-checked vectors), Vectors (MV), Tested (T), Checked mechanically (C). `10-theorems.md` states the Lean build has "no `sorry`, no added axioms (`#print axioms` lists only propext, Quot.sound, Classical.choice)". T42–T56 are "stated and unproved" except T48, T51, T53 (proved on models in `RCE/Discharged.lean`, each annotated "not a proof about the checker/host/emitted code"). "None of T16–T31 is a proof about the Rust reference evaluator."
- Kani: 10 harnesses, Kani 0.67.0, 0 of 7,425 checks failed; the certificate hash lives in `proofs/kani.cert` and gates promotion (paper 5.3).
- The paper's own audit numbers disagree across commits (2,192 theorem constants over 31 files at `1eb5f02`; 4,877 vs 4,875 at `e416fd0` over 58 files) and the paper says "Neither number says anything about how many theorems are proved about the Rust evaluator".

The repo's own honest statement of the gap: "The Lean core covers scalars, products, sums, bounded loops; the Rust checker covers a far larger language; the two are linked by generated vectors and differential tests, not proof" (paraphrase of `soundness.md` S1/S6/S7 and `10-theorems.md`). S7 "Refinement" is staged as (a) differential agreement, (b) ANF simulation (T34), (c) per-target forward simulation; only (a) and a core fragment of (b) exist.

## KQ1. How mechanized language specs (WebAssembly/SpecTec, CakeML, CompCert, Rust, K) handle the spec-text vs formal-model gap

### Takeaway
The projects that closed the gap did it by making the formal definition the *only* authored artifact and generating prose, LaTeX, interpreter and prover definitions from it (SpecTec, adopted by the Wasm CG in March 2025); or by making the executable semantics literally the spec with a test suite as the conformance oracle (K/KEVM, Rust's 2026 executable-spec goal). Nobody keeps two hand-written sources (prose and formal model) in sync by review; they either generate one from the other or they accept drift and bound it with conformance tests.

### Cited Findings
- SpecTec (PLDI 2024, DOI 10.1145/3656440, presented 27 June 2024) is a DSL and toolchain intended as "a single source of truth" for the Wasm semantics; from one definition it generates a typeset specification "with formal definitions and prose pseudocode descriptions" and a meta-level interpreter; the generated interpreter "passes 100% of the applicable official Wasm test suite"; it found "ten errors in five proposals slated for the next Wasm version" — [PLDI 2024 page](https://pldi24.sigplan.org/details/pldi-2024-papers/64/Bringing-the-WebAssembly-Standard-up-to-Speed-with-SpecTec); [publication record, authors Youn, Shin, Lee, Ryu, Breitner, Gardner, Lindley, Pretnar, Rao, Watt, Rossberg](https://www.doc.ic.ac.uk/~pg/publications/Youn2024WasmSpectec.html)
- On 27 March 2025 Rossberg reported the Wasm Community Group "voted to adopt [SpecTec] for authoring future editions of the Wasm spec"; backends: LaTeX "with bells and whistles (like cross-references)", "English prose(!) in Sphinx mark-up", "Definitions (and possibly lemmata) for the Coq proof assistant", a machine-readable AST, a meta interpreter, and a recently started `.wast` test-file backend; "The official Wasm 3.0 spec will be produced with SpecTec"; "SpecTec will be automatically invoked as part of the spec build process" — [webassembly.org news, 2025-03-27](https://webassembly.org/news/2025-03-27-spectec/)
- Same post: what remains manual — "the sections on numeric primitives and on the text format are currently still written manually"; the meta interpreter check "only tells us something about execution" because decoding and validation still use the OCaml reference interpreter; the test suite's coverage has been "variable"; Coq soundness "can already handle soundness for the Wasm 1.0 subset. Scaling to the full language is work in progress." Historically "The formal spec was written before the prose. The prose was created by manually transliterating the formal rules." — [webassembly.org news, 2025-03-27](https://webassembly.org/news/2025-03-27-spectec/)
- Pre-SpecTec Wasm mechanization: Watt's 2017 Isabelle/HOL mechanisation of the draft "found bugs in the original specification that were fixed before publication"; FM 2021 "Two Mechanisations of WebAssembly 1.0" (Watt, Rao, Pichon-Pharabod, Bodin, Gardner, Nov 2021, DOI 10.1007/978-3-030-90870-6_4) gives WasmCert-Isabelle and WasmCert-Coq, "particularly complete and close to the published language standard", with an updated type-soundness result; WasmCert-Isabelle has a verified executable interpreter that "relies on an OCaml harness"; WasmCert-Coq's end-to-end verified interpreter without a harness was "ongoing work" — [VTSS publication page](https://vtss.doc.ic.ac.uk/publications/Watt2021Two.html)
- CakeML: semantics "specified in higher-order logic (HOL) in a functional big-step style"; the verified backend goes from AST to machine code for 6 architectures through 8 intermediate languages; "Frontend 1" is a proof-producing translator from HOL functions to CakeML AST with a proof the AST behaves like the function; parser (PEG) and type inferencer are "proved sound and complete against declarative specifications"; the compiler (frontend 2 + backend) is itself a HOL function, so feeding it through frontend 1 + backend yields "a verified binary that implements the compiler" (bootstrapping in the logic); POPL 2014 paper received the 2024 Most Influential POPL Paper award; the page recommends synthesis via the translator "for most new verified code" over post-hoc CFML proofs — [cakeml.org](https://cakeml.org/)
- CompCert's TCB (Monniaux & Boulmé, ESOP 2022, erratum 11 Oct 2022): the front end (preprocessing, lexing, typedef pre-parser, elaboration) is "most of them unverified, but trusted" while the parser is verified against an attribute LR(1) grammar via Menhir and the typechecker is verified; the formal semantics of C and Asm are trusted and validated by testing with Csmith and YarpGen; unverified assembly printing and pseudo-instruction expansion are "the main source of bugs"; authors' primary recommendation is to validate the assembly/ABI specification against the real platform, citing SAIL as a tested ISA semantics — [arXiv 2201.10280](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.10280)
- Rust, 2026 project goal "End-to-End Executable Rust Specification": build "a specification for the whole of Rust, from surface syntax down to detailed semantics, that is both legible and executable", as a literate Rust program that is also an interpreter; MiniRust = dynamic semantics ("what is/isn't UB"), a-mir-formality = static semantics (trait solving, borrow checking) using MiniRust for dynamics; the Reference "is the current English source of truth" and its grammar would be reused; English text "cannot be directly tested on actual programs"; spec sections split into authoritative and non-authoritative with a warning when a program hits a non-authoritative one; test suite "compares its behavior with rustc"; "a multi-year effort", one FTE requested and currently unfunded; owner Nadrieril, champion Niko Matsakis — [goals.rust-lang.org/2026/executable-spec](https://goals.rust-lang.org/2026/executable-spec.html)
- Ferrocene Language Specification: the Rust Project announced adoption of the FLS on 2025-03-26 (post exists at that URL; body not retrievable via fetch in this session) — [blog.rust-lang.org 2025-03-26](https://blog.rust-lang.org/2025/03/26/adopting-the-fls/)
- KEVM (CSF 2018): "an executable formal specification of the EVM's bytecode stack-based language" in K, evaluated for correctness and performance against the official Ethereum test suite; used to verify two ERC20 implementations — [FSL publication page](https://fsl.cs.illinois.edu/publications/hildenbrandt-saxena-zhu-rodrigues-daian-guth-moore-zhang-park-rosu-2018-csf.html)

### Inferences
- RCE already has the "spec text is the machine-read source" property for the grammar only (EBNF in `03-syntax.md` → LALR tables → three parsers). The typing rules and the evaluation rules of chapters 5–8 are prose plus an independently written Rust checker plus an independently written Lean core; that is exactly the pre-SpecTec Wasm situation (formal rules hand-transliterated into prose, verified by people) that SpecTec was built to eliminate.
- SpecTec's architecture maps onto RCE's three artifacts: SpecTec file = the rule tables (`core_rules.rs`, `LVAR_RULES`) if those were promoted from "test fixtures" to the authored source; prose backend = chapter 5/7 rule paragraphs; interpreter backend = the Rust evaluator *or* a generated Lean `eval`; Coq backend = `Rules.lean` but with definitions, not only `#check`s. The partial step RCE already took (`core_rules.rs` → `Rules.lean`, build fails if 5.3 names a rule the table lacks) is the right direction but only checks *names*, not rule *content*.
- SpecTec's own residue is instructive: numeric primitives and text format stayed hand-written, and decoding/validation stayed in the OCaml interpreter. RCE's analogue (float formatting `float_text`, `cmp_canon`, NaN handling found to differ between book and reference in S3) is exactly where SpecTec also could not generate, so expect that chapter 7's primitive edge-case tables are the part that will stay "vectors, not generation".
- CompCert's lesson for RCE's TCB accounting: the unverified pretty-printer/expander is where the bugs were; RCE's analogue is `EmitPrint.lean` → rustc and the Python/JS emitters (T9-L "tested"), plus `anf.rs` which "binds fewer nodes and covers the surface language" while `Anf.lean` covers a core fragment. Those are RCE's TargetPrinter/AsmExpand.

- Could not fetch the full SpecTec paper or the FLS adoption post body (redirect stub). SpecTec's Lean backend: not mentioned in the 2025 post; whether one exists in the spec repo's `spectec/` tree was not verified.
- Ott/Lem, Skel, Redex "semantics-first" practice: not searched this session (budget); no citations recorded. Their relevance is as alternatives to a hand-rolled rule-table generator.
- CakeML TCB list and size numbers are not on cakeml.org; the POPL 2014 paper (DOI 10.1145/2535838.2535841) is listed in RCE's own "References not yet read" ledger and should be read before citing.

## KQ2. Conformance and differential testing: generators, test suites, extracted oracles

### Takeaway
Random differential testing (Csmith, 325+ bugs; "six CPU-years" against CompCert with zero wrong-code bugs in the verified middle end) is the cheapest high-yield assurance for the parts that are not proved, and its documented value is in *locating the proof boundary*: every Csmith bug in CompCert was in the unverified front end or the trusted assembler interface. The strongest oracle is an interpreter extracted from the semantics (CakeML's translator, SpecTec's meta interpreter, RCE's own `rce-conform-lean`), with the official test suite as the shared yardstick.

### Cited Findings
- Csmith (Yang, Chen, Eide, Regehr, PLDI 2011): "more than 325 previously unknown bugs" across GCC, LLVM, commercial compilers; randomized differential testing with no oracle (majority vote), avoiding the 191 kinds of undefined and 52 kinds of unspecified behavior in C99 by static analysis and run-time checks; CompCert's wrong-code bugs were in its unverified front end (integer promotions/implicit casts; the developer "later extended the verified portion to cover these") and a PowerPC 16-bit displacement overflow where "CompCert's semantics did not constrain the immediate's width, assuming the assembler would catch it"; for the in-development CompCert "about six CPU-years of testing" found no wrong-code errors: "the middle-end bugs we found in all other compilers are absent" — [PLDI 2011 preprint](https://www-old.cs.utah.edu/~regehr/papers/pldi11-preprint.pdf)
- CompCert's maintainers themselves treat the trusted C and Asm semantics as validated "by testing with Csmith and YarpGen"; impure OCaml oracles are "not trusted to return correct results" because their outputs are checked (translation validation); fuel constants (`num_iterations=10^12`) are a named trust point — [arXiv 2201.10280](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.10280)
- SpecTec: the generated meta interpreter run against the official test suite passes "all applicable tests", but the test suite's coverage is "variable" and a `.wast` generation backend was started to improve coverage "with less manual work" — [webassembly.org 2025-03-27](https://webassembly.org/news/2025-03-27-spectec/)
- WasmCert-Isabelle: verified executable interpreter (instantiation included) run through an OCaml harness — [VTSS page](https://vtss.doc.ic.ac.uk/publications/Watt2021Two.html)
- CakeML: proof-producing translation from HOL functions to CakeML AST ("proves the AST behaves like the HOL function") is the recommended route for new verified code — i.e., the executable artifact is *derived* from the logic definition, not re-implemented — [cakeml.org](https://cakeml.org/)
- Verify-Rust-Std (Le Blanc & Lam, arXiv 2510.01072v2, 8 Oct 2025): Kani solved 6 of 9 completed challenges, VeriFast 2, Flux and goto-transcoder 0 of 27 posted; 361 of 9,078 unsafe functions/safe abstractions in `core` (3.98%) and 10 of 714 in `std` (1.4%) carry Kani contracts; "No confirmed safety bugs so far" but "Two specification bugs were found and fixed"; Kani results "hold only within finite scope, relying on the 'small scope hypothesis' and unwinding assertions"; the paper recommends Miri alongside verification for bug-finding — [arXiv 2510.01072v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.01072v2)
- RCE's own numbers (grounding): fuzz 100,000 programs, 516,012/516,012 rule near-misses and 392,362/392,362 syntax near-misses refused; tree-sitter and LALR(1) agree on 4,977 syntax mutants; `rce-conform-lean` 122/1/1,867 then 1,208/17/766; the 1 fail (`r5-generic-call`) is "a reference or test finding, not a core one"; 16 of the 17 later fails are stale harvested float vectors — `spec/book/00-overview.md`, `spec/proposals/soundness.md` (local clone).

### Inferences
- RCE's conformance harness already honours `unsupported` as a distinct verdict, which is the right discipline (SpecTec's "only tells us something about execution" is the same honesty). The number to track is not pass count but *unsupported count by construct*, because that is a direct measure of the proof boundary; `soundness.md` already lists the largest unsupported groups (f64 557, non-`run` ops 633, compound statements 373, method/path calls 204, event runs 53). That list is the backlog ordering for S1–S4.
- Csmith's CompCert history says the fuzzer will find bugs at exactly the seams RCE labels "tested": front end (parser/elaboration into the core: `core_elab`), `anf.rs` vs `Anf.lean`, emitters/printers, and the "rustc implements MiniRust" assumption. `rce-fuzz` currently targets grammar acceptance and near-miss refusal; it does not yet generate *semantic* differential cases (reference evaluator vs Lean `runMain` vs emitted targets) from the grammar. That generator is the single highest-value addition and is cheap relative to proof.
- The Rust-std effort's 3.98% / 1.4% contract coverage after ~16 months, with no confirmed safety bugs, is the realistic yield for bounded model checking at scale: it certifies spec statements, finds spec bugs, and rarely finds code bugs. RCE's 10 Kani harnesses are the same kind of evidence; they should be reported as "bounded, N unwinds, properties X" not as "verified".

### Gaps
- EMI (Le, Afshari, Su, PLDI 2014; [paper PDF](https://www.cs.ucdavis.edu/~su/publications/emi.pdf)) and YARPGen: not fetched; bug counts not verified here. QuickChick / Lean `Plausible` property testing of semantics: not searched. Rust crater: not searched. Mark as unverified if cited in the report.
- No source found that reports "cost per bug" or "cost per theorem" for differential-testing infrastructure specifically.

## KQ3. Proof-engineering at scale: cost, maintenance, repair, assurance reporting

### Takeaway
The only hard cost numbers in the public record are seL4's (≈12 person-years for the kernel functional-correctness proof, 200k lines of Isabelle for 8.7k lines of C, under $350/line; a redo estimated at 8–10 person-years), and they come from a team that built the frameworks first. The maintenance regime that works is: proofs in the same repo and CI as the code, every change rebuilds everything, `#print axioms`/`lake check`-style kernel rechecks, and an explicit enumerated TCB with a "trusted vs verified" list that is itself a document under version control. ML proof repair is a 2025–2026 research benchmark (127 problems), not a maintenance tool yet.

### Cited Findings
- seL4 numbers page: 8,700 lines of C plus 600 lines of ARM assembly; 7,500 lines verified; 600 lines assembly and 1,200 lines boot code unverified; 200,000 lines of Isabelle proof script ("about 3,500 A4 pages"); all seL4 proof files in 2015 ≈ 500,000 lines; "Design, documentation, implementation, and verification combined: about 25 person-years"; a redo "under 10 person-years"; industry EAL6 rule of thumb $1,000/line → ≈$8.7M "which the page says would give less assurance" — [trustworthy.systems l4.verified numbers](https://trustworthy.systems/projects/OLD/l4.verified/numbers)
- Klein, "From a Verified Kernel Towards Verified Systems": kernel verification itself "about 12 person-years" of the 25; the rest went to "frameworks, tools, proof libraries, the C verification framework, the memory model, and the C-to-Isabelle parser"; "$700 per line of code for the full 25 person-years, and under $350 per line for the 12 person-years on the kernel"; projected 8–10 person-years (≈$230/line) for a similar codebase; TCB assumptions: C compiler and linker, assembly code, hardware, correct TLB/cache-flush use, boot code; "The verified target was the ARM11 uniprocessor version; the x86 port is unverified" — [NICTA 4180 PDF](https://www.trustworthy.systems/publications/nicta_full_text/4180.pdf)
- CompCert TCB paper enumerates 15 trust items (Coq kernel incl. `coqchk -o` and `vm_compute`; extraction + OCaml with `Obj.magic`; axioms linking to OCaml with inhabitation lemmas; impure oracles checked by validators; pointer equality/hash-consing; unverified front end; optional unverified preprocessing passes; trusted C/Asm semantics; unverified assembly printing/expansion; clobbered-register specs with three fixed bugs; scheduling tables (performance-only); assembler/linker/toolchain; ABI/value modeling "never formally verified"; runtime (`malloc` always succeeds, IEEE RNE assumed); hardware) and for each states *why* it is trusted (review, testing, validation, or "assumed") — [arXiv 2201.10280](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.10280)
- Lean FRO Year 4 Part 1 roadmap (Sept 2026–Feb 2027, six-month cadence): "`lake check` exports a development and rechecks it with the official kernel"; "a mode where `lake build` skips the official kernel checks and only produces oleans"; "We will include comparator in the Lean distribution"; kernel hardening and "new external kernels" planned; `bv_decide` and `grind` usable together; "When the automation cannot close a goal, it should fail fast and tell the user why"; typeclass-instance caching "already shows significant gains on all of Mathlib"; new code generator design now, implementation "at the beginning of 2027" (stack-allocated objects, recursive join points, uniqueness annotations); Verso "produces PDFs and interactive web pages from a single literate source"; lean-beam (AI-agent interface) to be stabilized and shipped with Lean; Nerodia Lean/Python FFI — [lean-lang.org/fro/roadmap/y4-1](https://lean-lang.org/fro/roadmap/y4-1)
- ProofRepairBench / "RepairBench" (Patel, Piotrowski, Haller, Leather; ICLR 2026 VerifAI-2 workshop): 127 Lean repair problems (incorrect proof + error message → correct proof); motivated by one-shot LLM proofs rarely being error-free; no success rates on the listing page — [ICLR 2026 listing](https://www.iclr.cc/virtual/2026/10012643)
- QED at Large (Ringer et al., 2019/2020 survey) exists as the standard reference on proof engineering and maintenance; not fetched this session — [arXiv 2003.06458](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.06458)
- RCE baseline: Lean v4.12.0, no Mathlib, `#print axioms` lists only propext/Quot.sound/Classical.choice; the paper's audit numbers disagree between commits (2,192 vs 4,877 vs 4,875) and the paper itself says the count says nothing about theorems "about the Rust evaluator" — paper §3.7 and `10-theorems.md` (grounding).

### Inferences
- seL4's ratio (≈23–27 proof lines per verified C line, ≈16.7k proof lines per person-year as a back-of-envelope) is for *refinement to C*, the hardest kind. RCE's current Lean (23k lines) is almost entirely about models and a core calculus; a refinement from `RCE.Core.eval` to `interp.rs` (S7(c)) would be a seL4-class effort per construct and is out of reach for a solo builder. The feasible target is the CakeML/SpecTec direction: *derive* the executable from the proved definition (extract/compile Lean `eval` → run it as the reference, or generate the Rust evaluator's core from the Lean definition), not prove an independently written Rust interpreter equal to it.
- RCE's theorem-count metric is the wrong metric and the paper already half-admits it. CompCert's TCB document is the right artifact: a chapter listing every trusted item (rustc implements MiniRust; `core_elab` faithful; `anf.rs` ⊇ `Anf.lean`; `float_text`/`cmp_canon` match chapter 7; the SHA-256 implementation; the LALR table builder; the `decide`-vector generator; Lean 4.12.0 kernel; the `lake env lean --run` adapter path on macOS where compiled binaries do not load) with, per item, *why* it is trusted and *which test* backs it.
- Lean FRO's `lake check` (export + official-kernel recheck) and the planned comparator are the mechanism RCE should pin its "no sorry, three axioms" claim to in CI, rather than `#print axioms` run by hand. The audit (`spec/lean/audit/Audit.lean`, `scripts/lean-audit.sh`) should emit a machine-readable JSON that `claim-counts.py --check` compares against the paper's figures, so the 2,192/4,877/4,875 disagreement cannot recur.
- Proof repair by ML is a benchmark, not a practice, as of 2026; plan for proof breakage with the ordinary tools (`sorry`-free gate, blocked merges, small lemmas, generated vectors re-blessed with `RCE_BLESS=1`).

### Gaps
- No public per-feature maintenance cost figures for seL4 (the numbers page says only that numbers "have grown over time"). Staples et al. ICSE 2015 "leading indicator for cost of formal verification" and the "Productivity for proof engineering" slides were in results but not fetched.
- Mathlib CI specifics (bors/merge queue, cache, nightly-testing bump bot, deprecation policy): not fetched this session. DeepSpec assurance-level language: not searched.
- No source gives "cost per theorem" for a Lean language-semantics project; RCE's own git history is probably the best available data (date-stamped lines like "Executable event runs, 2026-10-15" in `soundness.md` suggest this could be mined).

## KQ4. Keeping docs and code in sync: executable specs, generated grammars, hash-pinned chapters, traceability

### Takeaway
The working patterns are (a) single-source generation (SpecTec → prose/LaTeX/Coq/interpreter/tests; Verso → PDF + web from one literate source; Rust's planned literate executable spec), (b) build-time counts and hashes that fail the build on drift (RCE already does this for grammar, chapter hashes and quoted figures), and (c) traceability matrices from requirement to test as used in safety qualification (Ferrocene: "Potential Errors and Tests Traceability", ISO 26262 ASIL D / IEC 61508 T3 / IEC 62304). RCE has (b) and part of (a) and none of (c).

### Cited Findings
- SpecTec outputs and build integration: see KQ1; "nothing will change about the overall procedure for building the spec" while SpecTec is invoked in the build; proposal authors "will extend SpecTec files rather than write much prose or LaTeX" — [webassembly.org 2025-03-27](https://webassembly.org/news/2025-03-27-spectec/)
- Rust executable spec: literate Rust program that is also an interpreter; authoritative vs non-authoritative sections with runtime warnings; grammar reused from the Reference — [goals.rust-lang.org/2026/executable-spec](https://goals.rust-lang.org/2026/executable-spec.html)
- Ferrocene Evaluation Report (public docs, "unsigned draft from the development branch"): sections "Potential Errors by Classes Traceability Matrix" (4.3) and "Potential Errors and Tests Traceability" (6); test suites: Compiletest, Library, Compiler, Linkchecker, Build System, Tidy, Self-Test; qualification material targets ISO 26262:2018 ASIL D / TCL 3, IEC 61508:2010 class T3, IEC 62304:2006+AMD1; core-library certification at ASIL B / SIL 2; method documents: HazOp Guide Words, HazOp Selected Nodes (rustc), Use Cases (rustc), Tool Analysis (rustc), Qualification Method (rustc) — [public-docs.ferrocene.dev evaluation report](https://public-docs.ferrocene.dev/main/qualification/evaluation-report/index.html)
- Verso: "produces PDFs and interactive web pages from a single literate source"; FRO is hardening Verso and Blueprints — [lean-lang.org/fro/roadmap/y4-1](https://lean-lang.org/fro/roadmap/y4-1)
- KEVM as an executable formal spec validated by the official test suite (CSF 2018) — [FSL page](https://fsl.cs.illinois.edu/publications/hildenbrandt-saxena-zhu-rodrigues-daian-guth-moore-zhang-park-rosu-2018-csf.html)
- RCE baseline (grounding, local clone): chapter footers `<!-- body-sha256 -->`, `INDEX.md` table of hashes and read-receipt questions, `rce-book verify receipt.json` with salted answers in `receipts.json`; "A correct receipt is evidence of reading, not of understanding"; `claim-counts.py --check` fails on a quoted figure that disagrees with the source; `.github/workflows/tree-sitter-sync.yml` and `xarch-parity.yml` exist; `# rce-version:` is "tolerated, ignored and not hashed"; release rule C.15 "a release label names exactly one spec commit" — `spec/book/READ-PROTOCOL.md`, `INDEX.md`, `00-overview.md`.

### Inferences
- Ferrocene's traceability is hazard → potential error → test, not spec-paragraph → test. For RCE the natural unit is W-rule → (checker test, Lean lemma, vectors, example/reject file). `09-wellformedness.md` is already a numbered rule list; the missing artifact is a generated matrix `W-rule × {checker test id, Lean constant, vector file, reject example}` that `rce-spec-check` emits and `claim-counts.py` compares to the chapter, failing when a W-rule has no row in any column. `core_rules.rs` already does this for 5.3 typing rules in one column (Lean name); extend it to all columns and all rule families (W-*, N1–N9 numeric notes, E-* evaluation rules, C.*, F.*).
- RCE's receipts protocol solves a problem no other project has (agent reading attestation) but it is orthogonal to sync; its useful by-product is that every chapter already has a stable body hash, which can key the traceability matrix and the vector files (a vector file should carry the body hash of the chapter paragraph it witnesses, so a chapter edit that changes 7.x's edge-case table invalidates the vectors generated from it until re-blessed).
- SpecTec's experience says the generation boundary will stop at numeric primitives and text format. RCE should decide explicitly that chapter 7's primitive tables (N1–N9, float formatting, `cmp_canon`) are *vector-governed*: the chapter's table rows are the source, a script generates both `F64UnVectors.lean`/`F32Vectors.lean` (checked `by decide`) and the Rust `float_rules.rs` test rows, and the chapter hash pins both. That is the cheapest form of single-sourcing and already matches the file layout in `spec/lean/RCE/Core/`.

### Gaps
- TLA+ at AWS (Newcombe et al., CACM 2015), P language, Alloy: not fetched this session; well known but no URL verified here. DO-178C traceability objectives: not searched. tree-sitter-from-EBNF generators: not searched (RCE already has a hand-ported tree-sitter grammar with an agreement test, which is the stronger arrangement).
- The Ferrocene traceability matrix contents (how a test is tied to an FLS paragraph id) were not retrievable from the index page; the FLS itself uses stable paragraph identifiers (`fls_xxxx`) per training knowledge, unverified here.

## KQ5. Solo/small-team feasibility: smallest credible TCB and proof surface; proof vs test boundary

### Takeaway
A credible "proved" claim from one person is achievable only when the proved object *is* the shipped object (extracted interpreter, generated rule tables, kernel-checked vectors) and the trusted remainder is enumerated with a backing test per item. The public evidence (CakeML's translator, CompCert's 15-item TCB, Verify-Rust-Std's 4% contract coverage after a year with a community, seL4's 12 person-years) says re-proving an independently written implementation is not a solo task; deriving the implementation from the proof is.

### Cited Findings
- CakeML: translator-based synthesis is "recommended for most new verified code" over post-hoc proof — [cakeml.org](https://cakeml.org/)
- CompCert: oracles untrusted because outputs are validated; small, auditable OCaml for smart constructors "can be easily tested and audited"; fuel limits are trust points — [arXiv 2201.10280](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.10280)
- seL4: 12 person-years for the kernel proof after frameworks existed; redo 8–10 — [NICTA 4180](https://www.trustworthy.systems/publications/nicta_full_text/4180.pdf); [numbers page](https://trustworthy.systems/projects/OLD/l4.verified/numbers)
- Verify-Rust-Std: multi-tool, community effort yields 9 of 27 challenges and ~4% contract coverage in `core`; recommends hybrid bounded + unbounded tools and Miri — [arXiv 2510.01072v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.01072v2)
- Rust executable spec: one FTE for a year requested, unfunded, "multi-year effort" — [goals.rust-lang.org/2026/executable-spec](https://goals.rust-lang.org/2026/executable-spec.html)
- Lean FRO: `lake check` official-kernel recheck; `grind`/`bv_decide` fail-fast; lean-beam agent interface — [y4-1 roadmap](https://lean-lang.org/fro/roadmap/y4-1)
- RCE baseline: the paper distinguishes P / P* / M / M+ / MV / T / C and states the T16–T31 and T48/T51/T53 caveats explicitly; the Kani certificate gates promotion; the S6 adapter runs Lean's `eval` as the executor with the reference front end for parse/check/admission ("Entry ... is the reference front end's ... the run is Lean's") — paper §3.7, §5.3; `soundness.md` S6 (grounding).

### Inferences
- The smallest credible proof surface for RCE is: (1) `RCE.Core` definitions (syntax, `HasTy`, `eval`/`evalC`, `delta`, `Log`) with `eval_sound`, `eval_det`, `eval_mono_le`, `delta_atomic`, `replay_fold` — already proved; (2) the *elaboration* `core_elab` as the only trusted bridge, made small and tested by round-trip (pretty-print core → re-parse → re-elaborate = identity) and by the conformance harness; (3) chapter 7 primitive tables as generated `by decide` vectors on both sides. Everything else (concurrency models, host models, Raft, sessions) is "M" and should be labelled as design evidence, not language guarantees.
- Where to draw the proof/test line, in RCE terms: *prove* what is stated for every program (T1–T5, T8, T3) on the core and make the core the executor for the subset; *test* the implementations (T9, T10 sampling, emitters, host); *refuse to claim* anything for constructs that are `unsupported` in the Lean adapter. The current pass counts already embody this; the missing step is to make "a construct accepted by `rce-check` but `unsupported` by `rce-conform-lean`" a reported, chapter-tagged number that gates the theorem-status table (a theorem labelled P cannot cover a construct the Lean core does not have).
- The honest status label for RCE's headline theorems today, borrowing CompCert's vocabulary, is "proved for the core; the reference evaluator agrees on N vectors and K conformance cases; the elaboration is trusted". That sentence should be generated, not written.

### Gaps
- No published "cost per theorem" figure for a Lean language mechanization; no public solo-maintainer mechanized-language case study found. RCE itself (one builder, 23k Lean lines, dated 2026-10 entries) is the closest data point.
- Lean `decide` scalability limits for large vector files (kernel reduction cost) were not researched; relevant because RCE's vector strategy depends on `by decide` staying fast as vector files grow (FRO's `grind`/`bv_decide` or `native_decide`/`Decidable` via `rfl` are the alternatives; `native_decide` enlarges the TCB to the compiler).

## KQ6. Lean 4 specifics 2025–2026 relevant to RCE

### Takeaway
The FRO's 2026–27 direction (official-kernel `lake check`, comparator shipped with Lean, `grind`+`bv_decide`, a new code generator starting 2027, Verso single-source docs, lean-beam for agents) is aligned with RCE's needs, but RCE is pinned to v4.12.0 and documents a macOS dyld failure for compiled binaries; the upgrade path is itself a sync obligation.

### Cited Findings
- FRO Y4-1 roadmap items (see KQ3): `lake check` official-kernel recheck; `lake build` olean-only mode; comparator in the distribution; new external kernels; `grind` + `bv_decide` together with fail-fast diagnostics; new do-notation "with first-class support for intrinsic verification"; VC-generator improvements; new code generator design in this period, implementation from early 2027; Verso hardening; lean-beam stabilized; roadmaps now six-monthly — [lean-lang.org/fro/roadmap/y4-1](https://lean-lang.org/fro/roadmap/y4-1)
- Lean WebAssembly work: a Reservoir package `@T-Brick/wasm` exists ([reservoir.lean-lang.org/@T-Brick/wasm](https://reservoir.lean-lang.org/@T-Brick/wasm)); a Chalmers seminar "A verified WebAssembly interpreter in Lean" was held 12 June 2024 ([Chalmers event](https://www.cse.chalmers.se/research/group/security/event/2024/2024-06-12-alex)); neither was fetched for content.
- RCE baseline: `lean-toolchain` = `leanprover/lean4:v4.12.0`, no Mathlib; "Compiled Lean 4.12 binaries do not load on current macOS (dyld rejects the `__DATA_CONST` segment), so the adapter runs `lake env lean --run CoreMain.lean`" — `spec/lean/lean-toolchain`, `soundness.md` S6 (grounding).

### Inferences
- Running the Lean executor via `lake env lean --run` instead of a compiled binary means the S6 oracle depends on the interpreter path of Lean 4.12, which is slower and not the path `lake check` validates; the toolchain pin should be upgraded to a release where compiled binaries load, and the adapter should ship the compiled executor whose hash is recorded in the conformance log the way `proofs/kani.cert` is.
- Aeneas (Rust → Lean translation) was not researched this session; it is the candidate for proving properties of `interp.rs` fragments directly in Lean rather than re-modelling them, which is the S7 "refinement" alternative with the smallest new-code cost. Flag as an open evaluation.

### Gaps
- Aeneas status 2025–2026, Lean's own compiler verification, `lake` lockfile/Reservoir reproducibility guarantees, and the content of the Lean-Wasm projects: not fetched. Lean 4 release cadence and v4.12 → current migration cost: not researched.

## KQ7. Synthesis: what would make "spec = checker = proofs" a build-time invariant in RCE (adopt / refuse / open)

### Takeaway
Treat it as four equalities, each enforced by a generator plus a diff gate, with a generated status table as the only place the words "proved/tested/trusted" are written: spec→grammar (done), spec→rules (half done: names only), rules→checker and rules→Lean (generate both from one table), semantics→executor (run the Lean core as the oracle; derive, don't re-prove), and everything else listed in a CompCert-style TCB chapter with a backing test per line.

### Cited Findings
- Single-source generation and its residue (SpecTec, 2025-03-27) — [webassembly.org](https://webassembly.org/news/2025-03-27-spectec/)
- Derive executable from logic (CakeML translator) — [cakeml.org](https://cakeml.org/)
- Enumerated TCB with per-item justification (CompCert, 2022) — [arXiv 2201.10280](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.10280)
- Differential testing locates the proof boundary (Csmith/CompCert, 2011) — [PLDI 2011 preprint](https://www-old.cs.utah.edu/~regehr/papers/pldi11-preprint.pdf)
- Bounded model checking yields spec bugs, rarely code bugs, at low coverage (Verify-Rust-Std, Oct 2025) — [arXiv 2510.01072v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.01072v2)
- Official-kernel recheck and comparator tooling (Lean FRO, 2026–27) — [y4-1 roadmap](https://lean-lang.org/fro/roadmap/y4-1)
- Hazard-to-test traceability as qualification evidence (Ferrocene) — [evaluation report](https://public-docs.ferrocene.dev/main/qualification/evaluation-report/index.html)
- Executable, literate spec with authoritative/non-authoritative sections (Rust 2026 goal) — [goals.rust-lang.org](https://goals.rust-lang.org/2026/executable-spec.html)

### Inferences (recommendations in RCE terms)

Adopt:
1. **Promote the rule tables to the authored source.** `checker/src/core_rules.rs` and `conc::LVAR_RULES` become `spec/rules/*.toml` (or a Lean file, SpecTec-style) from which `rce-book build` renders the rule paragraphs of chapters 5, 7, 9 and C/F, `core_rules.rs` is generated for the checker, and `RCE/Core/Rules.lean` is generated with *definitions and stated lemma signatures*, not `#check`s. `rce-spec-check` fails if any of the three outputs differ from the committed files (same mechanism as `RCE_BLESS=1` today). This turns the current one-way "5.3 names a rule the table lacks" check into a three-way equality.
2. **Generate a traceability matrix** `W-rule / N-note / E-rule / C.x / F.x × {checker test, Lean constant, vector file, example, reject}` from the rule source; `claim-counts.py --check` fails on an empty cell for any rule the chapter marks normative. Chapter 9's reader-visible rule list becomes a rendering of this matrix. This is Ferrocene's "potential errors and tests traceability" at the granularity RCE already has.
3. **Make the Lean core the executor of record for its subset.** Keep `rce-conform-lean`, but (a) ship a compiled executor whose hash is logged like `proofs/kani.cert`, (b) report `unsupported` counts per chapter construct in the status table, and (c) add a grammar-driven *semantic* fuzzer (reference vs Lean vs emitters), which is where Csmith found CompCert's front-end bugs. The status label for T1–T5/T8 should be generated from these counts.
4. **Vector-govern chapter 7's primitive tables.** Source = the table rows in `07-form-evaluation.md`; outputs = `F64UnVectors.lean`/`F32Vectors.lean`/`Vectors.lean` (`by decide`) and `tests/float_rules.rs`; the vector files carry the chapter body hash they were generated from; a chapter edit without re-blessing fails the build. This is the SpecTec residue (numerics, text format) handled the way SpecTec could not.
5. **Write the TCB chapter.** A `18-trusted.md` listing every trusted item (rustc ⊨ MiniRust; `core_elab`; `anf.rs` ⊇ `Anf.lean`; SHA-256 impl; LALR table builder; vector generators; Lean 4.12 kernel and the `lake env lean --run` path; Kani 0.67 unwind bounds; `float_text`/`cmp_canon`), each with the test id that backs it and the theorem whose status it caps. CompCert's document is the template; Ferrocene's "known problems" list is the operational analogue.
6. **Pin "no sorry, three axioms" to a machine check in CI** (`lake check` once on a toolchain that has it; until then `audit/Audit.lean` emitting JSON consumed by `claim-counts.py`), and make the paper's audit numbers generated, so the 2,192/4,877/4,875 inconsistency cannot recur.
7. **Status levels:** keep P / P* / M / M+ / MV / T / C but add two generated columns: "executor" (which program runs the construct in the conformance harness: Lean core / Rust reference / emitted target) and "unsupported count", and forbid a P label on any theorem whose quantified constructs include one the Lean core does not elaborate. Models (T16–T31, T37–T41, T48/T51/T53) should be rendered in a separate table headed "design models", as the paper's own caveats already say.

Refuse:
- A seL4/CompCert-style refinement proof from `RCE.Core` to `interp.rs` (S7(c) as stated): ≈12 person-years at seL4 ratios for the hard part, and the public record has no solo precedent. Replace with derivation (Lean core as executor; later, generated Rust from the rule source) plus differential testing.
- Counting theorem constants as an assurance metric (the paper's 2,192/4,877 figures): CompCert and seL4 report TCB items and person-years, not theorem counts; the number is also unstable across RCE commits.
- ML proof repair as a maintenance strategy before 2027: it is a 127-problem benchmark (ICLR 2026 workshop), not tooling.
- `native_decide` or compiled-oracle shortcuts to make vectors faster: they move the compiler into the TCB; use `decide` with smaller vector files, or `bv_decide`/`grind` where FRO's fail-fast diagnostics apply.
- Two normative texts of any kind (the overview already refuses this for `SURFACE.md`/`FORM.md`; extend the same rule to the Lean files: Lean definitions are *generated* or *subordinate*, never a second authored semantics).

### Gaps (open problems)
- No published measurement of how much a rule-table-generated prose/Lean/checker pipeline costs to build or maintain at RCE's scale; SpecTec took a multi-institution team 2022–2025 to reach adoption and still has manual residue.
- Whether `by decide` vectors scale to thousands of cases per file in Lean 4.12 without kernel timeouts; unresearched.
- Aeneas (Rust → Lean) as a cheaper route to proving `interp.rs` fragments: status unverified this session.
- How to state T9 for emitters without a verified printer: CompCert's answer (validate against the platform; SAIL-style tested ISA semantics) implies RCE needs a tested "MiniRust ⊆ rustc" vector suite, which does not exist.
- The read-receipt protocol's salts are public and answers are short; it attests reading, not sync, and should not be counted as an assurance mechanism in the status table.
