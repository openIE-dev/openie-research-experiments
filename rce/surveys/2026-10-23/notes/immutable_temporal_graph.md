RCEdb design under review (from the spec): D is a SHA-256 hash-chained append-only event log; every index is a pure fold of a prefix of D (HS-7); queries are RCE terms in canonical key order with canonical result order and a set/bag type (HS-8); state as of seq k is the fold of D up to k (HS-9); the proposed G-1..G-6 refinement makes the primary index a typed property graph projected from the log, every node and edge carrying the creating and retracting event id, queries as typed values in the MGQL read-only GQL fragment, bag default, termination by bounded quantifiers / TRAIL-style restrictors / stratified fixpoints, tables and documents as special cases of the graph.

## KQ1. Immutable and versioned databases: Datomic, XTDB, Dolt, TerminusDB, Fluree, immudb, QLDB, Noms, Irmin

### Takeaway
Every surviving system in this family stores an append-only record of facts or chunks plus derived, rebuildable indexes; the two things they all learned the hard way are (a) history growth needs an explicit retention/erasure story from day one (Datomic excision, XTDB evict/erase, Dolt GC all arrived as bolt-ons and are described by their own makers as blunt), and (b) the single-writer log (Datomic's transactor, QLDB's journal) buys serializability and simple as-of semantics at the cost of write scalability. QLDB, the closest commercial analogue of "the log is the database", was shut down on 31 July 2025 with no public rationale.

### Cited Findings

**Datomic**
- A datom is a five-part tuple: entity, attribute, value, transaction, and assert/retract flag; every datom retains a reference to the transaction that asserted or retracted it — [Jepsen, Datomic Pro 1.0.7075 analysis, May 2024](https://jepsen.io/analyses/datomic-pro-1.0.7075)
- Peers read storage directly but can only request writes through the transactor, "a single designated process in charge of writes" — [Jepsen 2024](https://jepsen.io/analyses/datomic-pro-1.0.7075); writing to the transactional log is serialized on a single thread; community workaround for outgrowing one transactor is splitting into multiple databases each with its own transactor — [Datomic forum, "The single transactor and write performance"](https://forum.datomic.com/t/the-single-transactor-and-write-performance/275)
- As-of filters a database to datoms as of a point in time and existing queries run unchanged against it; `since` keeps only datoms after a point; `history` returns all present and past datoms — [Datomic docs, supported ops](https://docs.datomic.com/whatis/supported-ops.html)
- Datomic's indexes are covering indexes (they contain the data rather than point to it) — [Datomic glossary](https://docs.datomic.com/glossary.html); indexes are stored as shallow trees of immutable segments, updated by background indexing jobs and merged with an in-memory representation of recent changes — [Datomic docs, indexes](https://docs.datomic.com/query/indexes.html); third-party sources give trees at most three levels deep with segments as leaves and roughly 50 KB / 1,000–20,000 datoms per segment (third-party, unofficial) — [dbdb.io](https://dbdb.io/db/datomic/revisions/11), [Tonsky, Unofficial guide to Datomic internals](https://tonsky.me/blog/unofficial-guide-to-datomic-internals/)
- Four sorted indexes: EAVT (row-like access), AEVT (column-like), AVET (attribute+value lookup, off by default because costlier to maintain, enabled per attribute with `:db/index true` or `:db/unique`), VAET (reverse index for reference attributes only) — [Datomic docs, indexes](https://docs.datomic.com/query/indexes.html)
- Hickey's "Deconstructing the Database" (QCon SF 2012) describes a database value as a log of transaction assertions and retractions plus covering indexes and data segments, with the live index lazily loaded because the source is immutable — [QCon SF 2012 slides](https://qconsf.com/sf2012/dl/qcon-sanfran-2012/slides/RichHickey_DeconstructingTheDatabase.pdf)
- Jepsen (funded by Nubank, May 2024): Datomic Pro "appeared to offer Strong Session Serializable isolation, and Strong Serializable for histories restricted to update transactions"; but Datomic "defines unusual intra-transaction semantics in which operations are applied logically concurrent with one another, rather than sequentially", which "might violate three of the most widely accepted formalizations of Serializability" depending on interpretation, and invariants preserved by individual transaction functions can be broken when those functions are combined in one transaction — [Jepsen blog, 2024-05-15](https://jepsen.io/blog/2024-05-15-datomic-pro-1.0.7075); Datomic's response: "We found no behavior which violated Datomic's core safety claims" — [Datomic blog, May 2024](https://blog.datomic.com/2024/05/Jepsen-tests-Datomic.html)
- Excision = "complete removal of a set of datoms matching a predicate", intended for privacy removal and domain retention periods; "irrevocable", backup strongly encouraged — [Datomic docs, excision](https://docs.datomic.com/on-prem/excision.html). A Datomic forum moderator: excision "is NOT intended, nor is it suitable for, 'clean up' of old/temporary data or reduction of storage use"; alternative for high-churn attributes is `:db/noHistory true`, whose purpose "is to conserve storage, not to make semantic guarantees about removing information" — [Datomic forum, "Data non-retention and redaction"](https://forum.datomic.com/t/data-non-retention-and-redaction/665)
- Datomic Cloud (2021 exchange): "we do not currently have a feature to support the complete removal of data in Datomic Cloud"; suggested workarounds "encryption + throw away the key" or storing personal data outside Datomic — [Datomic forum, "Support for excision or similar"](https://forum.datomic.com/t/support-for-excision-or-similar/323); a user argued that because retention limits require erasing at the ingest rate, excision "is not a practical solution" — same thread
- A forum thread titled "Does excision count towards the 1 billion datom limit?" indicates a documented ~1-billion-datom sizing guideline for a single Datomic database (title only; threshold not verified from docs in this session) — [Datomic forum](https://forum.datomic.com/t/does-excision-count-towards-the-1-billion-datom-limit/1132)

**XTDB (v1 "Crux" and v2)**
- Two axes: system time (when the database ingested a change) and valid time (when the fact holds); all data is bitemporal "without having to think about storing or updating additional columns"; system-time columns "cannot be modified"; valid time editable only on new versions, defaulting to the transaction's system time; design "inspired by SQL:2011" with `FOR SYSTEM_TIME`, `FOR ALL SYSTEM_TIME`, `FOR PORTION OF VALID_TIME` — [XTDB docs, data model](https://docs.xtdb.com/intro/data-model), [XTDB blog, launching v2](https://xtdb.com/blog/launching-xtdb-v2)
- v1 design (Crux, 2019): transactions go to an immutable log; "the main transaction log contains only hashes and is immutable, and all document content is stored in a dedicated document log (or Key-Value store) that can be evicted"; `evict` removes all versions of a document in a valid-time window while the transaction history remains; "intended to assist with technical compliance for information privacy regulations" — [JUXT, Introducing Crux](https://juxt.pro/blog/introducing-crux), [XTDB tutorial, Evict](https://nextjournal.com/xtdb-tutorial/evict)
- v2 rationale (maintainers): v1 "mostly targeted towards transactional queries"; v2 improves OLAP, separates storage from compute so history can sit in cheaper object storage (S3), is built on Apache Arrow columnar files, adds cross-time queries (temporal joins, temporal range scans); SQL was a driver ("SQL is a massive audience"); "XTDB 2.x is firmly grounded in the relational model"; Datalog was replaced by XTQL because "v2 has tables"; v2 does not support recursive CTEs (native fixpoint) — [XTDB v2 hub](https://www.xtdb.com/v2), [XTDB key concepts](https://docs.xtdb.com/concepts/key-concepts), [XTDB discuss, 1.0 vs 2.0](https://discuss.xtdb.com/t/best-version-for-use-case-1-0-vs-2-0/571)
- Compaction: "standard LSM compaction removes superseded versions, but XT keeps them unless you specifically need us to irrevocably 'ERASE' it, for legal reasons" — [XTDB dev diary 2023/2024](https://xtdb.com/blog/dev-diary-feb-24); community (2024): "ERASE is quite a blunt instrument", "you can't use v2 currently and expect to reclaim space or keep storage limited"; staff planned retention lifecycle policies later — [Clojurians log, xtdb 2024-11-21](https://clojurians-log.clojureverse.org/xtdb/2024-11-21); Dec 2025 thread: retention policies configurable since xtdb PR #2591 — [Clojurians log, xtdb 2025-12-05](https://clojurians-log.clojureverse.org/xtdb/2025-12-05)
- A third-party package maps `erase()` to `ERASE FROM ... WHERE ...` in v2, "removes the row and its whole history", listed for GDPR — [laravel-xtdb2 on Packagist](https://packagist.org/packages/vuthaihoc/laravel-xtdb2) (third-party; the official SQL reference was not read)
- v2.1.0 release reported Dec 2025 (third-party listing, unverified) — [dev.co XTDB page](https://dev.co/databases/open-source/xtdb)

**Dolt / DoltHub / Noms**
- Dolt is a fork of Noms; the attic-labs/noms repository is archived: "Noms is not being maintained ... recommends Dolt" — [attic-labs/noms README](https://github.com/attic-labs/noms). Noms (2016, Attic Labs, $8.1M raise) took its cues from Git, Camlistore, ipfs, bup; content-addressing deduplicates identical content; "all previous versions of the database are retained by default"; file-system and S3 chunk stores — [TechCrunch, Aug 2016](https://techcrunch.com/2016/08/02/attic-labs-noms-noms/), [noms README](https://github.com/attic-labs/noms)
- Dolt performance vs MySQL (vendor-reported, sysbench): about 15x slower in 2020; Dec 2024 summary roughly 25% slower on reads and 13% faster on writes — [DoltHub, "10% Slower Than MySQL", 2024-12-23](https://dolthub.com/blog/2024-12-23-2024-perf-summary); docs latency page: ~10% faster writes, ~5% slower reads, ~40% of MySQL TPC-C throughput — [Dolt docs, benchmarks/latency](https://dolthub.com/docs/sql-reference/benchmarks/latency); April 2026: ~5% faster than MySQL on sysbench — [DoltHub, 2026-04-09](https://www.dolthub.com/blog/2026-04-09-improving-doltlite/); June 2026 post isolates Prolly-tree cost by comparing DoltLite with stock SQLite on the same SQL engine (numbers not surfaced) — [DoltHub, 2026-06-08](https://dolthub.com/blog/2026-06-08-how-fast-is-doltlite/)
- Independent measurement: BranchBench (arXiv, April 2026) found Dolt's branch operations very fast but "Dolt's read latency is 2–4000× higher than Neon's across workflows" — [BranchBench, arXiv 2604.17180](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.17180)
- Storage: Prolly-tree chunk boundaries chosen probabilistically targeting ~4 KB compressed blocks; chunks stored by hash (SHA-512 truncated to 20 bytes in Dolt; BLAKE3 in DoltLite); storage "will scale with the size of the changes, as opposed to the size of the database times the number of versions"; most writes rewrite multiple chunks and "can result in writing a large portion of the tree" — [Dolt docs, storage engine](https://docs.dolthub.com/architecture/storage-engine), [DoltHub, commit graph and structural sharing, 2020](https://dolthub.com/blog/2020-05-13-dolt-commit-graph-and-structural-sharing/), [DoltHub, Dolt/DoltLite storage comparison, 2026-07-08](https://dolthub.com/blog/2026-07-08-dolt-doltlite-storage-comp)
- Sizing rule of thumb (vendor, "very rough"): 4 KB × insert/update transactions × average indexes updated per transaction × tree depth = uncollected storage; multiply by fraction of transactions committed for permanent history; "for long histories, Dolt will surpass traditional database disk usage" — [DoltHub, Sizing Your Dolt Instance, 2023-12-06](https://www.dolthub.com/blog/2023-12-06-sizing-your-dolt-instance)
- GC walks reachable chunks from branch tips; auto-GC since Dolt 1.75; GC reclaims only unreferenced chunks, "not about removing history" — [Dolt docs, garbage collection](https://docs.dolthub.com/sql-reference/server/garbage-collection), [DoltHub, disk space, 2026-06-18](https://dolthub.com/blog/2026-06-18-dolt-disk-space); operator runbook (third-party): auto-GC fires at ~125 MB of new chunks; a 120 GB pre-GC store typically drops to 5–20 GB — [Gas City, Dolt bloat recovery](https://docs.gascity.com/troubleshooting/dolt-bloat-recovery); zstd dictionary-compressed archives shrink footprint 30–50% over Snappy — [DoltHub, disk space, 2026](https://dolthub.com/blog/2026-06-18-dolt-disk-space)
- No Jepsen report on Dolt was found (jepsen.io services page and searches returned none) — [Jepsen services](https://jepsen.io/services)

**TerminusDB, Fluree**
- TerminusDB: git-like immutable versioned model with branch, merge, time travel; delta encoding makes branch/merge/push/pull/clone/time-travel possible; operations persisted in a forward-only log — [Solid forum post on TerminusDB](https://forum.solidproject.org/t/terminusdb-a-delightful-database-for-linked-data/3140), [SocialHub thread](https://socialhub.activitypub.rocks/t/terminusdb-exciting-stuff-for-fediverse-projects/736)

**immudb**
- Uses "a combination of Merkle Trees and linear chains"; a persistent Merkle tree whose leaves are transaction hashes gives logarithmic proofs for history; a transient per-transaction tree over key-value-metadata entries whose root is EH (Entries Hash); a linear chain (Alh) links each transaction hash to the previous; KV, document and SQL models with a PostgreSQL wire protocol — [immudb docs (0.8.x how-it-works and later developer docs)](https://docs.immudb.io/0.8.0/how-it-works.html), [immudb docs](https://docs.immudb.io/1.0.0/quickstart.html); immudb's vendor positions immudb Vault as a QLDB replacement — [Codenotary blog](https://codenotary.com/blog/navigating-the-transition-from-amazon-qldb-to-immudb-vault)

### Inferences (judgements on RCEdb)
- Adopt (already in HS-7/HS-9): "index is a rebuildable projection of an immutable log" is the common factor of Datomic (covering indexes as immutable segments), XTDB v1 (log of hashes + evictable doc store), Dolt/Noms (content-addressed chunks) and QLDB (journal-first). RCEdb's stronger claim — the index is *defined* as a fold written in the same language — is unusual; none of the surveyed systems expose the index definition as user-visible pure code.
- Adopt with care (G-2, retracting event id): Datomic's five-tuple shows that carrying the asserting transaction per fact costs one id per datom and makes as-of/since/history trivial. RCEdb's node/edge carrying both creating and retracting event ids is a strict superset. Judgement: adopt, but note Datomic stores retraction as a *separate* datom, not as a mutation of the original; a mutable "retracted-by" field inside an otherwise immutable node breaks "the projection is a fold" only if the fold is not allowed to replace the node value. Spec should state that the retraction writes a new node version (as XTDB does with system-time columns that "cannot be modified"), not an in-place update.
- Refuse to leave open: a retention/erasure rule. Every system here added erasure late and calls it blunt (Datomic excision "irrevocable", "not suitable for cleanup"; XTDB ERASE "a blunt instrument"; Dolt GC "not about removing history"). RCEdb with a SHA-256 hash chain has the harder version of the problem: erasing an event's payload breaks the chain unless the chain hashes a commitment (hash of payload) rather than the payload, which is exactly the XTDB v1 split (log holds only hashes; document store evictable). Judgement: RCEdb should adopt the XTDB-v1 split — D chains hashes of payloads; payloads live in an evictable store; eviction leaves a tombstone whose hash still verifies — and the spec should say what a fold does when it meets an evicted payload (Datomic's answer: excised datoms simply vanish from history views, which changes the fold result for past seqs; that contradicts HS-9's "state as of k is the fold up to k" unless the fold is defined over the evicted log).
- Leave open: single writer. Datomic's transactor and QLDB's journal both serialize writes on one process; RCE's δ is already a single deterministic step so RCEdb inherits single-writer semantics by construction. The Datomic forum workaround (shard into several databases, each with its own log) is the only route to write scaling that keeps the "log is the database" property; RCEdb should state whether multiple logs are in scope.
- Leave open (lesson from Jepsen on Datomic): "database as a value" + transaction functions produced a documented intra-transaction concurrency surprise. RCE's δ applies one event at a time, so the question is whether an RCEdb "transaction" is one event or a batch; if a batch, the spec must say whether batch members see each other's effects (Datomic: no, logically concurrent; Jepsen: this breaks composed invariants).
- Leave open: history size. Dolt's own guidance ("for long histories Dolt will surpass traditional database disk usage") and the XTDB community complaint are the only measured-ish statements found; no deployment growth curve for Datomic or XTDB was found. RCEdb's claim "any past state is queryable with no storage beyond snapshots" (HS-9) is true but understates the cost: the log itself grows without bound and refolding from seq 0 is O(n); Datomic's shallow segment trees and Dolt's structural sharing exist precisely to avoid refolding.

## KQ2. Datalog engines and relational logic: Soufflé, LogicBlox, Datomic/XTDB Datalog, DDlog, Rel, Dedalus; why Datalog keeps returning

### Takeaway
Datalog keeps returning because stratified negation plus finite fixpoints give a language that is declarative, terminating by construction, and compilable to fast specialised code (Soufflé's partial-evaluation-to-C++ is the benchmark); the recurring failures are at the edges: engines diverge on semantics of extensions, optimisation passes introduce cross-rule bugs, and production users (XTDB) dropped Datalog when they needed SQL and tables.

### Cited Findings
- Soufflé (Jordan, Scholz, Subotić, CAV 2016, LNCS 9780 pp. 422–430): synthesises Datalog into monolithic C++ analyzers using semi-naive evaluation "as a scaffold for partial-evaluation-based translation", specialising relational data structures per program; compiles through a Relational Algebra Machine (RAM) representation; reported to outperform available Datalog tools on large program-analysis benchmarks and be competitive with hand-crafted tools; points-to analysis on OpenJDK7 in under a minute — [Soufflé CAV 2016 (UCL Discovery)](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/1474713/), [Soufflé wiki](https://github.com/oracle/souffle/wiki); a 2019 follow-up says generated code has "similar performance to a hand-written program" — [Provenance for Large-scale Datalog, arXiv 1907.05045](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.05045)
- Differential Datalog (DDlog, VMware): "a dialect of Datalog that automates incremental computation"; compiler translates programs to Differential Dataflow — [VMware Research, Differential Datalog](https://research.vmware.com/publications/differential-datalog)
- Dedalus: reduces to "a subset of Datalog with negation, aggregate functions, successor and choice" with stratified monotonic reasoning with negation over time — [Dedalus (UC Berkeley)](https://digicoll.lib.berkeley.edu/record/137135)
- Rel (RelationalAI): "the starting point of Rel is Datalog with first-order logic formulas and aggregation in the bodies", with recursion built on Datalog foundations — [Rel paper, arXiv 2504.10323](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.10323v2)
- Stratified negation definition: a program is stratified if no dependency-graph cycle contains a negative edge; stratum(p) > stratum(q) when p depends negatively on q — [UMONS Datalog primer](https://web.umons.ac.be/app/uploads/sites/84/2024/06/primerDatalog.pdf)
- Engine reliability: a 2024 study found cross-rule optimisation bugs across Datalog engines (Soufflé, DDlog and others) — [Finding Cross-rule Optimization Bugs in Datalog Engines, arXiv 2402.12863](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.12863)
- Hellerstein, "The Declarative Imperative" / "Datalog Redux" (PODS 2010 invited lecture): reflects on seven years of Datalog extensions for networking and distributed systems, argues declarative database theory can underpin parallel and distributed programming — [Datalog Redux (UW copy)](https://courses.cs.washington.edu/courses/cse544/11wi/papers/datalog-redux.pdf)
- Datalog 2.0 workshop (5th edition, Dallas, Oct 2024): "Datalog has resurrected as a lively topic with applications in many different areas of computer science, as well as industry" — [CEUR-WS Vol-3801 preface](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-3801/xpreface.pdf); Ketsman & Koutris 2022 survey of modern Datalog engines notes renewed industry and research interest — [Foundations and Trends in Databases DBS-073](https://nowpublishers.com/article/DownloadSummary/DBS-073)
- 2025 wave: Google open-sourced Mangle (Aug 2025), a Datalog-based Go library adding aggregation, function calls and optional type checking (secondary coverage only) — [Medium, Google Mangle](https://medium.com/render-beyond/meet-google-mangle-a-smarter-programming-language-for-databases-dependencies-and-data-chaos-b7f6181f5afa); CozoDB (Datalog, time-travel, vectors) has conflicting maintenance signals: dbdb.io lists it as abandoned since 2024, a Dec 2025 community issue asks if it is maintained, last release 0.7.6 — [dbdb.io CozoDB](https://dbdb.io/db/cozodb), [LeanKG migration note](https://docs.rs/crate/leankg/0.20.2/source/docs/plan-migrate-cozo-to-postgres-pgvector.md)
- XTDB dropped Datalog in v2 for XTQL/SQL because v2 has tables; v2 lacks recursive CTEs (native fixpoint) — [XTDB discuss](https://discuss.xtdb.com/t/best-version-for-use-case-1-0-vs-2-0/571)
- Fluree's Datalog guarantees termination by budgets (max iterations/facts/time/memory), not by proof — [Fluree docs](https://labs.flur.ee/docs/db/v4.0/query/datalog-rules)

### Inferences (judgements on RCEdb)
- Adopt (G-5 stratified fixpoint): stratified negation with aggregation only over lower strata is the consensus from Soufflé, Dedalus, DDlog and Rel; termination follows from finiteness of the graph. RCEdb's choice to *refuse* non-stratifiable queries at check time is stricter than Fluree's budget approach and matches Soufflé's compile-time stratification check.
- Adopt (from Soufflé): semi-naive evaluation as the normative evaluation order for recursive queries, because it is the one strategy with a known, mechanisable correspondence to the naive fixpoint; RCEdb needs this to keep "same result, canonical order" independent of engine.
- Refuse: unbounded "Datalog as the query language" (XTDB v1's path). XTDB's reversal shows a fact-based Datalog store without tables fails the SQL audience; RCEdb's G-6 (tables as a graph special case) sidesteps this only if a table query reads as a table query, which is a usability claim not yet tested.
- Leave open: cross-rule optimisation bugs (arXiv 2402.12863) are the strongest argument for RCEdb's "no optimiser that changes meaning" stance (HS-7: structure is a placement choice). But that stance has a measured cost: Soufflé's speed comes precisely from specialising data structures per rule. RCEdb should state whether a certified optimiser (MGQL-style, with a Lean proof of equivalence) is in scope.
- Leave open: HS-8 says "Recursive queries are not in D.5" while G-5 allows stratified fixpoints — these need reconciling in the spec.

## KQ3. Graph databases and GQL: Neo4j/Cypher, TigerGraph, Memgraph, Kuzu, AGE, Neptune, ISO GQL and GPML, property graph vs RDF; ordering, bags, path restrictors; strings vs typed queries

### Takeaway
GQL (ISO/IEC 39075, April 2024) standardises bag semantics, three-valued nulls and path modes, with TRAIL/DIFFERENT EDGES as defaults because they guarantee termination; the formal literature (Cypher SIGMOD 2018, GPML, Core GQL, MGQL) consistently leaves out ORDER BY and updates, and engines diverge on path-pattern answers. String queries carry a documented injection surface that parameters do not close (labels and property names cannot be parameters), which is the strongest external support for typed query values.

### Cited Findings
- Cypher 9 (Francis, Green, Guagliardo, Libkin et al., SIGMOD 2018, pp. 1433–1445) is the first version governed by the openCypher Implementers Group — [Cypher SIGMOD 2018 (Edinburgh PURE)](https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/files/56321692/cypher_sigmod18_crc_1.pdf); the companion "Formal Semantics of the Language Cypher" gives denotational semantics for a read-only core and explicitly excludes "all update (write) clauses, line-ordering and aggregation" — [arXiv 1802.09984](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.09984)
- GQL published by ISO/IEC in April 2024 as ISO/IEC 39075:2024 — [Ultipa GQL conformance](https://www.ultipa.com/docs/v5/gql/gql-conformance); path modes: WALK no constraint, TRAIL no repeated edge, ACYCLIC no repeated node, SIMPLE repeat allowed only when first and last node coincide; "TRAIL and DIFFERENT EDGES are defaults in popular languages and guarantee termination of pattern matching, since traversed paths cannot repeat edges and are bounded by the graph's edge count" — [PathFinder, arXiv 2306.02194](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.02194v2), [Same Pattern, Different Answer, arXiv 2609.23032](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.23032)
- Engine divergence: a 2026 paper gives "a reference semantics and divergence map for GQL and SQL/PGQ path patterns", i.e. the same pattern yields different answers across implementations — [arXiv 2609.23032](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.23032); vendor conformance tables mark ACYCLIC/SIMPLE/WALK unsupported with TRAIL default — [Ultipa path patterns](https://www.ultipa.com/docs/v5/gql/path-patterns)
- MGQL (Thimmaiah, Lin, Gligoric, OOPSLA 2026): "first mechanized, small-step operational semantics for a substantial fragment of GQL", covering pattern matching and traversal under TRAIL, three-valued null propagation, aggregation, composite queries, a schema-aware type system; Lean 4 implementation "more than 23,000 lines across 14 modules" with progress/preservation proofs and a certified type checker; states that earlier formalizations (GPC, Core GQL, Core PGQ) leave out "nulls, bags, schemas, and composite queries" — [MGQL arXiv 2608.24565](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.24565), [SPLASH 2026 listing](https://2026.splashcon.org/details/oopsla-2026/85/MGQL-An-Executable-Small-Step-Semantics-of-GQL), [Gligoric PDF](https://users.ece.utexas.edu/~gligoric/papers/ThimmaiahETAL26MGQL.pdf). The local RCE spec adds (from its own read of MGQL) that MGQL does not cover ORDER BY, selectors such as ANY SHORTEST, other path modes, or updates, and validates on six LDBC SNB queries — [/home/user/rce/spec/RCE-SPEC.md, E.11]
- "A Researcher's Digest of GQL" (Francis, Gheerbrant, Guagliardo, Libkin, Marsault, Martens, Murlak, Peterfreund, Rogova, Vrgoč), ICDT 2023 invited, DOI 10.4230/LIPIcs.ICDT.2023.1 — [DBLP](https://dblp.uni-trier.de/pid/47/9855.html)
- Kuzu (Feng, Jin, Chen, Liu, Salihoğlu, CIDR 2023): targets many-to-many, cyclic and recursive joins; columnar disk storage, CSR adjacency lists and join indices, vectorized and factorized query processor; planner mixes binary joins with worst-case-optimal multiway intersections because cyclic queries with many-to-many relationships "can generate unnecessarily large amounts of intermediate results under traditional binary join plans" — [Kuzu TR (Waterloo)](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~ssalihog/papers/kuzu-tr.pdf), [Kuzu blog, WCOJ](https://blog.kuzudb.com/post/wcoj)
- Cypher injection: Neo4j's KB says "not everything in a query is parameterizable" and string concatenation remains vulnerable; labels "cannot be parameters" — [Neo4j KB, Protecting against Cypher injection](https://neo4j.com/developer/kb/protecting-against-cypher-injection/), [Varonis, Neo4jection](https://www.varonis.com/blog/neo4jection-secrets-data-and-cloud-exploits); CVE-2026-46591 (Apache Camel Neo4j): values were bound as parameters but property names (JSON keys) "were still concatenated into the query string verbatim" — [stack.watch CVE-2026-46591](https://stack.watch/vuln/CVE-2026-46591/); the same pattern in MemOS — [MemOS issue #2205](https://github.com/MemTensor/MemOS/issues/2205)
- Property graph vs RDF: Angles et al. 2017 survey compares SPARQL, Gremlin, Cypher semantics and complexity, and proposes a formalisation of the property graph model — [arXiv 1610.06264](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1610.06264); Hartig: node/edge identifiers are local to a property graph whereas URIs are global, RDF lacks native edge properties and is not a true multigraph, and "there is no commonly agreed-upon formal definition of the property graph model" — [Reconciliation of RDF* and Property Graphs, arXiv 1409.3288](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1409.3288), [Hartig SEMANTiCS 2019 slides](https://2019.semantics.cc/sites/2019.semantics.cc/files/SlidesAMAR2019-public.pdf)

### Inferences (judgements on RCEdb)
- Adopt (G-3, typed query values): the CVE-2026-46591 pattern — values parameterised, structure concatenated — is exactly the hole that a typed pattern value closes, because labels and property names are types and fields rather than text. This is the best-evidenced win in the whole G-1..G-6 set.
- Adopt (G-4/G-5, bag default + TRAIL): matches GQL's defaults and MGQL's mechanised fragment; TRAIL's termination argument (bounded by edge count) is the same bound RCEdb cites.
- Leave open but flag as risk: RCEdb cites MGQL as "grounded in ISO section by section", yet arXiv 2609.23032 documents that engines give different answers to the same path pattern. RCEdb's safety is that its reading is fixed by its own typed semantics, not by vendor behaviour; the cost is that an RCE pattern's "stated GQL reading" may not match any shipping engine for ACYCLIC/SIMPLE/WALK, which MGQL does not cover. Spec should restrict the vocabulary to TRAIL until MGQL or RCE's own proof covers more.
- Refuse (for now): selectors (ANY SHORTEST etc.). MGQL excludes them; GQL lets them pick among ties; RCEdb's rule "pick by canonical order" is a sound but RCE-only reading. Keep them out of the normative fragment until proven.
- Leave open: Kuzu's evidence that factorized/WCOJ execution matters for cyclic many-to-many patterns. RCEdb's HS-7 says structure is a placement choice; the Kuzu result says plan shape changes intermediate-result size by orders of magnitude for exactly the queries G-3 admits. A graph-pattern RCEdb without WCOJ-style evaluation may be correct and impractically slow on cyclic patterns; this is an unmeasured risk.
- Leave open: RDF vs property graph. RCEdb's G-1 picks property graphs with local ids; Hartig's point that URIs are global suggests RCEdb needs a stated story for cross-log identity (content hash of creating event + local id would give global ids for free, a judgement).

### Gaps
- The ISO 39075 text itself is paywalled and was not read; bag semantics and ordering rules are known only through GPML/MGQL/Cypher papers.
- TigerGraph, Memgraph, Apache AGE, Amazon Neptune: no primary sources on their ordering or path-mode semantics surfaced in this session.
- No source was found that compares the runtime cost of typed query values versus parsed strings.

## KQ4. Provenance: why/where/how provenance, semirings, ProvSQL, provenance in Datalog; cost of recording creating/retracting event per fact

### Takeaway
Semiring (how-) provenance is the unifying theory (Green–Karvounarakis–Tannen 2007; surveyed by Cheney, Chiticariu, Tan 2009), and the one measured general-purpose implementation, ProvSQL, reports a constant ~2–3x query-time overhead; per-fact creating/retracting ids (Datomic's tx component) are the cheap, lineage-only form of why-provenance and are not a substitute for how-provenance of derived facts.

### Cited Findings
- Cheney, Chiticariu, Tan, "Provenance in Databases: Why, How, and Where", Foundations and Trends in Databases 1(4):379–474, 2009, DOI 10.1561/1900000006; covers why-, how- (including provenance semirings) and where-provenance and their applications (confidence computation, view maintenance/update, debugging, annotation propagation) — [survey PDF (Edinburgh)](https://homepages.inf.ed.ac.uk/jcheney/publications/provdbsurvey.pdf), [now publishers DBS-006](https://nowpublishers.com/article/Details/DBS-006)
- ProvSQL (Sen, Maniu, Senellart; ICDT 2025 and arXiv 2504.12058): stores annotations in the universal semiring so provenance is computed once and specialised to any semiring at query time; uses provenance circuits to avoid duplicating subexpressions; "in aggregate the overhead of provenance tracking is constant (around 2 to 3 times the cost of the query without provenance); variations exist for individual queries"; handles multi-gigabyte databases; benchmarked against GProM and MayBMS; supports Boolean reachability, counting, probability, Shapley values, where-provenance and data-modification tracking; current PGXN release 1.12.0 — [arXiv 2504.12058v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.12058v2), [LIPIcs ICDT 2025.33](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol328-icdt2025/html/LIPIcs.ICDT.2025.33/LIPIcs.ICDT.2025.33.html), [PGXN provsql](https://pgxn.org/dist/provsql/)
- Soufflé provenance: "Provenance for Large-scale Datalog" (2019) and the POPL 2021 "Debugging Large-Scale Datalog: A Scalable Provenance Evaluation Strategy" add provenance to the compiled engine — [arXiv 1907.05045](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.05045), [POPL 2021 listing](https://popl21.sigplan.org/details/POPL-2021-research-papers/64/Debugging-Large-Scale-Datalog-A-Scalable-Provenance-Evaluation-Strategy)
- Datomic: every datom retains a reference to the asserting/retracting transaction (one component of the five-tuple) — [Jepsen 2024](https://jepsen.io/analyses/datomic-pro-1.0.7075)
- Fluree: logical transaction counter `t` per transaction — [Fluree docs glossary](https://labs.flur.ee/docs/db/v4.0/reference/glossary)

### Inferences (judgements on RCEdb)
- Adopt (G-2): storing creating and retracting event ids per node/edge is the Datomic pattern and is cheap for base facts: two fixed-width ids per element. Estimate (researcher's, not sourced): with 8-byte seq ids, 16 bytes per element, which for a node with a handful of scalar fields is on the order of 10–30% of the element, and for an edge with no properties can be 50% or more of the element. Datomic's tx component is 1 of 5 datom fields and Datomic treats that as acceptable at billion-datom scale.
- Leave open: G-2 gives where/why-lineage for base facts only. For *derived* facts (anything a fold computes from several events) ProvSQL shows that how-provenance costs 2–3x at query time and needs circuits to stay compact. RCEdb should say whether derived nodes carry a single "creating event" (the last event that touched them, which is lineage, not provenance) or a provenance set. The fold itself gives the honest answer: the provenance of any derived value is the prefix of D, which is correct but useless; a per-element semiring annotation is the known fix, with ProvSQL's measured cost.
- Refuse: putting provenance annotations in the hash-chained log itself. ProvSQL and Soufflé both compute provenance as a derived structure; keeping it as part of the index (a projection, HS-7) preserves the ability to drop or recompute it.

### Gaps
- No measured storage overhead for the Datomic tx component or XTDB system-time columns was found; the per-element estimate above is the researcher's.
- Green, Karvounarakis, Tannen (PODS 2007) was not surfaced as a primary page in this session; it is cited through the Cheney survey and ProvSQL.

## KQ5. Canonical ordering: who defines result order, what determinism costs, sorted vs hash indexes

### Takeaway
No surveyed query language defines result order without ORDER BY: SQL, Cypher (its formal semantics excludes ordering) and GQL (MGQL excludes ORDER BY) all leave it to the engine. Datomic and XTDB keep *indexes* sorted (EAVT/AEVT/AVET/VAET; XTDB v2 Arrow files), which makes a canonical scan order available cheaply but is not exposed as a result-order guarantee.

### Cited Findings
- Cypher formal semantics excludes "line-ordering" — [arXiv 1802.09984](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.09984); MGQL excludes ORDER BY (local spec reading of MGQL) — [/home/user/rce/spec/RCE-SPEC.md, E.11]
- Datomic's four indexes are sorted by their component order (E-A-V-T etc.) and are covering — [Datomic docs, indexes](https://docs.datomic.com/query/indexes.html), [Datomic glossary](https://docs.datomic.com/glossary.html); Jepsen notes "Datomic stores multi-value attributes as unordered sets" — [GeekNews summary of Jepsen report](https://news.hada.io/topic?id=14852)
- XTDB v2 stores data as Apache Arrow columnar files on object storage — [XTDB v2 hub](https://www.xtdb.com/v2)
- Prolly trees (Dolt) are ordered search trees with probabilistic chunk boundaries, so a full-table scan is in key order — [Dolt docs, storage engine](https://docs.dolthub.com/architecture/storage-engine)
- Engines return different answers to the same GQL/PGQ path pattern (ordering is one of the divergence axes in a 2026 divergence map) — [arXiv 2609.23032](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.23032)

### Inferences (judgements on RCEdb)
- Adopt (HS-8 canonical order): RCEdb is alone among the surveyed systems in making result order part of the semantics. The cost is a sort (or ordered merge) on every result that is not produced by an index-order scan; sorted covering indexes (Datomic) and ordered Prolly trees (Dolt) show the index side is cheap. Estimate (researcher's): the extra cost is O(r log r) per result of r rows when the plan does not preserve key order, which for hash joins is always.
- Refuse hash maps as the canonical index structure; adopt sorted maps as the *reference* structure while keeping HS-7's "structure is a placement choice" — a hash index may serve a keyed read, but any scan must produce canonical order, so the reference implementation should be a sorted map (judgement, consistent with Datomic's choice).
- Leave open: Datomic's unordered multi-value sets and bag semantics in GQL both mean "one row per homomorphism" with no inherent order; RCEdb's canonical order over bags requires a total order on rows including duplicates, which 7.1 must define for rows containing floats/NaN and nulls (MGQL's three-valued nulls make this non-trivial).

### Gaps
- No source quantifying the runtime cost of deterministic result ordering was found.
- No Datomic or XTDB statement on query result order (as opposed to index order) was found.

## KQ6. Content addressing and Merkle structures: Prolly trees, Merkle B-trees, Merkle search trees, verifiable logs; cost of hashing every row

### Takeaway
Content-addressed trees (Prolly trees in Noms/Dolt, Merkle search trees of Auvolat & Taïani) make structural sharing, diff and verification cheap but turn every write into a rewrite of a root-to-leaf path plus hashing; Dolt's two decades of benchmark posts show this overhead can be driven to roughly MySQL parity for OLTP, while QLDB's shutdown and immudb's hybrid design show that verifiability alone did not sustain a product.

### Cited Findings
- Merkle Search Trees (Auvolat & Taïani, SRDS 2019): deterministic B-tree-like structure where an item's level is the count of leading zeros of its hash; built as an anti-entropy primitive for state-based CRDTs that converges deterministically across peers — [Auvolat slides](https://dept-info.labri.fr/~gavoille/descartes/slides-paris-auvolat.pdf), [merkle-search-tree crate docs](https://docs.rs/merkle-search-tree); used in ATProto/Bluesky; Prolly trees instead choose boundaries from content patterns; the two are "cousins" with key differences — [Gustafson, Merklizing the key/value store, 2023](https://joelgustafson.com/posts/2023-05-04/merklizing-the-key-value-store-for-fun-and-profit), [jzhao Prolly Trees note](https://jzhao.xyz/thoughts/Prolly-Trees)
- Dolt: chunks hashed (SHA-512/160), ~4 KB target, most writes rewrite multiple chunks and can rewrite a large portion of the tree; GC needed for intermediate forms persisted between commits — [Dolt storage engine docs](https://docs.dolthub.com/architecture/storage-engine), [DoltHub GC 2020](https://www.dolthub.com/blog/2020-10-16-garbage-collection-in-dolt/), [DoltHub generational GC 2021](https://www.dolthub.com/blog/2021-08-13-generational-gc/)
- Measured end-to-end cost (vendor): Dolt moved from ~15x slower than MySQL (2020) to ~25% slower reads / 13% faster writes (Dec 2024) to ~5% faster on sysbench (Apr 2026); TPC-C ~40% of MySQL throughput — [DoltHub 2024-12-23](https://dolthub.com/blog/2024-12-23-2024-perf-summary), [Dolt docs latency](https://dolthub.com/docs/sql-reference/benchmarks/latency), [DoltHub 2026-04-09](https://www.dolthub.com/blog/2026-04-09-improving-doltlite/)
- immudb: persistent Merkle tree over transaction hashes (log proofs) + per-transaction entry tree (EH) + linear chain (Alh); "the main Merkle tree proves history up to a given transaction, and the linear proof bridges from there to the current state" — [immudb docs](https://docs.immudb.io/0.8.0/how-it-works.html)
- QLDB: journal stores an immutable record of all committed transactions; shut down 31 July 2025; replacement lacks cryptographic verifiability — [InfoQ 2024](https://www.infoq.com/news/2024/07/aws-kill-qldb), [awsglossary (third-party)](https://awsglossary.org/terms/qldb)

### Inferences (judgements on RCEdb)
- Adopt (already E.3): a linear SHA-256 hash chain over events is immudb's "Alh" linear proof and QLDB's journal digest; it gives O(n) verification of history. immudb's lesson is to add a Merkle tree over transaction hashes so a prefix proof is O(log n); RCEdb should leave this open as an index (HS-7 projection), not a change to D.
- Refuse: hashing every *index row* (Dolt-style Prolly trees) as a normative requirement. Dolt's history shows ~4 years of engineering to reach parity and still 40% of MySQL on TPC-C; since RCEdb indexes are rebuildable projections checked "by a digest per seq" (HS-7), a digest over the fold result per seq gives verifiability without per-chunk content addressing. Leave content-addressed indexes open as a placement choice for diff/merge use cases (G-6 documents, branching).
- Leave open: Merkle search trees are the right structure if RCEdb ever has multiple logs that must reconcile (Auvolat & Taïani's CRDT setting); irrelevant for a single-writer log.

### Gaps
- The MST paper's own measurements were not surfaced; no head-to-head MST vs Prolly benchmark exists in the sources found.
- No per-row hashing microbenchmark (cost of SHA-256 per event relative to fold cost) was found.

## KQ7. Time-travel storage cost, excision/erase tools, GDPR tension with immutable logs

### Takeaway
No surveyed vendor publishes a measured history-growth curve; the honest statements are Dolt's ("for long histories Dolt will surpass traditional database disk usage") and XTDB's community ("can't ... expect to reclaim space"). Every system resolves the GDPR tension the same way: keep the log immutable but make payloads evictable (XTDB's hash-only log + evictable documents; Datomic Cloud's "encrypt and throw away the key"; Datomic Pro's irrevocable excision as a last resort).

### Cited Findings
- Datomic excision: irrevocable, for privacy and retention-period removal, not for storage reduction; `:db/noHistory` conserves storage without semantic guarantees; Datomic Cloud lacked excision (2021), workaround "encryption + throw away the key" — [Datomic docs, excision](https://docs.datomic.com/on-prem/excision.html), [Datomic forum 665](https://forum.datomic.com/t/data-non-retention-and-redaction/665), [Datomic forum 323](https://forum.datomic.com/t/support-for-excision-or-similar/323)
- XTDB: transaction history "immutable, meaning transactions will never be removed"; v1 log holds only hashes, document store evictable; `evict` removes document versions while history stays; v2 keeps superseded versions unless ERASE'd "for legal reasons"; ERASE "a blunt instrument" and space not reclaimable (community, 2024); retention policies configurable since PR #2591 (Dec 2025 thread) — [XTDB docs, why XTDB](https://docs.xtdb.com/intro/why-xtdb), [JUXT Introducing Crux](https://juxt.pro/blog/introducing-crux), [XTDB dev diary](https://xtdb.com/blog/dev-diary-feb-24), [Clojurians log 2024-11-21](https://clojurians-log.clojureverse.org/xtdb/2024-11-21), [Clojurians log 2025-12-05](https://clojurians-log.clojureverse.org/xtdb/2025-12-05)
- Dolt: full history kept; disk garbage from intermediate Prolly-tree forms; GC does not remove history; long histories exceed traditional DB disk use; 120 GB → 5–20 GB after GC in one operator's runbook (third-party) — [DoltHub sizing 2023](https://www.dolthub.com/blog/2023-12-06-sizing-your-dolt-instance), [Dolt GC docs](https://docs.dolthub.com/sql-reference/server/garbage-collection), [Gas City runbook](https://docs.gascity.com/troubleshooting/dolt-bloat-recovery)
- Fluree stores only deltas so versioning does not multiply storage (vendor claim, unmeasured) — [Fluree blog](https://www.flur.ee/blog/data-centric-time)
- QLDB data not migrated by 31 July 2025 was permanently deleted (vendor blog) — [Codenotary blog](https://codenotary.com/blog/navigating-the-transition-from-amazon-qldb-to-immudb-vault)

### Inferences (judgements on RCEdb)
- Adopt: the XTDB v1 split (D chains *commitments* to payloads; payloads are a separately-stored, evictable projection input). This is the only design in the set that keeps a verifiable chain and satisfies erasure; Datomic Cloud's "throw away the key" is the same idea with encryption as the commitment.
- Adopt: a `noHistory`-style per-type or per-field declaration (Datomic) so high-churn fields do not accrete history in the graph projection; it costs nothing in D (the log still has the events) and bounds index size.
- Leave open and must be specified: what HS-9's fold yields for a seq whose events have been evicted. Datomic's excision changes history views retroactively; XTDB's evict leaves the transaction but not the content. RCEdb's "state as of k is the fold up to k" needs a defined fold over tombstones (a judgement: the fold should treat an evicted payload as a typed `evicted` value so the fold still terminates and type-checks, and the digest per seq must be the digest of the pre-eviction fold so old digests still verify).
- Refuse: "no storage beyond snapshots" (HS-9) as a cost statement. Dolt and XTDB both show that the log plus the cumulative versions in the index are the dominant cost; snapshots only bound refold time, not storage. The spec should separate the two.

### Gaps
- No measured history growth for any Datomic or XTDB production deployment was found; the only numbers are Dolt's rough sizing formula and one third-party operator's GC figures.
- Whether XTDB v2's ERASE now reclaims space (post PR #2591) was not confirmed from official docs.
- No legal analysis of whether a hash of erased personal data in an immutable log satisfies GDPR Art. 17 was found; the sources only assert "intended to assist with technical compliance".
