# RCEdb among database systems and DeltaDB: what the record says the host contract should state

RCEdb's core claim ("the log is the database": a SHA-256 hash-chained log D, indexes as pure folds of a prefix, typed terminating queries in canonical order, as-of by folding a prefix, one admitter per log) is the mainstream conclusion of the shared-log lineage (Tango 2013, Kreps 2013, Kleppmann 2015, TigerBeetle, command logging) stated as a law rather than an implementation, and nothing in the surveyed record contradicts it. What the record adds lies almost entirely around the log, in six places RCEdb's spec is silent or optimistic: durability (every tested engine lost data under power fault in OSDI 2014's 8/8 and 60-vulnerability studies, fsync is not portable, and TigerBeetle treats single-disk fsync as insufficient), unbounded history (Temporal caps a history at 51,200 events/50 MB; Dolt and XTDB admit long histories cost more disk than a mutable store; refold is O(|D|) without checkpoints), erasure (Datomic, XTDB and Dolt all bolted on "blunt" erasure late; a hash chain makes it harder unless D chains commitments rather than payloads, as XTDB v1 did), event-schema evolution (the first-ranked challenge in the only peer-reviewed field study of event-sourced systems), verification cost (a plain chain gives O(N) proofs; CT/QLDB/immudb added Merkle trees for O(log N) and checkpointed verify-on-open), and the partial-order proposal's theorem T62, which is only true if dep-independent events commute (the CALM/Mazurkiewicz condition), and whose read side is unsafe unless reads are monotone (Laddad et al. 2022). Energy is almost unmeasured in the literature: the only audited per-transaction figure is HP's 2010 TPC-Energy 5.93 W/KtpmC (≈0.36 J per TPC-C transaction, derived estimate) and the only mobile storage baseline is 800 µJ/KB for random fsync'd writes (Mohan 2017). DeltaDB from Zed shares the slogan but logs character-level CRDT edits with causal-DAG identity, server-synced and closed-source, and has not published hash-chaining, verification or growth policy; its one transferable idea is storing the conversation that produced a change in the same log. The report recommends roughly twenty adoptions (mostly wording and host-contract rules), nine refusals, and leaves eleven questions open, and ends with a reconciled design. Every "adopt/refuse/open" verdict is the researchers' judgement; every number carries its source and date; vendor-reported figures are marked as such.

## Relational engines: the write-ahead log as a recovery device, and the durability defaults

The relational engines' logs are recovery devices, not canonical artefacts. PostgreSQL writes full-page images into WAL after each checkpoint to defend against torn pages, and a 2026 pgsql-hackers proposal to add a doublewrite buffer argues "full-page writes and checkpoint frequency are fundamentally at odds", with a reply warning it "would probably mean doing two fsyncs where we only now do one" ([pgsql-hackers 2026](https://www.postgresql.org/message-id/CANeUpr_cOYTy_Gg48zrb%3D-jv-y7o_4pFvwYu6%3DuECDPqJuZVfw%40mail.gmail.com); [reply](https://www.postgresql.org/message-id/CAGbZs7grxqvQXrLFZuz4rhRb5jMOnrJ3YG4EHuoHej-UhJwWNg%40mail.gmail.com)); the same tension was being debated in 2011 ([pgsql-hackers 2011](https://www.postgresql.org/message-id/4EFC449F02000025000441CD@gw.wicourts.gov)). None of this apparatus applies to RCEdb, which has no mutable heap to protect; the one lesson that transfers is that commit cost is the fsync and must be amortised. The PostgreSQL record for group commit runs from no gain at all (Haas, 2 clients; a POWER7 machine with commit_delay) to **34 → 159 TPS (~4.7x) at 20 clients on a slow-fsync disk** (Jeff Janes, pgbench, 2012) ([Janes](https://www.postgresql.org/message-id/CAMkU=1whmdC+o7RP-Lc3Gd_7OS2LM4S6Ltk5jc2ZrsNjvL4gvg@mail.gmail.com); [Haas](https://www.postgresql.org/message-id/CA%2BTgmoZ2FsfEi%3DWDzsuT45b%3D11q08ddp0QKjDduKta9VnpMJfw%40mail.gmail.com)), with a consumer SSD showing only 1,169 → 1,233 TPS at one client and 2,611 → 3,022 at five (Krogh, 2012) ([Krogh](https://www.postgresql.org/message-id/4F25A39D.5080801%40krogh.cc)). RCEdb's "durable mark moves only on a bound Sync event" is group commit by construction; the spec should state the batching window and its latency floor explicitly.

Durability failures have three roots, each with a measured study. Zheng et al. (OSDI 2014) tested eight databases under simulated power faults and found "All 8 databases exhibit erroneous behavior" ([USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/node/186197); [slides](https://www.ece.iastate.edu/%7Emai/docs/papers/2014_OSDI_slides.pdf)); Pillai et al. (OSDI 2014) found **60 crash vulnerabilities across 11 applications** that assumed append atomicity or ordering their file systems did not promise ([ADSL](https://research.cs.wisc.edu/adsl/Publications/alice-osdi14.html)); and fsyncgate (reported March 2018) showed PostgreSQL had for years assumed fsync() would report and retain errors Linux did not, after which "PostgreSQL will now PANIC on fsync() failure", backpatched to 9.4, with "similar changes... in InnoDB/MySQL, WiredTiger/MongoDB" ([LWN 752063](https://lwn.net/Articles/752063/); [PostgreSQL wiki](https://wiki.postgresql.org/wiki/Fsync_Errors)). SQLite documents the third root honestly: in WAL mode with synchronous=NORMAL (its compile-time default for WAL) "a committed transaction might roll back following a power loss" ([SQLite docsrc](https://www.sqlite.org/docsrc/info/bcf687da9c172292)). RCEdb's T39 ("after a failed barrier the admitter appends nothing") is the correct response and is stronger than any surveyed engine's default; what it lacks is the rule that recovery must come from on-disk D only, never from memory (Rebello et al., below).

On semantics the engines got the standard right and practice wrong. SQLite states that "the order in which result rows are returned... is undefined if there is no ORDER BY clause" and that tie order under ORDER BY is also undefined ([SQLite requirements mirror](https://svnlliurexnet.gva.es/leia/mods-backports/trunk/sqlite3/www/requirements.html); [docsrc](https://www.sqlite.org/docsrc/info/a7856e4125c57d75)); RCEdb's canonical order is strictly stronger but must be total over the full row, with NULL and NaN placement fixed in the type. Time travel exists in CockroachDB only "within the garbage collection window" set by `ttlseconds` ([CockroachDB docs](https://cockroachlabs.com/docs/v22.1/as-of-system-time)); RCEdb inverts the cost (history free, as-of O(k)), and the proposed provenance refinement (creating/retracting event id per node and edge) turns as-of-k into a keyed filter `created <= k < retracted`, the SQL:2011 system-versioning trick without its GC. Incremental view maintenance exists as pg_ivm, which its developer reports as "more than 200 times faster than re-computation" on TPC-H (**developer-reported**, 2021) but which a competitor describes as "blocking writes until the materialized view update is complete" with outer joins and ORDER BY unsupported until recently ([pgsql-hackers IVM](https://www.postgresql.org/message-id/20210112190308.ee9e98c24080310a487349f3%40sraoss.co.jp); [Epsio blog, partisan](https://www.epsio.io/blog/pg-ivm); [PGConf.EU 2026](https://www.postgresql.eu/events/pgconfeu2026/sessions/session/8219-pg_ivm-recent-improvements-and-future-directions-in-incremental-view-maintenance/)); HS-7 is IVM done as a specification, and the engines' experience argues for *deferred* maintenance (an index may lag, its digest says which k it is at). No SQL engine proves termination: SQL Server's MAXRECURSION defaults to 100 and tops out at 32,767 ([Database Journal](https://www.databasejournal.com/ms-sql/tips-for-using-common-table-expressions/)), PostgreSQL 14 added a CYCLE clause; these are rails, not proofs, and HS-8's language restriction is the real answer. The 2024 Stonebraker/Pavlo survey could not be fetched; its reported positions (graph DBMSs will not displace relational engines that add SQL/PGQ; blockchain databases have no use case for a single operator) are **unverified background** and, if confirmed, argue for the property graph as a derived index rather than a data model and for the hash chain to be justified as an integrity device, never as trust or consensus.

## Log-centric and streaming systems: schema evolution, history size and flush defaults

The lineage's claim is RCEdb's claim. Tango (SOSP 2013) states that an object's state exists as "a history, which is an ordered sequence of updates stored durably in the shared log, and any number of views" ([Tango PDF](https://www.cs.yale.edu/homes/mahesh/papers/tangososp.pdf)); Kleppmann's 2015 talk called it "a surreptitious attempt to take the database architecture we know, and turn it inside out" ([Kleppmann 2015](https://martin.kleppmann.com/2015/03/04/turning-the-database-inside-out.html)). The practice record finds three recurring costs, none of them about the log itself. The only peer-reviewed field study of event-sourced systems (Overeem et al., 25 engineers, JSS 2021) ranks **schema evolution first** among five challenge areas and recommends versioned events, upcasting and, in some cases, copy-and-transform ([arXiv 2104.01146](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2104.01146)); RCEdb's spec has no stated rule for versioned events, and copy-and-transform (rewriting the log) is incompatible with a hash chain and should be refused outright. Replay cost grows linearly with history: Temporal warns at 10,240 events or 10 MB and errors at **51,200 events or 50 MB** per workflow, relying on continue-as-new ([Temporal limits](https://docs.temporal.io/workflow-execution/limits)); the figure is per-workflow, not comparable to a single D, but the mechanism (snapshot or cut is mandatory) is. Code drift breaks replay: Temporal's `getVersion` writes a marker event into the history so old executions take old branches, and a community thread shows nondeterminism persisting outside the patched branch ([Temporal versioning](https://docs.temporal.io/develop/java/workflows/versioning); [community thread](https://community.temporal.io/t/non-determinism-after-versioning/7527)). The transferable lesson is that "the code is part of the history": a program-version field in the entry hash or at each cut turns replay with a different delta into a digest mismatch (HS-7) rather than a silently different state. RCEdb's delta being total and pure removes the nondeterministic-user-code class that Temporal, Restate and Inngest police only by convention; the spec should make purity a checked property.

Kafka's durability does not meet RCEdb's host contract. Kafka's own operations guide says "We recommend using the default flush settings which disable application fsync entirely" because "a failed node will always recover from its replicas" ([Kafka docs](https://kafka.apache.org/0110/operations/hardware-and-os/)). Jepsen found in Redpanda 21.10.1 (April 2022) the same message at multiple offsets and messages reordered to earlier offsets (issue #3003), duplicate writes with idempotence enabled, and acknowledged messages that never appeared in any poll ([Jepsen Redpanda](https://jepsen.io/analyses/redpanda-21.10.1)); in Bufstream 0.1.0 (November 2024) it found "more serious problems in Kafka's transaction protocol, including write loss, aborted reads, and torn transactions" that "remain unresolved" and affect Kafka itself ([Jepsen Bufstream](https://jepsen.io/blog/2024-11-12-bufstream-0.1.0)). Two rules follow: "durable" must mean fsync'd (or quorum-fsync'd) before the Sync event, and the Sync event must carry the acknowledged seq and entry hash so a client detects the "same message at two offsets" anomaly as a hash mismatch; and multi-chain atomicity layered as a two-phase protocol would inherit Kafka's unresolved failure class, so atomicity must be one entry or one cut. Throughput numbers in this space are vendor-contested and are **not usable as targets**: Confluent reports Kafka at 605 MB/s with 5 ms p99 at 200 MB/s (2020, **vendor**), StreamNative reports Pulsar at 605 MB/s with Kafka p99 "up to seconds" (2020/2022, **vendor**), Redpanda claims "10x faster tail latencies" (**vendor**) ([Confluent](https://www.confluent.io/blog/kafka-fastest-messaging-system/); [StreamNative](https://streamnative.io/blog/tech/2020-11-09-benchmark-pulsar-kafka-performance); [Redpanda](https://redpanda.com/blog/redpanda-vs-kafka-performance-benchmark)). The researchers' **estimate** is that a single-writer hash-chained log with fsync per window is bounded by device fsync latency (sub-millisecond on NVMe, several milliseconds on cloud disks), not by SHA-256.

The streaming-SQL theory is a direct fit. DBSP (Budiu et al., PVLDB 16(7), 2023, VLDB best paper) gives "a general algorithm for incremental view maintenance over arbitrary DBSP programs" and implements both SQL and Datalog ([PVLDB](https://www.vldb.org/pvldb/vol16/p1601-budiu.pdf)); Feldera states the proofs "were machine checked using the Lean Theorem Prover" (**vendor statement**) ([Feldera](https://feldera.com/blog/Best-Research-Paper-VLDB-2023)). RCEdb's DBSP proposal (Q^Δ = D ∘ Q ∘ I) is well founded with two conditions: Z-sets carry integer multiplicities, so a set-typed query is a Z-set plus `distinct` (non-linear), and the type must say which; and because the chain rule is an equality, HS-7's per-seq digest becomes a test of the implementation, not the theory. Flo (POPL 2025) names "streaming progress and eager execution" as the two properties that make streaming outputs deterministic ([arXiv 2411.08274](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.08274)); RCEdb should make progress an entry in D (the Sync event or a cut), never a wall-clock watermark. Performance claims here are all author- or vendor-run and unreproduced: Noria's 14M requests/s on one server (OSDI 2018, **author-reported**), Feldera's "millions of events per second on a laptop" (**vendor**), Materialize's "sub-10 ms" (**vendor**) ([Noria](https://www.usenix.org/conference/osdi18/presentation/gjengset); [Feldera docs](https://docs.feldera.com/concepts); [Cockroach Labs interview](https://www.cockroachlabs.com/blog/how-frank-mcsherrys-company-achieves-sub-10ms-latency-at-scale/)).

The partial-order proposal has a research lineage and an unproven obligation. FuzzyLog (OSDI 2018) exposed "a partially ordered shared log abstraction" and on 6 nodes supported **3M/s single-key writes and 150K/s atomic cross-shard renames** ([USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/osdi18/presentation/lockerman)); Delos (OSDI 2020) virtualised the ordering protocol and "upgraded its consensus protocol without downtime for a 10X latency improvement" ([USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/osdi20/presentation/balakrishnan)). RCEdb's entry hash over {chain, seq, event, deps} is a Merkle DAG, which FuzzyLog did not have; but "seq" must be per chain, and state hashes are well defined only at cuts. Theorem T62 (any dep-consistent linearisation folds to the same cut state) requires that dep-independent events commute under delta, the Mazurkiewicz-trace independence condition; this is a proof obligation per pair of independent event types, not a free theorem, and the spec should say whether it is proven for all delta or only for delta restricted to events shown to commute (**judgement**, not sourced). CALM supplies the read-side rule: "the programs that have consistent, coordination-free distributed implementations are exactly the programs that can be expressed in monotonic logic" ([Hellerstein & Alvaro](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1901.01930)); Laddad et al. (PVLDB 2022) add that "observations of CRDT state are unconstrained and unsafe" unless the query is monotone ([PVLDB](https://www.vldb.org/pvldb/vol16/p856-power.pdf)). Anna's "over two orders of magnitude" speedup is **author-reported** for a high-contention multi-master KVS and is not a prediction for RCEdb ([arXiv 1809.00089](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1809.00089)). Finally, transparency logs supply the compaction answer: RFC 6962 Merkle trees "show that any particular version of the log is a superset of any particular previous version" ([RFC 6962](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc6962)); QLDB built the same construction (SHA-256, one block per transaction, Merkle digest) and was retired on **31 July 2025** with unmigrated data deleted ([AWS QLDB docs](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/fr_fr/qldb/latest/developerguide/verification.html); [Classmethod](https://dev.classmethod.jp/articles/amazon-qldb-eos-announcement/)); Trillian "is now in maintenance mode" in favour of tiled logs ([Trillian](https://google.github.io/trillian/)).

## Immutable, temporal, Datalog and graph stores: erasure, recursion and path termination

Every surviving immutable store keeps an append-only record plus rebuildable indexes, and every one learned erasure the hard way. Datomic's datom is a five-tuple (entity, attribute, value, transaction, assert/retract) and writes go through "a single designated process in charge of writes" ([Jepsen Datomic, May 2024](https://jepsen.io/analyses/datomic-pro-1.0.7075)); its excision is "irrevocable" and a forum moderator states it "is NOT intended, nor is it suitable for, 'clean up'", while Datomic Cloud in 2021 had no removal feature and suggested "encryption + throw away the key" ([Datomic excision](https://docs.datomic.com/on-prem/excision.html); [forum 665](https://forum.datomic.com/t/data-non-retention-and-redaction/665); [forum 323](https://forum.datomic.com/t/support-for-excision-or-similar/323)). XTDB v1 (Crux) made the split RCEdb needs: "the main transaction log contains only hashes and is immutable, and all document content is stored in a dedicated document log... that can be evicted" ([JUXT](https://juxt.pro/blog/introducing-crux)); XTDB v2's community called ERASE "quite a blunt instrument" in 2024, with retention policies arriving via PR #2591 by December 2025 ([Clojurians 2024](https://clojurians-log.clojureverse.org/xtdb/2024-11-21); [Clojurians 2025](https://clojurians-log.clojureverse.org/xtdb/2025-12-05)). Dolt's own sizing post says "for long histories, Dolt will surpass traditional database disk usage" and its GC is "not about removing history" ([DoltHub sizing 2023](https://www.dolthub.com/blog/2023-12-06-sizing-your-dolt-instance); [Dolt GC docs](https://docs.dolthub.com/sql-reference/server/garbage-collection)). Jepsen's Datomic analysis also found that intra-transaction operations are "applied logically concurrent with one another", so invariants preserved by individual transaction functions can break when combined ([Jepsen blog](https://jepsen.io/blog/2024-05-15-datomic-pro-1.0.7075)); RCEdb must say whether a "transaction" is one event or a batch and whether batch members see each other. The consequence for RCEdb is the single most important structural change in this report: **D should chain commitments (hashes) to payloads, with payloads in an evictable store**, and the fold must be defined over tombstones (a typed `evicted` value) so that pre-eviction digests still verify; HS-9's "no storage beyond snapshots" is a cost claim the Dolt and XTDB evidence contradicts and should be reworded to separate storage from refold time.

Content addressing of index rows has a measured price. Dolt went from about 15x slower than MySQL (2020) to roughly 25% slower reads and 13% faster writes (December 2024) to about 5% faster on sysbench (April 2026), all **vendor-reported**, with TPC-C at ~40% of MySQL throughput ([DoltHub 2024](https://dolthub.com/blog/2024-12-23-2024-perf-summary); [DoltHub 2026](https://www.dolthub.com/blog/2026-04-09-improving-doltlite/); [Dolt latency docs](https://dolthub.com/docs/sql-reference/benchmarks/latency)); the one independent measurement (BranchBench, arXiv April 2026) found Dolt's read latency "2–4000× higher than Neon's" ([arXiv 2604.17180](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.17180)). Prolly-tree hashing of every index chunk should therefore be a placement option, not a norm; HS-7's digest over the fold result gives verifiability without it. immudb's design (Merkle tree over transaction hashes plus a linear tail) shows the standard fix for O(N) chain verification ([immudb docs](https://docs.immudb.io/0.8.0/how-it-works.html)).

Datalog keeps returning because stratified negation plus finite fixpoints terminate by construction and compile to fast code: Soufflé synthesises Datalog into C++ via partial evaluation and does points-to analysis on OpenJDK7 in under a minute (CAV 2016) ([UCL Discovery](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/1474713/)), but a 2024 study found cross-rule optimisation bugs across Soufflé, DDlog and others ([arXiv 2402.12863](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.12863)), which is the strongest argument for RCEdb's "no optimiser that changes meaning" stance and also its cost, since Soufflé's speed comes from exactly that specialisation. Fluree guarantees Datalog termination only "by the budget controls (max iterations, max facts, max time, max memory)" ([Fluree docs](https://labs.flur.ee/docs/db/v4.0/query/datalog-rules)); XTDB dropped Datalog for XTQL/SQL in v2 "because v2 has tables" and lacks recursive CTEs ([XTDB discuss](https://discuss.xtdb.com/t/best-version-for-use-case-1-0-vs-2-0/571)). The spec has an internal conflict to resolve: HS-8 says recursive queries are not in D.5 while G-5 admits stratified fixpoints.

GQL (ISO/IEC 39075:2024, April 2024) standardises bag semantics, three-valued nulls and path modes; "TRAIL and DIFFERENT EDGES are defaults in popular languages and guarantee termination of pattern matching, since traversed paths cannot repeat edges and are bounded by the graph's edge count" ([PathFinder, arXiv 2306.02194](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.02194v2)), yet a 2026 divergence map shows engines returning different answers to the same path pattern ([arXiv 2609.23032](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.23032)). MGQL (OOPSLA 2026) is "the first mechanized, small-step operational semantics for a substantial fragment of GQL", **23,000+ lines of Lean 4** with a certified type checker, but it excludes ORDER BY, selectors such as ANY SHORTEST, non-TRAIL path modes and updates ([MGQL](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.24565); local spec E.11). The best-evidenced win for typed query values (G-3) is the injection record: Neo4j's own KB says labels "cannot be parameters", and CVE-2026-46591 (Apache Camel Neo4j) bound values as parameters while property names "were still concatenated into the query string verbatim" ([Neo4j KB](https://neo4j.com/developer/kb/protecting-against-cypher-injection/); [CVE-2026-46591](https://stack.watch/vuln/CVE-2026-46591/)). Kuzu's evidence that cyclic many-to-many patterns "generate unnecessarily large amounts of intermediate results under traditional binary join plans" without worst-case-optimal joins is an unmeasured risk for a graph-pattern RCEdb that treats plan shape as a placement choice ([Kuzu TR](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~ssalihog/papers/kuzu-tr.pdf)). On provenance, ProvSQL (ICDT 2025) reports that how-provenance costs "around 2 to 3 times the cost of the query without provenance" ([arXiv 2504.12058](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.12058v2)); G-2's creating/retracting ids are the cheap lineage form (researchers' **estimate**: 16 bytes per element at 8-byte seqs, 10–30% of a small node, 50%+ of a property-less edge) and are not how-provenance for derived facts. No surveyed query language defines result order without ORDER BY; Datomic's four sorted covering indexes and Dolt's ordered Prolly trees show the index side of canonical order is cheap ([Datomic indexes](https://docs.datomic.com/query/indexes.html)); the researchers' **estimate** of the extra query-side cost is O(r log r) per result when the plan does not preserve key order, which for hash joins is always.

## Deterministic, verified and tamper-evident systems, and the energy record

TigerBeetle is the closest shipped analogue to RCEdb and contradicts its durability wording. Its safety docs say the VOPR simulator runs cluster code under network, storage and process faults "at 1000x speed, 24/7 on 1024 cores" (**vendor**), and that durability is cluster-wide because disk firmware lies: the design "exceeds the fsync durability of a single disk" ([TigerBeetle safety](https://docs.tigerbeetle.com/concepts/safety/); [DESIGN.md mirror](https://github.com/tbg/tigerbeetle/blob/main/docs/DESIGN.md)). Its simulator injects up to **8% read and 9% write corruption** per replica (2023 blog) ([TigerBeetle 2023](https://tigerbeetle.com/blog/2023-07-11-we-put-a-distributed-database-in-the-browser)). Jepsen (0.16.11–0.16.30, June 2025) found seven crashes and two safety issues, "exceptional resilience to disk corruption", no way to handle total loss of a node's data, and requests retried forever ([Jepsen TigerBeetle](https://jepsen.io/analyses/tigerbeetle-0.16.11)). Independent throughput is far below the "1M transactions per second" design target (an investor statement, not a benchmark): SoftwareMill measured **~42k TPS** long-running on a local single node (~2.8x batched PostgreSQL at ~15k) and ~4,100–4,500 TPS at a fixed 5k req/s in the cloud with p50 ~32 ms ([SoftwareMill local](https://softwaremill.com/tigerbeetle-vs-postgresql-performance-benchmark-setup-local-tests/); [cloud](https://softwaremill.com/tigetbeetle-vs-postgresql-performance-benchmark-harness-cloud-tests)). The realistic reference class for a single-admitter, single-threaded RCEdb is tens of thousands of events per second with batching (**estimate**).

Deterministic simulation is the testing regime the field converged on. FoundationDB's authors credit a single-process simulator with Flow and BUGGIFY ("unusual (but not contract-breaking) behavior") for the system's stability and for catching bugs that appear only after particular crash/restart sequences, and state in the paper that simulation cannot reliably detect performance problems ([CACM 2023](https://cacm.acm.org/?p=227399); [fdb-paper](https://www.foundationdb.org/files/fdb-paper.pdf)). Antithesis found an etcd stale-read bug "only once in about every 19 days of testing" that "traditional chaos testing would likely never reproduce" ([Antithesis 2026](https://antithesis.com/blog/2026/causality_analysis/)); Turso's SQLite rewrite ships a native DST suite with `--seed` and `--doublecheck` replay comparison plus Antithesis ([tursodatabase/turso](https://github.com/tursodatabase/turso)). RCEdb can build this cheaply: delta is already pure, T37 replays with adapters disconnected, and T3 (two replays give identical state hashes) is VOPR's state checker as a runtime invariant. On determinism in transaction processing, Calvin (SIGMOD 2012) ran "half a million TPC-C transactions per second on a cluster of commodity machines" with a 10 ms sequencer epoch as its latency floor ([Calvin](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/courses/archive/fall19/cos418/papers/calvin.pdf)); command logging reached ~1.5x the throughput of main-memory ARIES in VoltDB at the cost of recovery time (Malviya 2012), and adaptive logging claimed ~10x faster recovery because "every transaction must be redone one by one" ([MIT thesis](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/75716); [SIGMOD 2016](https://www.comp.nus.edu.sg/~ooibc/alogging-sigmod16.pdf)). "The log is the database" is command logging, and its measured penalty is refold time; checkpoints of S are the standard mitigation and HS-7/HS-9 should be cost-labelled O(|D|) without them. Verified-storage work shows what is provable and at what price: DaisyNFS (OSDI 2022) isolated crash and concurrency reasoning into a transaction layer with 22x proof-to-code versus 2x for the rest ([OSDI 2022](https://www.usenix.org/conference/osdi22/presentation/chajed)); PoWER (OSDI 2025) placed crash-consistency requirements in API preconditions with provable corruption detection, verifying CapybaraKV in under a minute ([OSDI 2025](https://www.usenix.org/conference/osdi25/presentation/leblanc)); seL4 cost "about 25 person years" for ~7,500 lines of C ([Trustworthy Systems](https://trustworthy.systems/projects/OLD/l4.verified/numbers)); and extraction, compiler and runtime are always in the TCB ([PLDI 2024](https://rocq-prover.org/papers/verified-extraction-from-coq-to-ocaml)). RCEdb should publish a TCB statement and not claim "verified database" on the strength of the Lean theorem about delta alone; a Verus host for the tiny append/sync/open surface is plausible at months of effort (**estimate**, no Verus person-year figures exist).

Durability primitives are not portable. Rebello et al. (ATC 2020) tested ext4, XFS and Btrfs with PostgreSQL, LMDB, LevelDB, SQLite and Redis: "pages are always marked clean after a failed fsync", and "although applications use many failure-handling strategies, none are sufficient" ([ATC 2020](https://www.usenix.org/conference/atc20/presentation/rebello)). Zheng et al. (FAST 2013) found **13 of 15 SSDs** exhibited bit corruption, shorn writes, unserializable writes or device failure under power fault ([FAST 2013](https://www.usenix.org/conference/fast13/understanding-robustness-ssds-under-power-fault)). macOS fsync does not flush the drive cache and needs F_FULLFSYNC, which Apple says "will be significantly slower"; Windows FlushFileBuffers does flush ([Apple forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/800906); [pgsql-hackers Munro](https://www.postgresql.org/message-id/CA%2BhUKG%2Ba-7r4GpADsasCnuDBiqC1c31DAQQco2FayVtB9V3sQw%40mail.gmail.com)). Verify-on-open by a sequential SHA-256 chain is bandwidth-bound: BoringSSL reaches ~1.57 GB/s with SHA-NI on 16 KB inputs on an EPYC VM ([BoringSSL](https://boringssl.googlesource.com/boringssl/+/17c8c811049a82d6c34897c718503444529c8d8b)), giving the researchers' **estimate** of ~0.7 s per GB and **~11–12 minutes per TB** (≈40 min without SHA-NI), which is why a checkpointed chain head is needed. Transparency logs supply that: RFC 9162 Merkle trees give O(log N) inclusion and consistency proofs, measured over the wire at ~1.3 kB sent / 2.9 kB received per inclusion proof in a 2017 study ([RFC 9162](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9162.html); [arXiv 1711.07278](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1711.07278)); immudb's client SDKs checked only the first and last links of the linear chain (CVE-2022-36111) ([GHSA](https://github.com/codenotary/immudb/security/advisories/GHSA-672p-m5jq-mrh8)), so the verifier itself must be simulator-tested; Git's SHA-1→SHA-256 transition shows the cost of not versioning the hash algorithm ([Git transition doc](https://code.googlesource.com/git/+/HEAD/Documentation/technical/hash-function-transition.txt)). For replication, CockroachDB's consistency checker compares SHA-512 snapshots per range on a 24 h cycle and "it is not possible for the system to identify whether the Raft leader or its replica is responsible" ([Cockroach Labs](https://www.cockroachlabs.com/blog/trust-but-verify-cockroachdb-checks-replication/)); RCEdb's pair (log-head hash, state hash) can attribute fault, a genuine advantage worth stating.

The energy ledger is nearly empty. The only standardised, audited per-transaction figure is HP's July 2010 TPC-Energy result: **5.93 W/KtpmC** on TPC-C at 1,193,472 tpmC and 5.84–6.72 W/tpsE on TPC-E ([TPC press 2010](https://www.tpc.org/information/press/tpcpress20100707.asp)), which the researchers derive as **≈0.36 J per TPC-C transaction**, whole-system wall meter (**estimate**, 16 years old). Schiper, Pedone and van Renesse (DSN 2014) found "the most efficient replication protocol achieves less than 60% of the energy efficiency of a stand-alone server on the TPC-C benchmark", with a primary-backup variant on low-power backups reaching 79% ([DSN 2014](https://www.inf.usi.ch/pedone/Paper/2014/2014DSNb.pdf)), the single largest sourced energy effect relevant to replicated D. A 2026 RAPL study found an index scan cut per-query energy ~72% over a sequential scan at 1% selectivity ([Vestnik RSREU 2026](https://vestnik.rsreu.ru/en/archive/2026/issue-95/1824-1995-4565-2026-95-66-72)); ARM clusters showed 2.6–9.5x energy-efficiency advantage for in-memory database work (CCGrid 2012) ([VTT](https://cris.vtt.fi/en/publications/energy-and-cost-efficiency-analysis-of-arm-based-clusters/)). No source gives joules per fsync, and no shipped database reports energy per operation. The three-way est_j / reported_j / measured_j labelling RCEdb already mandates maps exactly onto the literature's three instruments (modelled footprint, RAPL counter, wall meter), and Tsirogiannis et al. (SIGMOD 2010) warn CPU power is non-linear in utilisation ([Unpaywall](https://unpaywall.org/10.1145%2F1807167.1807194)), so any per-event figure must subtract a stated idle baseline.

## Embedded, local-first and CRDT stores: a single writer, loss windows and reads over convergent state

Every mature embedded engine converged on one writer plus many readers and explicit media assumptions. SQLite's WAL allows "only one writer at a time"; since 3.7.9 it assumes powersafe overwrite and "does not add any redundancy to the database file for the purpose of detecting corruption or I/O errors" ([SQLite wal](https://sqlite.org/wal.html); [psow](https://sqlite.org/psow.html)); its crash testing is simulated (crash on the N-th xSync) and the project reports **590 times as much test code as library code** with 100% MC/DC coverage (**project-reported**, 3.42.0, 2023) ([SQLite testing](https://sqlite.org/testing.html)). LMDB and redb are copy-on-write B+trees with one writer; redb's design doc states its atomic commit "rel[ies] on the assumption that single-byte writes are atomic" and offers 2PC because XXH3 "is not collision resistant" so a malicious writer could forge a complete-looking commit ([redb design](https://github.com/cberner/redb/blob/master/docs/design.md)), an argument for RCEdb's SHA-256 chain. RocksDB's FAST 2021 retrospective lists the lesson that "data corruption needed to be detected earlier and at every layer of the system", the opposite of SQLite's posture and the one RCEdb's chain and per-seq digest already take ([FAST 2021](https://usenix.org/conference/fast21/presentation/dong)); RocksDB's 10–30x write amplification figure comes only from a third-party mirror of its wiki ([Mintlify mirror](https://www.mintlify.com/facebook/rocksdb/concepts/architecture)). Engines that relaxed durability by default (sled's 500 ms flush, SQLite WAL+NORMAL, Chrome 121's IndexedDB "relaxed" default claiming 3–30x speedups, **vendor**) all document a post-power-loss loss window ([sled](https://github.com/spacejam/sled); [Chrome blog](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/indexeddb-durability-mode-now-defaults-to-relaxed/)); RCEdb is the only design surveyed that makes the window a logged event (`host.Synced(upto)`). On mobile, SQLite's Android default (DELETE-FULL) issues four fsync() calls per insert and WALDIO's fix reached **5.1x** inserts/s over WAL mode (ATC 2015) ([ATC 2015](https://usenix.org/system/files/conference/atc15/atc15-paper-lee-wongun.pdf); [KAIST](https://oslab.kaist.ac.kr/wp-content/uploads/esos_files/publication/conferences/international/coarsegrained_mtime_update_SAC.pdf)); the one primary mobile energy measurement (Mohan et al., HotStorage 2017, Monsoon meter on an eMMC Android phone, the only PDF read in full) found storage at **36% of system energy** under random I/O, random fsync'd 4 KB writes at **800 µJ/KB and 15x a sequential write**, and F2FS at half of ext4's energy for most workloads ([HotStorage 2017](https://www.usenix.org/conference/hotstorage17/program/presentation/mohan)). The researchers' **extrapolation** is that a sequentially appended 4 KB entry costs on the order of 0.2 mJ rather than 3 mJ, so D's sequential append with one fsync per window is the lowest-energy shape this evidence supports, and random index rewrites should be deferred.

The local-first field split in 2024, and the split is RCEdb's design. ElectricSQL announced on 17 July 2024 that "adoption dynamics for creating a new database from scratch are tough" and pivoted to a Postgres sync layer with "Writes go through your own API endpoint" ([Electric Next](https://electric-sql.com/blog/2024/07/17/electric-next)). Replicache re-runs pure mutators on the server and replays unconfirmed local mutations "like a git rebase"; the server result "replaces the speculative one" ([Replicache](https://doc.replicache.dev/concepts/how-it-works)). PowerSync holds a client at its last checkpoint while uploads are pending so it "never has to resolve conflicts locally", at the cost of a visible "flicker" when an asynchronous backend misses a change ([PowerSync consistency](https://docs.powersync.com/architecture/consistency)). That is an admitter plus an optimistic client fold, and RCEdb has a simplification none of them have: because the admitter's fold and the client's fold are the same delta over the same bytes (T37), "rebase" is just re-folding the pending chain after the admitted prefix, with no second mutator implementation. CRDT-first systems paid in size and complexity: Automerge 3 reduced "pasting Moby Dick" from about 700 MB to 1.3 MB of memory (**vendor**) ([Automerge 3](https://automerge.org/blog/automerge-3/)); on the 260k-op editing trace Automerge 2.1.10 parsed in 1,805 ms against Yjs's 39 ms ([crdt-benchmarks](https://github.com/dmonad/crdt-benchmarks)); cr-sqlite's v1 is "history-free" and plans a causal event log, which D already is ([cr-sqlite](https://github.com/vlcn-io/cr-sqlite)). Kleppmann's 2022 BFT-CRDT paper (ops naming predecessor hashes) is the shape of the proposed `{chain, seq, deps, hash}` entry ([PaPoC 2022](https://martin.kleppmann.com/papers/bft-crdt-papoc22.pdf)); interleaving and move anomalies ([PaPoC 2019](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/~arb33/papers/KleppmannEtAl-InterleavingAnomalies-PaPoC2019.pdf)) are sequence-CRDT problems RCEdb avoids because a list ordered by key is a canonical scan. Ditto chose HLCs because "physical clocks were not reliable in a distributed mesh network" ([Ditto](https://legacydocs.ditto.live/common/how-it-works/crdt)), supporting HC-18..20's clock-free safety, while LiteFS's time-based Consul lease is the dependence RCE refuses ([LiteFS](https://www.fly.io/docs/litefs/how-it-works)). Durable Objects' input/output gates (hold outgoing messages until pending writes complete) are RCEdb's "no λ output before `host.Synced`" rule stated by another vendor ([Cloudflare DO](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/concepts/what-are-durable-objects/)), and D1's session bookmark is the admitted seq passed back for read-your-writes ([D1](https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/best-practices/read-replication/)). Browser storage is the least durable layer: DuckDB-WASM's npm `latest` "creates the OPFS files but never writes to them" as of 18 September 2026 ([DuckDB](https://duckdb.org/2026/09/18/opfs-wasm.html)); a browser must be a replica with a pending chain, never durable D.

## Delta and DeltaDB

Delta, announced 12 August 2026 and in public beta since 16 September 2026 on macOS, Linux, Windows and the web, is "a multiplayer environment for coding with agents and reviewing what they build", organised around threads that bind an agent conversation to the worktree it edits; Zed says it has disabled pull requests on Delta's own private repository and that **33 developers have landed 570 changes** through threads (**company-reported**, no quality or defect metric attached) ([Zed: Introducing Delta](https://zed.dev/blog/introducing-delta); [Zed: Replace PRs](https://zed.dev/blog/delta-public-beta); [The New Stack, Sep 2026](https://thenewstack.io/zed-delta-github-alternative/)). DeltaDB, first described in the August 2025 Series B post ("records every operation rather than only commits", "uses CRDTs"), is as published a CRDT operation log beside Git: "Each edit, from a person or an agent, is recorded as a delta with a stable identity, its author, and the version it builds on", where the base is "either earlier deltas or a Git blob", so "ordering then comes from the causal graph rather than a shared clock"; it is "loosely based on diamond-types" and storage "grows with the size of the edits instead of the number of edits squared" (a complexity claim, not a benchmark) ([delta.dev/deltadb](https://delta.dev/deltadb); [Zed: Sequoia Backs Zed](https://zed.dev/blog/sequoia-backs-zed); [Irwin, 15 Oct 2026](https://delta.dev/blog/future-of-version-control)). Data lives "on your machine and on our servers" and agent conversations "pass through Zed Cloud" ([Delta data storage](https://delta.dev/docs/privacy-and-security/data-storage)). As of 23 October 2026 no engine source, schema, wire format, benchmark, size limit, GC policy or Jepsen-style analysis has been published; the only public Zed repositories mentioning DeltaDB are a Claude Code plugin and a Nix flake ([delta-claude](https://github.com/zed-industries/delta-claude); [delta-nix](https://github.com/zed-industries/delta-nix)); and, critically for comparison with RCEdb, **nothing published says deltas are hash-chained, content-addressed, or that replay is verified**: "stable identity" is described as causal-parent-derived. HN's substantive objections ("a more advanced git blame"; it does not show *why* code changed; removing PRs removes the merge gate without saying where it goes) map onto RCE's design space, since RCE's `intent`/`example` admission gate is exactly the unoccupied position ([HN 49727245](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49727245); [Ghumare](https://rohitghumare.com/blog/zed-delta-after-pull-requests/)). The agent-PR record explains why a gate matters: AIDev reports acceptance of Codex ~64%, Devin ~49%, Copilot ~35% against a 76.8% human baseline ([AIDev](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2507.15003v1)), LinearB 2026 reports AI PRs merging within 30 days 32.7% of the time versus 84.4% unassisted (**vendor benchmark**) ([LinearB](https://linearb.io/library/ai-in-software-development)), and 67.9% of 654 rejected agent PRs carried no reviewer feedback ([arXiv 2602.04226](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.04226v1)). Among the wider field, only Unison gives definitions hash-of-syntax-tree identity with repointable names, the closest analogue to RCE's content-addressed `defs` ([Unison](https://www.unison-lang.org/docs/the-big-idea/)); Pijul and Jujutsu make changes and conflicts first-class; none records a checked reason for a change.

## Reconciled design

The log stays as specified, but its entries and its host contract change shape. **Entries**: each entry hashes {chain, per-chain seq, event, deps, program_version, commitment(payload)}, with the payload itself in a separately stored, evictable store (XTDB v1's split); the hash algorithm is a versioned header field. **Cuts and checkpoints**: a `cut` entry carries {state_digest, merkle_root over entry hashes, prev_checkpoint}; HS-9 is restated as "state at k is the fold of D from the latest verified checkpoint at or below k", HS-7 and HS-9 are cost-labelled O(|D|) without checkpoints, verify-on-open checks the suffix after the last checkpoint (turning the estimated 11–12 min/TB into seconds), and a Merkle tree over D is an index giving O(log N) inclusion and consistency proofs. **Erasure**: eviction replaces a payload with a tombstone; the fold meets a typed `evicted` value and still terminates and type-checks; the digest at k remains the pre-eviction digest; `noHistory`-style declarations bound index growth; rewriting the log is refused. **Schema evolution**: events carry a schema version; upcasters are pure functions applied at fold time; a change to delta is a program_version change and shows up as a digest mismatch, never a silently different state. **Host contract**: "durable" means fsync per OS (Linux fdatasync/fsync, macOS F_FULLFSYNC, Windows FlushFileBuffers) or quorum-fsync before the Sync event; the Sync event carries the acknowledged seq, the entry hash and a possible failure, after which the admitter exits and recovers from on-disk D only, truncating a torn tail; the batching window and its latency floor are stated; λ outputs depending on an entry may be gated on its durable mark; clients carry the admitted seq as a bookmark; retries are bounded with an explicit failure event; the durability class is labelled `per_os | replicated | power_tested` next to `verified_host=false` and a written TCB (Lean kernel, SHA-256, extraction/compiler, OS sync semantics, firmware, replica count). **Queries and indexes**: canonical order is total over the full row including ties, NULL and NaN; aggregates fold in canonical order with no reassociation; the type records monotonicity, and non-monotone reads over replicated state require a cut; the path vocabulary is TRAIL only, selectors are out, stratified fixpoints (G-5) are reconciled with HS-8's recursion clause and evaluated semi-naively; indexes may lag the log with a digest stating their k, are defined by DBSP's incremental form with the set/bag distinction explicit, use a sorted map as the reference structure, hold provenance (G-2 ids; semiring annotations if ever) as projections and never inside D, and treat foreign stores as refoldable caches; the property graph is a derived index with typed query values, not a replacement data model. **Partial order**: op-based merges expressed as deps only; atomicity across chains is one entry or one cut, never a layered 2PC; T62 carries an explicit proof obligation that dep-independent events commute; an offline device appends to its own chain and refolds the pending chain after the admitted prefix, never advancing its admitted prefix while holding unadmitted entries; a browser is a replica with a pending chain. **Assurance**: a deterministic host simulator running the real code with stubbed clock, network and append, with BUGGIFY-style contract-permitted anomalies and a power-fault harness, checking that every replica's fold yields the same state hash; state hashes travel in replica acknowledgements paired with the log-head hash. **Energy**: every figure is labelled est_j / reported_j / measured_j, reported as a ratio to work plus the raw interval with the idle baseline subtracted, "committed events per joule" for replicated deployments, and no per-event advantage is claimed until a measured_j number exists; the only baselines to cite are HP's 2010 5.93 W/KtpmC and Mohan's 2017 800 µJ/KB. **From Delta**: the conversation that produced an event is itself events in D; emitted artefacts anchor to Git blobs/commits through the emit-target registry.

Eleven questions stay open and should be listed as such in the spec: multiple logs versus sharded chains (and cross-log identity); batch-versus-event transaction semantics; halt versus repair-from-replica on a verify-on-open mismatch; VR versus Raft for failover; in-process versus whole-VM simulation; third-party auditability (split-view detection); a certified optimiser and WCOJ-style evaluation for cyclic patterns; a total order over bags with floats and nulls; a text/sequence rule for collaborative editing; F_FULLFSYNC on Apple for D; and the energy of a durable append.

## Conclusion

The survey changes RCEdb's self-description more than its architecture. The log-as-database idea has been validated by a decade of shared-log research and by production systems from TigerBeetle to Durable Objects, so RCEdb's novelty is not the log but what it adds: a fold whose definition is user-visible pure code, a digest at every sequence number, and a theorem about delta. The record's lesson is that every one of those systems ended up with the same three appendages RCEdb's spec still treats as optional or implicit (checkpoints with verifiable roots, a payload/commitment split for erasure, and an explicit, per-OS, replica-counted statement of what "durable" means), and that the partial-order proposal's safety rests on two conditions the field has already named (commutativity of independent events for T62, monotonicity of reads per Laddad et al.) rather than on the hash DAG itself.

Two things RCEdb can claim that no surveyed system can are worth stating in the spec because the evidence supports them: divergence between replicas is attributable (the pair of log-head hash and state hash says which replica left the log, which CockroachDB's checker cannot), and the offline client needs no second implementation of its mutators (the admitter's fold is the client's fold). A third claim, energy per event with provenance, is available to RCEdb only because its unit of work is an event and its host contract names the instrument; the literature's ledger holds one audited figure from 2010 and one phone measurement from 2017, so the first measured_j number RCEdb publishes with an idle baseline will be, as far as this survey could find, the first of its kind for any database.
