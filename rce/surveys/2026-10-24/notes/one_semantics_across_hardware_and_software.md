**Synchronous languages: Esterel, Lustre/SCADE, SIGNAL**

- The 2003 survey revisits the 1991 Proc. IEEE special section on Esterel, Lustre and Signal. It argues that synchronous languages had become "a technology of choice for modeling, specifying, validating, and implementing real-time embedded applications." Its sections on hardware compilation could not be read (extract). Benveniste, A., Caspi, P., Edwards, S. A., Halbwachs, N., Le Guernic, P., de Simone, R., "The Synchronous Languages 12 Years Later," Proceedings of the IEEE 91(1):64–83, 2003. DOI 10.1109/JPROC.2002.805826
- Berry's draft book is the reference semantics for Pure Esterel. It gives the circuit translation used by the Esterel v5 compiler and states that a program is constructive if and only if its possibly cyclic circuit reaches electrical stabilization in bounded time. It also argues that the constructive behavioral, operational and electrical semantics are equivalent. This is a hand (paper) proof (extract) [self-reported]. Berry, G., "The Constructive Semantics of Pure Esterel," draft version 3, 2002. https://www-sop.inria.fr/members/Gerard.Berry/Papers/EsterelConstructiveBook.pdf
- This paper defines "constructive circuits" as cyclic gate-level circuits that settle to a unique value in bounded time under the up-bounded non-inertial delay model, and proves that ternary simulation decides this class. It also proves the match is not exact under the older up-bounded inertial model, which is the formal tie between Esterel's software semantics and physical gates (extract). Mendler, M., Shiple, T. R., Berry, G., "Constructive Boolean circuits and the exactness of timed ternary simulation," Formal Methods in System Design 40(3):283–329, 2012. DOI 10.1007/s10703-012-0144-6
- The Coq-checked part of the Esterel semantic chain covers only loop-free Kernel Esterel and stops at a microstep semantics. The authors say the final simulation step to circuits, which would prove the circuit translation correct, is left to later work (extract). Rieg, L., Berry, G., "Towards a Coq-verified Chain of Esterel Semantics," Leibniz Transactions on Embedded Systems 10(1), 2025. DOI 10.4230/LITES.10.1.2
- This is the first equational calculus for Esterel, and its authors say it helped them find bugs in Esterel implementations. A POPL 2020 student-research abstract lists proving the calculus sound and adequate against the circuit translation as future work (extract). Florence, S. P., You, S.-H., Tov, J. A., Findler, R. B., "A Calculus for Esterel: If can, can. If no can, no can," PACMPL 3(POPL), Art. 61, 2019. DOI 10.1145/3290374
- Esterel v7 was developed at Esterel Technologies from 2001 to 2009 on the same kernel semantics. One compiler produced C for software and Verilog/VHDL circuits for hardware, and the C was obtained by implementing the circuit's Boolean equations in software. Named industrial users were Intel, Texas Instruments, STMicroelectronics and NXP (extract) [self-reported]. Berry, G., lecture "Esterel v7 hardware synthesis and software compilation," course "Time and events in computing," Collège de France (date not retrieved). https://www.college-de-france.fr/en/agenda/lecture/time-and-events-in-computing/esterel-v7-hardware-synthesis-and-software-compilation ; and Berry, G., "Esterel v7: From Verified Formal Specification to Efficient Industrial Designs," FASE 2005, LNCS. DOI 10.1007/978-3-540-31984-9_1
- An IEEE standardization working group for Esterel (P1778) was started in April 2007 and dissolved in March 2011 (extract) [secondhand, Wikipedia "Esterel"]. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esterel
- The Columbia Esterel Compiler supports a subset of Esterel V5. From one source it emits either C, through several back ends, or a circuit as BLIF or Verilog (extract) [self-reported]. Edwards, S. A., Zeng, J., "Code Generation in the Columbia Esterel Compiler," EURASIP Journal on Embedded Systems 2007, Art. 52651. DOI 10.1155/2007/52651 ; project page https://www.cs.columbia.edu/~sedwards/cec
- Lustre is a synchronous dataflow language for reactive control. Its synchronous interpretation lets it be compiled into an efficient sequential program, and the paper also uses it to describe synchronous circuits (extract). Halbwachs, N., Caspi, P., Raymond, P., Pilaud, D., "The synchronous data flow programming language LUSTRE," Proceedings of the IEEE 79(9):1305–1320, 1991.
- Vélus is a compiler from core Lustre to assembly, built in Coq on top of CompCert. It proves that repeated execution of the generated code implements the source dataflow semantics. The target is software only (extract). Bourke, T., Brun, L., Dagand, P.-É., Leroy, X., Pouzet, M., Rieg, L., "A Formally Verified Compiler for Lustre," PLDI 2017, pp. 586–601. https://github.com/INRIA/velus
- The Vélus README limits what the proof covers. The uninitialized `pre` operator "is not yet treated" and must be rewritten as `0 fby e`, and the optional `main_sync` simulation entry point "is not formally verified" (read) [self-reported]. INRIA, Vélus README, GitHub INRIA/velus @27ba860, 2026. https://github.com/INRIA/velus
- Scade 6 (2008) combines Lustre dataflow with control structures from Esterel and SyncCharts. Its qualified code generator KCG (first version 1999) is used at the highest safety levels under DO-178C, IEC 61508, EN 50128, IEC 60880 and ISO 26262. A model runs in bounded memory "provided that imported functions do" (extract) [self-reported]. Colaço, J.-L., Pagano, B., Pouzet, M., "Scade 6: A Formal Language for Embedded Critical Software Development," TASE 2017 (invited). https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-01666470
- Ansys material says KCG is qualified at DO-330 TQL-1, so its output can be used without being verified. That equivalence rests on a tool-qualification process, not on a proof (extract) [self-reported]. Ansys/Esterel slides, TASE 2017 talk. https://tase2017.i3s.unice.fr/sites/default/files/tase17_jlcolaco_1409.pdf
- The C subset that KCG generates allocates all memory statically (no heap, no variable-length arrays), has no recursive calls, and has only loops with constant bounds. A valid model must run each cycle in bounded space and time. KCG's targets are software (C) (extract) [secondhand]. "Automatically Generating Test Cases for Safety-Critical Software via Symbolic Execution," arXiv:2209.11138, 2022 (authors not shown in the extract). https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.11138
- The Polychrony toolset for SIGNAL generates C, C++ and Java, and the extracts found no HDL back end. Code generation needs a tree-shaped clock hierarchy and an acyclic scheduling graph ("endochrony"). A translation validator for the clock-calculus phase found previously unknown compiler bugs (extract). Ngo, V. C., Talpin, J.-P., Gautier, T., "Translation Validation for Clock Transformations in a Synchronous Compiler," FASE 2015. https://people.rennes.inria.fr/Jean-Pierre.Talpin/papers/fase15.pdf

**Synchronous dataflow (SDF) and Kahn process networks (KPN)**

- In SDF, each node consumes and produces a fixed number of samples per firing. This lets the compiler schedule nodes ahead of time, which removes most run-time overhead (extract). Lee, E. A., Messerschmitt, D. G., "Synchronous Data Flow," Proceedings of the IEEE 75(9):1235–1245, 1987. DOI 10.1109/PROC.1987.13876
- A periodic schedule requires the topology matrix to have rank s−1, where s is the number of nodes. A strictly positive integer null-space vector then gives each node's firing count, and a simulated firing pass rules out deadlock (extract) [secondhand, course notes]. Lee, E. A., Messerschmitt, D. G., "Static Scheduling of Synchronous Data Flow Programs for Digital Signal Processing," IEEE Trans. Computers C-36(1):24–35, 1987. DOI 10.1109/TC.1987.5009446
- In a Kahn network the token sequences do not depend on process speed or execution order. The price of that generality is that, with unbounded FIFOs, both bounded-buffer execution and termination are undecidable in general (extract) [secondhand]. Kahn, G., "The Semantics of a Simple Language for Parallel Programming," Information Processing 74 (IFIP Congress), 1974.
- Parks' scheduler runs a Kahn network forever in bounded buffers whenever that is possible, starting with small buffers and growing the smallest one on "artificial" deadlock. It was implemented in Ptolemy (extract). Parks, T. M., "Bounded Scheduling of Process Networks," PhD dissertation, UC Berkeley, UCB/ERL M95/105, 1995. https://www2.eecs.berkeley.edu/Pubs/TechRpts/1995/ERL-95-105.pdf
- The paper places dataflow process networks as a special case of Kahn networks, with unidirectional FIFOs, non-blocking writes and blocking reads (extract). Lee, E. A., Parks, T. M., "Dataflow Process Networks," Proceedings of the IEEE 83(5):773–801, 1995. https://ptolemy.berkeley.edu/publications/papers/95/processNets/

**Ptolemy II**

- Ptolemy II proposes "hierarchical heterogeneity": each model of computation is confined to a level of the hierarchy instead of being mixed amorphously, with "domain polymorphism" for reusing components across them (extract). Eker, J., Janneck, J. W., Lee, E. A., Liu, J., Liu, X., Ludvig, J., Neuendorffer, S., Sachs, S., Xiong, Y., "Taming Heterogeneity—the Ptolemy Approach," Proceedings of the IEEE 91(1):127–144, 2003. DOI 10.1109/JPROC.2002.805829
- A 2008 extension built on the existing C code-generation framework produces synthesizable VHDL and testbench VHDL from Ptolemy II models, for FPGA and ASIC (application-specific integrated circuit) designs (extract) [self-reported]. Leung, M.-K., Filiba, T. E., Nagpal, V., "VHDL Code Generation in the Ptolemy II Environment," UCB/EECS-2008-140, 2008. https://www2.eecs.berkeley.edu/Pubs/TechRpts/2008/EECS-2008-140.html
- In the current repository, the code-generation tree `ptolemy/cg/.../procedural/` has only `c`, `java` and FMI back ends. A `ptolemy/actor/lib/vhdl` actor library "usually used in conjunction with the VHDL code generator" remains. No equivalence proof between the Java simulation and generated code was found (read: own tree listing). icyphy/ptII @800a9fa, 2026-10-21. https://github.com/icyphy/ptII

**Lingua Franca (LF) and reactors**

- Reactors are components with ports and hierarchy. They get their determinism from logical time, an event scheduler and synchronous-reactive communication, and they can run in parallel in shared-memory and distributed settings (extract). Lohstroh, M., Íncer Romeo, Í., Goens, A., Derler, P., Castrillon, J., Lee, E. A., Sangiovanni-Vincentelli, A., "Reactors: A Deterministic Model for Composable Reactive Systems," CyPhy 2019, LNCS 11971:59–85, Springer, 2020.
- LF is presented as a coordination language whose reactor semantics remove the nondeterminism that unconstrained actor interactions allow (extract). Lohstroh, M., Menard, C., Bateni, S., Lee, E. A., "Toward a Lingua Franca for Deterministic Concurrent Systems," ACM TECS 20(4):1–27, 2021. DOI 10.1145/3448128
- LF targets C, C++, Python, TypeScript and Rust. A program "is deterministic unless the programmer explicitly uses nondeterministic constructs," and the compiler flags causality loops that would prevent a deterministic reaction order (read) [self-reported]. lf-lang/lingua-franca README @a6f29e1; website `docs/introduction.md` @9767f29, 2026. https://github.com/lf-lang/website-lingua-franca
- The guarantee covers coordination, not the code inside reactions. The docs say "C is not a safe language," that programmers can "circumvent the semantics of Lingua Franca and introduce nondeterminism." They also note that `malloc`'d data is passed between reactors under reference counting rather than banned (read) [self-reported]. website-lingua-franca `docs/reference/target-language-details.mdx` @9767f29. https://github.com/lf-lang/website-lingua-franca
- The hardware work defines "hardware reactors" as a subset of the reactor model and implements them with reactor-chisel, a hardware runtime, plus Chisel and Codesign targets for system-on-chip FPGAs. Runtime overhead means circuits modeled at clock-cycle granularity are "not realistic in physical time," so the approach suits coarse-grained computation (extract). Jellum, E. R., Schoeberl, M., Lee, E. A., Orlandic, M., "Codesign of Reactor-Oriented Hardware and Software for Cyber-Physical Systems," ACM TRETS 17(4), Art. 55, 2024. DOI 10.1145/3672083
- The reactor-chisel README states its aim as "programming hardware and software for SoC FPGAs at the 'same level of abstraction'." It requires a fork of the LF compiler (`chisel3` branch), and agreement between targets is checked by integration tests (read) [self-reported]. erlingrj/reactor-chisel README @d94dd2d. https://github.com/erlingrj/reactor-chisel

**Precision-timed (PRET) machines**

- The paper argues that average-case speed has been favoured over timing predictability: multi-level caches and deep speculative pipelines make worst-case execution time depend on implementation details and context. It proposes cycle-accurate timing control in the instruction set, scratchpad memories and an interleaved pipeline (extract). Edwards, S. A., Lee, E. A., "The Case for the Precision Timed (PRET) Machine," DAC 2007 (WACI session), pp. 264–265. DOI 10.1145/1278480.1278545 ; tech report UCB/EECS-2006-149
- A follow-up defines precision-timed languages as ones where time is an integral part of the semantics, and argues that repeatable timing matters more than predictable timing (extract). Broman, D., Zimmer, M., et al., "Precision Timed Infrastructure: Design Challenges," UCB/EECS-2013-161, 2013. https://www2.eecs.berkeley.edu/Pubs/TechRpts/2013/EECS-2013-161.pdf
- FlexPRET is a 5-stage, fine-grained multithreaded RISC-V core written in Chisel and run under Verilator or on Xilinx FPGAs. Hard real-time threads are scheduled "only at a constant rate" for isolation, while soft threads share the remaining cycles. LF ships a FlexPRET target (read) [self-reported]. pretis/flexpret README @53af96f; Zimmer, M., Broman, D., Shaver, C., Lee, E. A., "FlexPRET: A Processor Platform for Mixed-Criticality Systems," RTAS 2014. https://github.com/pretis/flexpret

**Bluespec and Kôika**

- One BSV/BH source compiles either to standard Verilog or to Bluesim, a C++ cycle simulator. The README says it is proven in the Flute, Piccolo and Shakti designs (read) [self-reported]. B-Lang-org/bsc README @d0ecddf, 2026. https://github.com/B-Lang-org/bsc
- The user guide says Bluesim and Verilog show "identical cycle behavior ... because all these back-ends go through identical scheduling in bsc." It lists where they can differ: don't-care values (`?`, `-opt-undetermined-vals`), separately written primitives in error cases (for example `deq` on an empty unguarded FIFO), `$random`, violated `always_enabled`, 4-state X/Z logic, and imported Verilog. Some schedules are accepted for Verilog but rejected for Bluesim. In short, equivalence is by construction and tested, not proved (read) [self-reported]. BSC User Guide, `doc/user_guide/user_guide.tex` §"Cycle-accuracy between Bluesim and Verilog simulation," bsc @d0ecddf. https://github.com/B-Lang-org/bsc
- The meaning of a design is rule atomicity: when rules fire in parallel, the compiler inserts scheduling and multiplexing so that "the net state change is consistent with the atomic semantics of rules" (read) [self-reported]. BSV Reference Guide, `doc/BSV_ref_guide/BSV_lang.tex`, bsc @d0ecddf.
- Kôika's semantics are a Coq reference interpreter. The one-rule-at-a-time property is machine-checked (`OneRuleAtATime.v`), and `compiler_correct` (`CircuitCorrectness.v`) proves that the generated circuits "compute the exact same results" as the source. The final Verilog printer is "a thin unverified layer," and the C++ simulator Cuttlesim is "separate (unverified) code" (read) [self-reported]. mit-plv/koika README @8921e30; Bourgeat, T., Pit-Claudel, C., Chlipala, A., Arvind, "The Essence of Bluespec: A Core Language for Rule-Based Hardware Design," PLDI 2020. DOI 10.1145/3385412.3385965 ; Pit-Claudel, C., Bourgeat, T., Lau, S., Arvind, Chlipala, A., ASPLOS 2021. DOI 10.1145/3445814.3446720

**Clash**

- Clash takes its syntax and semantics from Haskell. The same source runs as software in GHC/GHCi (for example `sampleN`) and compiles to synthesizable VHDL, Verilog or SystemVerilog (read) [self-reported]. clash-lang/clash-compiler README @a59d9f5; Baaij, C., Kooijman, M., Kuper, J., Boeijink, A., Gerards, M., "CλaSH: Structural Descriptions of Synchronous Hardware Using Haskell," DSD 2010, pp. 714–721. DOI 10.1109/DSD.2010.21
- The docs list what Clash gives up:
  - data-dependent recursion such as `fibR` (it "describes an infinitely deep structure"; Clash does no behavioural synthesis with a stack), and even finitely unrollable user recursion like `mapV`;
  - recursive data types other than `Vec` and `RTree`, because every value needs a static bit-size, so lists are unsupported;
  - `Float`/`Double`, IO, ST and the FFI.

  Value recursion, which becomes feedback loops, is supported (read) [self-reported]. "Limitations of Clash," Clash.Tutorial, clash-prelude 1.6.5, Hackage. https://hackage.haskell.org/package/clash-prelude-1.6.5/docs/Clash-Tutorial.html
- The same page says outright: "The translation of Integer is not meaning-preserving." Arbitrary-precision `Integer` becomes 32 or 64 bits depending on the compiling machine or `-fclash-intwidth`, so a `fromIntegral` on `Signed 128` can drop 64 or 96 bits in hardware but not in GHC simulation (read) [self-reported]. Same source.

**CIRCT (Circuit IR Compilers and Tools)**

- CIRCT calls itself an "(experimental!)" effort to apply MLIR (Multi-Level Intermediate Representation) and LLVM methods to hardware tools. Its dialects are lowered, "perhaps in a target-specific fashion," to target dialects, so meaning is defined per dialect rather than by a single formal semantics (read) [self-reported]. llvm/circt README and `docs/Charter.md` @09536ae, 2026-10-23. https://github.com/llvm/circt
- One HW/Seq/Comb IR feeds Verilog emission, SystemC emission ("another way to simulate") and the Arc dialect. Arc is compiled through LLVM to CPU code by the arcilator simulator, which rejects recursive coroutines (read) [self-reported]. `docs/Dialects/Arc.md`, `docs/Dialects/SystemC/RationaleSystemC.md` @09536ae.
- Equivalence across lowerings is checked, not built in. `circt-lec` uses the Z3 SMT solver to prove two HW/Comb/Seq modules equivalent. Meanwhile `comb.divu`/`divs` by zero yields an "undefined value" whose function "may be different between compilations," and each front end must encode its own language's rule: X in SystemVerilog, a trap in VHDL, or 0 in some vendor blocks (read) [self-reported]. `docs/Tools/circt-lec.md`, `docs/Dialects/Comb/RationaleComb.md` @09536ae.

**SystemC and C-based HW/SW co-design**

- In SystemC the order of picking runnable processes is implementation-defined. It is only required not to vary between runs for a fixed implementation, version, application and input, so the semantics are those of a reference simulation kernel rather than a formal model (extract) [secondhand, LRM copy and citing papers]. IEEE Std 1666-2011, "IEEE Standard for Standard SystemC Language Reference Manual," 2011.
- The Accellera synthesizable subset marks dynamic process spawning (`SC_FORK`, `sc_spawn`) as Not Supported. It allows only combinational `SC_METHOD`s with static sensitivity, and supports `SC_THREAD`/`SC_CTHREAD` only with restrictions (extract). Accellera, "SystemC Synthesizable Subset," v1.4.7, March 2016. https://www.accellera.org/images/downloads/standards/systemc/SystemC_Synthesis_Subset_1_4_7.pdf
- Intel's SystemC-to-SystemVerilog compiler (ICSC) rules out recursion, `new`/`delete` for non-SystemC objects, pointer arithmetic and `&`, with only the tool's own `sc_new` allowed. It also lists cases where SC and SV results differ: `~` on `bool`; overflow, where `(k - m) % 11` is non-equivalent; and `u << 32`, which gives "1 in SC, 0 in SV" (read) [self-reported]. intel/systemc-compiler wiki, "SystemC supported." https://github.com/intel/systemc-compiler/wiki/SystemC--supported
- Handel-C is a mix of CSP (Communicating Sequential Processes) and C for FPGAs. Its timing rule is that assignment, I/O and delay statements each take exactly one clock cycle and every other statement or expression takes none. The formal denotational semantics consider only a single clock domain and ignore many data features (extract) [secondhand]. Butterfield, A., Woodcock, J., "A 'Hardware Compiler' Semantics for Handel-C," MFCSIT 2004; and "Semantic Domains for Handel-C," ENTCS. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S157106610480762X
- ROCCC 2.0 is an open-source compiler from a subset of C to VHDL for FPGA accelerators. It bans recursion and pointers that cannot be statically unaliased (extract) [self-reported, same research group]. Villarreal, J., Park, A., Najjar, W., Halstead, R., "Designing Modular Hardware Accelerators in C with ROCCC 2.0," FCCM 2010, pp. 127–134; Guo, Z., et al., "Optimized Generation of Data-path from C Codes for FPGAs," arXiv:0710.4716.
- LegUp compiles standard C to a hybrid of a MIPS soft processor and hardware accelerators. Code that uses dynamic memory, recursion or (in the first release) floating point stays in software on the processor. The 4.0 FAQ gives the hardware input as ANSI C "without recursive functions, or dynamic memory," and the papers report over 1000 downloads (extract) [self-reported]. Canis, A., et al., "LegUp: An Open-Source High-Level Synthesis Tool for FPGA-Based Processor/Accelerator Systems," ACM TECS 13(2), Art. 24, 2013. https://janders.eecg.utoronto.ca/pdfs/ACM_TECS_journal.pdf ; FAQ http://legup.eecg.utoronto.ca/docs/4.0/faq.html

**What this establishes**

- **Machine-checked proof of one meaning across targets is rare and partial.** Kôika proves its compiler to circuits but leaves the Verilog printer and the C++ simulator unverified. Vélus proves Lustre to assembly, which is software only, and excludes `pre`. The proof tying Esterel software semantics to circuits (constructive iff the circuit stabilizes) is a hand proof backed by Mendler–Shiple–Berry, and its Coq chain stops before circuits. Sources: Kôika README / Bourgeat et al. 2020; Bourke et al. 2017 / Vélus README; Berry 2002; Mendler et al. 2012; Rieg & Berry 2025.
- **Most toolchains that emit both software and hardware get matching behaviour by sharing a front end and testing, and each documents where it breaks.**
  - Bluespec: shared scheduling, with a list of exceptions.
  - Clash: `Integer` "not meaning-preserving."
  - Intel ICSC: lists non-equivalent operators.
  - CIRCT: undefined values may differ between compilations, with equivalence left to `circt-lec`.
  - SCADE KCG: equivalence certified through tool qualification, and KCG targets software only.

  Sources: BSC User Guide; Clash.Tutorial; ICSC wiki; CIRCT docs; Colaço et al. 2017 / Ansys slides.
- **The same restrictions recur wherever one meaning is achieved.**
  - State of statically known size: Clash bit-sizes; SCADE static allocation and constant-bound loops.
  - No recursion and no heap in the hardware part: Clash, SCADE, LegUp, ROCCC, ICSC, SystemC subset.
  - A discrete logical clock or tick: Esterel/Lustre instants, LF tags, Bluespec rules per cycle, Clash `Signal`.
  - Rejecting causality cycles: Esterel non-constructive programs; LF causality loops.
  - Fixed rates for static schedules: SDF.

  Sources: as cited in each section above.
- **Models that keep generality push decidability or determinism onto a runtime or the programmer.** Kahn networks are deterministic, but bounded memory is undecidable and needs Parks' dynamic scheduler. LF's determinism covers coordination only, while reaction bodies in C can break it. SystemC's process order is implementation-defined. Sources: Kahn 1974 (via secondary sources); Parks 1995; LF docs; IEEE 1666-2011 (via LRM copy).
- **Functional determinism is separate from timing determinism.** PRET and FlexPRET put timing into the instruction set and the hardware thread scheduler to get repeatable timing. reactor-chisel reports that hardware reactors modeled at clock-cycle granularity are not realistic in physical time. Sources: Edwards & Lee 2007; Broman et al. 2013; FlexPRET README; Jellum et al. 2024.
