# Working note 14: Concurrency semantics across fabrics: memory consistency models and forward-progress guarantees

Raised by the completeness critic: The survey tests 'one meaning' through floating point (IEEE 754 divergence, ReproBLAS), WebAssembly nondeterminism, and synchronous HW/SW languages (Esterel, Lustre, Bluespec, Clash). The meaning of any shared-memory parallel program also depends on two further things: the memory consistency model and the scheduler's forward-progress or fairness guarantees. Both differ between x86 and Arm CPUs, NVIDIA and AMD GPUs, the Vulkan and OpenCL models, Wasm threads, and MCUs or HLS-generated FPGA logic, which often have no defined model at all. A spin-wait that is correct on a CPU can hang on a GPU that lacks independent forward progress. Without this literature the synthesis cannot claim, or rule out, one meaning for parallel code. That is the case that matters on GPUs and FPGAs.

**GPU hardware and ISA memory models**
- Read-read coherence (coRR) violations: 100k runs, same CTA (thread block), `.cg` loads. GTX 540m 11642, Tesla C2075 8879, GTX 660 9599, GTX Titan 9787. GTX 750 (Maxwell), HD 6570 and HD 7970 showed 0. Weak behaviours appeared on every chip tested except the GTX 280 (Fig. 1). — J. Alglave, M. Batty, A. F. Donaldson, G. Gopalakrishnan, J. Ketema, D. Poetzl, T. Sorensen, J. Wickerson, "GPU Concurrency: Weak Behaviours and Programming Assumptions," ASPLOS 2015, doi:10.1145/2694344.2694391 (copy read: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/tyler-utah/tyler-utah.github.io/main/files/asplos2015.pdf)
- Inter-CTA message passing with L1 loads (mp-L1), no fence: GTX5 4979, TesC 10581, GTX6 3635, Titan 6011, GTX7 3 per 100k. On the Tesla C2075 no fence restored ordering: membar.cta 308, membar.gl 187, membar.sys 162 (Fig. 3). AMD mp without fences: GCN 1.0 2956, TeraScale 2 9327. With fences GCN 1.0 still showed mp because the OpenCL compiler removed the fence between the loads (Sec. 3.1.2). — Alglave et al. 2015, as above
- Published idioms broke. The fenceless CUDA by Example spin lock (cas-sl) read stale values: TesC 47, GTX6 43, Titan 512, HD6570 508, HD7970 748 per 100k (Fig. 9). The fenceless work-stealing deque (dlb-lb) gave TesC 750, GTX6 399, Titan 2292, HD7970 13591 (Fig. 8). NVIDIA published an erratum after the report. — Alglave et al. 2015, as above
- Frequencies depend on stress "incantations" (Table 6). On the GTX Titan, inter-CTA sb and lb were 0 in all 8 configurations without memory stress. With stress they reached sb 6673, lb 2247 and mp 4347 per 100k. On the HD 7970, lb reached 38664 while sb never exceeded 2. The proposed SPARC-RMO-based NVIDIA model was validated against 10930 tests run 100k times each [self-reported]. — Alglave et al. 2015, as above
- PTX is not multi-copy atomic, and unlike HSA/HRF it "does not declare racy programs to be illegal". `fence.sc` "corrects the weak SB behavior seen with membar in previous NVIDIA GPU architectures". Mixed-size data races fall outside the axioms (Secs. 1, 3.4, 3.6). — D. Lustig, S. Sahasrabuddhe, O. Giroux, "A Formal Analysis of the NVIDIA PTX Memory Consistency Model," ASPLOS 2019, doi:10.1145/3297858.3304043 (copy: https://github.com/NVlabs/ptxmemorymodel)
- The scoped-RC11-to-PTX mapping is proved sound in Coq "for programs of any size": about 3100 lines, checks in about 15 s. Alloy testing was limited to 4–6 events; the coherence check took 6.4 h at bound 5 (Fig. 17) [self-reported]. RC11's no-thin-air axiom was dropped because it "contradicts current GPU behavior". The proof covers PTX only. — Lustig et al. 2019, as above
- CUDA Volta independent thread scheduling [secondhand; NVIDIA-authored]: Volta gives "explicit forward progress guarantees that explicitly enable programmers to write starvation-free algorithms", and warp-synchronous programming "has been obsoleted as of Volta" (Sec. 2.1). — Lustig et al. 2019, as above
- A "natural" OpenCL-to-PTX mapping is unsound under Alglave et al.'s PTX model. It is sound (within Alloy bounds) under a stronger PTX model the authors propose and validate on NVIDIA GPUs. Two bugs were found in a published OpenCL-to-AMD-style-GPU mapping, and the work confirms x86 is multi-copy atomic and Power is not (Sec. 1, Table 2) [self-reported]. — J. Wickerson, M. Batty, T. Sorensen, G. A. Constantinides, "Automatically Comparing Memory Consistency Models," POPL 2017 (copy: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/tyler-utah/tyler-utah.github.io/main/files/popl2017.pdf)
- Formalising OpenCL with AMD's remote-scope promotion found bugs in litmus tests, in a work-stealing queue and in the proposed GPU implementation. A corrected implementation is proved sound [self-reported]. OpenCL requires both events to use the *same* scope; the HRF-relaxed proposal (Gaster et al. 2015) drops that requirement. — J. Wickerson, M. Batty, B. M. Beckmann, A. F. Donaldson, "Remote-Scope Promotion: Clarified, Rectified, and Verified," OOPSLA 2015 (LaTeX source: https://github.com/johnwickerson/papers/tree/master/OOPSLA2015)
- HRF [secondhand]: the HSA specification uses HRF, which "extends the concept of DRF to programs with explicitly-scoped instructions". HRF treats racy programs as illegal, unlike PTX. I did not read Hower et al.'s paper. — Lustig et al. 2019, Secs. 1 and 7, as above
- Large-scale WebGPU/Vulkan testing: 106 devices (58 unique) from 7 vendors, about 395.5 billion iterations, 35.4 million weak behaviours. Under WebGPU: Intel 0.2m weak in 105.3b, Apple 9.7m in 104.4b, NVIDIA 10.8m in 125.3b, AMD 14.7m in 60.4b. Under Vulkan: PowerVR 0 in 6.1m. AMD averaged 25.3× more weak behaviours than Intel, and two new bugs turned up on embedded Arm and NVIDIA devices (Table 1). WebGPU atomics guarantee only coherence. I read an anonymised submission draft titled "Project X". — R. Levine, M. Cho, D. McKee, A. Quinn, T. Sorensen, "GPUHarbor: Testing GPU Memory Consistency at Large," ISSTA 2023 (copy: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/tyler-utah/tyler-utah.github.io/main/files/gpuharbor2023.pdf)

**GPU language and API models**
- Vulkan: "SequentiallyConsistent memory semantics is not supported and must not be used", and "Applications must ensure that no data races occur". Invocations outside an atomic's memory scope "may observe the values … in an order that disagrees with the scoped modification order". — Khronos Group, Vulkan Specification, Appendix "Memory Model," https://github.com/KhronosGroup/Vulkan-Docs/blob/main/appendices/memorymodel.adoc
- Vulkan: "The relative execution order of invocations of the same shader type is undefined". A keyword search of the memory-model appendix and the shaders chapter found no forward-progress guarantee. — Khronos Group, Vulkan Specification, "Shaders," https://github.com/KhronosGroup/Vulkan-Docs/blob/main/chapters/shaders.adoc
- WGSL "follows the Vulkan Memory Model". All atomic built-ins use Relaxed semantics, so ordering applies only "among atomic operations acting on the same memory locations". workgroupBarrier and storageBarrier are AcquireRelease. — W3C GPU for the Web WG, "WebGPU Shading Language," https://github.com/gpuweb/gpuweb/blob/main/wgsl/index.bs
- SYCL requires "the same memory consistency model … across host and device code", based on C++ happens-before. However, "the complete set of memory orders is not guaranteed to be supported by every device", and the same is true of scopes. Atomics on one location without inclusive scope are undefined behaviour. — Khronos SYCL WG, SYCL 2020 Specification, Sec. "Memory model," https://github.com/KhronosGroup/SYCL-Docs/blob/sycl-2020/adoc/chapters/architecture.adoc
- CUDA C++ extends C++ with thread scopes. A data race now exists when an access "is not atomic at a scope that includes the thread that performed the other operation" (undefined behaviour). System-scope atomicity depends on device attributes such as hostNativeAtomicSupported and concurrentManagedAccess. — NVIDIA, CCCL/libcu++ docs, "Memory model," https://github.com/NVIDIA/cccl/blob/main/docs/libcudacxx/extended_api/memory_model.rst

**WebAssembly**
- The Wasm model is proved SC-DRF (sequentially consistent for data-race-free programs) [self-reported]. A counterexample shows JavaScript's model is not SC-DRF despite its specification's claim; TC39 provisionally accepted a fix [self-reported]. Memory growth cannot be given SC semantics under desirable compilation schemes, and implicit bounds-check observations "guarantee no synchronization whatsoever". An Arm rmem example (MP+dmb+ctrl, Fig. 8) shows a relaxed bounds check. — C. Watt, A. Rossberg, J. Pichon-Pharabod, "Weakening WebAssembly," OOPSLA 2019, doi:10.1145/3360559 (ACM id 3360559, taken from the Wasm spec macros; extended draft read: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/conrad-watt/conrad-watt.github.io/main/papers/watt2019.pdf)
- C/C++-to-Wasm compilation maps all atomics to seqcst. Its correctness is argued from SC-DRF and covers only the axiomatic part. The Wasm-to-x86/ARMv7/AArch64 schemes reuse C/C++11 SC-atomic mappings (Fig. 12), but mixed-size accesses make full correctness "difficult" to investigate. It is only "expected" to be provable for a non-mixed-size fragment (Sec. 7.2). No GPU target is discussed. — Watt et al. 2019, as above
- The threads proposal: "Currently all atomic memory access instructions are sequentially consistent". Wait traps on unshared memory, thread creation is left to the embedder, and the web example notes "Blocking on the main thread is not allowed". — WebAssembly CG, "Threading proposal for WebAssembly," https://github.com/WebAssembly/threads/blob/main/proposals/threads/Overview.md
- "WebAssembly does not declare data races to be undefined behaviour". Aligned multi-byte reads may tear only when they race with a partially overlapping write or are wider than 4 bytes. The model is "derived from" Watt et al. 2019. — WebAssembly CG, threads spec, "Relaxed Memory Model," https://github.com/WebAssembly/threads/blob/main/document/core/exec/relaxed.rst

**Forward progress: language specifications**
- C++ defines concurrent, parallel and weakly parallel progress, plus blocking with delegation. Whether `main` and `std::thread`/`std::jthread` get concurrent progress is implementation-defined ("General-purpose implementations should"). Visibility of the last atomic value in finite time is also only a "should". — ISO/IEC JTC1/SC22/WG21, C++ Working Draft [intro.progress], https://github.com/cplusplus/draft/blob/main/source/basic.tex (main branch, read 2026-10-24)
- Under `par`, library threads give parallel progress only if `std::thread` gives concurrent progress. A relaxed spin-wait on another iteration is marked "incorrect: assumes execution order". `par_unseq` threads give weakly parallel progress, and calling vectorization-unsafe functions (e.g. a mutex lock) from them is undefined behaviour that "risks deadlock". Implementations may fall back to sequential execution. — C++ Working Draft [algorithms.parallel.exec], https://github.com/cplusplus/draft/blob/main/source/algorithms.tex
- SYCL work-items provide "at least weakly parallel forward progress guarantees"; anything stronger is implementation-defined. A group barrier must "eventually select and potentially strengthen" another work-item, and `queue::wait` blocks with delegation. Commands are "not guaranteed to begin executing until a host thread blocks on their completion". Portable code must not assume sub-group progress. — SYCL 2020 Specification, Sec. "Forward progress," as above
- CUDA C++ "aims to provide parallel forward progress" for device threads. Once one makes progress, all threads in its thread-block cluster (or cooperative grid) eventually do, but "Threads in other thread-block clusters are not guaranteed". `while(true);`, yield loops and spins on automatic-storage atomics may never progress. A host spin on a device flag without repeated CUDA API calls may not terminate, and a kernel on an independent stream "may starve" another. — NVIDIA, CCCL/libcu++ docs, "Execution model," https://github.com/NVIDIA/cccl/blob/main/docs/libcudacxx/extended_api/execution_model.rst
- RISC-V guarantees eventual success only for "constrained" LR/SC loops (at most 16 instructions, no loads/stores inside). Unconstrained sequences "might never succeed", and "portable software must detect the case that the sequence repeatedly fails". — RISC-V International, ISA Manual Vol. I, "Zalrsc," https://github.com/riscv/riscv-isa-manual/blob/main/src/unpriv/zalrsc.adoc

**Forward progress: empirical GPU results**
- An inter-workgroup spin-wait can deadlock because GPUs may delay workgroups (occupancy-bound execution). The XF barrier with 1024 workgroups × 256 threads deadlocked on all 8 GPUs (4 vendors); with 128 workgroups it deadlocked on all but the GTX 980 and R9; with 2 it never did. CPU OpenCL implementations (Intel, AMD) also behaved occupancy-bound. With a ticket lock, the occupancy-discovery protocol reached about 100% recall, and at least 95% in the worst cases [self-reported]. — T. Sorensen, A. F. Donaldson, M. Batty, G. Gopalakrishnan, Z. Rakamarić, "Portable Inter-workgroup Barrier Synchronisation for GPUs," OOPSLA 2016 (copy: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/tyler-utah/tyler-utah.github.io/main/files/oopsla2016.pdf)
- Starvation-freedom by scheduler (Table 2):
  - fair: barrier, mutex and producer-consumer all yes;
  - HSA: barrier no, mutex no, producer-consumer one-way only;
  - occupancy-bound execution (OBE): barrier occupancy-limited, mutex yes, producer-consumer occupancy-limited;
  - OpenCL (unfair): all no.

  Existing GPU applications need more than HSA or OBE alone. OpenCL and CUDA wording is quoted (OpenCL: a "conforming implementation may choose to serialize" workgroups) [secondhand quotes]. — T. Sorensen, H. Evrard, A. F. Donaldson, "GPU Schedulers: How Fair Is Fair Enough?," CONCUR 2018, doi:10.4230/LIPIcs.CONCUR.2018.23
- 483 synthesised progress tests ran on 8 GPUs from 5 vendors. AMD was abandoned because timeouts forced hard reboots. On an i7-9700K CPU every test terminated, at both 8 and 800 threads. On the Apple A12 and Arm Mali-G77, 11 of the 122 weak-LOBE (linear OBE) conformance tests failed to terminate (A12 deterministically on 10 of 11; G77 on over half the runs). This refutes the earlier hypothesis that GPUs follow LOBE. — T. Sorensen, L. F. Salvador, H. Raval, H. Evrard, J. Wickerson, M. Martonosi, A. F. Donaldson, "Specifying and Testing GPU Workgroup Progress Models," OOPSLA 2021, doi:10.1145/3485508
- No violations of weak HSA or OBE were seen in that campaign. An Apple-supplied mutex test did violate OBE on the A12 and A14 [secondhand, within the paper]. With the chunked heuristic, 246 of the 251 tests that separate fair scheduling from LOBE failed to terminate on all GPUs, and GPUs were more prone to livelock than CPUs. On an RTX 4000, cooperative launch and Volta independent thread scheduling passed all weak tests but not all strong ones. That scheduler and the SIMT one were "incomparable". — Sorensen et al. 2021, as above

**CPU ISAs**
- x86-TSO [secondhand; I did not read Sewell et al. CACM 2010]. Herd's TSO model keeps R→R and M→W order plus MFENCE- and atomic-implied barriers, so store→load reordering (store buffering) is allowed. Iorga et al. state the Xeon "implements the x86-TSO memory model" with per-core store buffers. — herdtools7, `tso.cat`, https://github.com/herd/herdtools7/blob/master/herd/libdir/tso.cat; Iorga et al. 2021, Sec. 2 (below)
- Armv8 [secondhand; I did not read Pulte et al.]. The paper is cited as "Simplifying ARM concurrency: multicopy-atomic axiomatic and operational models for ARMv8," PACMPL 2(POPL) 19:1–19:29 (2018). Arm's own herd model, `aarch64.cat`, states one "external visibility requirement", `irreflexive ob`. — reference list of Iorga et al. 2021; https://github.com/herd/herdtools7/blob/master/herd/libdir/aarch64.cat
- RVWMO is weak but "multi-copy atomic", so fences are thread-local and not cumulative. It has a progress axiom, and mixed-size behaviour is "not yet fully understood". Under Ztso, all loads behave as acquire-RCpc, all stores as release-RCpc and AMOs as acquire+release-RCsc. RVTSO-only code "is not guaranteed to run correctly on RVWMO", and RVWMO implementations "should simply refuse to run RVTSO-only binaries". — RISC-V ISA Manual Vol. I, `rvwmo.adoc`, `ztso.adoc`, `mm-explanatory.adoc`, https://github.com/riscv/riscv-isa-manual/tree/main/src/unpriv

**FPGA and HLS**
- On Xeon+FPGA systems using the CCI-P interface, an FPGA write followed by a read of the same location can return the stale value. That happened in about 0.1% of 1M iterations under heavy traffic, breaking coherence. Targeting the same channel is not enough: a write-response wait or a fence is needed, and message passing needs a fence across all channels. Of 583 disallowed outcomes, none appeared in 1M runs each; some of the 180 allowed ones did. Lossy queues were 20–27% faster (enqueue) and 30–40% faster (dequeue). — D. Iorga, A. F. Donaldson, T. Sorensen, J. Wickerson, "The Semantics of Shared Memory in Intel CPU/FPGA Systems," OOPSLA 2021, doi:10.1145/3485497
- Ramanathan et al., FPGA 2017. The paper PDF (nadeshr.github.io) was unreachable; the authors are named as in the task brief, order not verified. The artifact repo's LegUp/ModelSim transcripts for pthreads C with volatile, non-atomic shared variables print "Flag (y) is 1, but data (x) is 0" (message passing) and "r1 1 r2 0" (coherence). Such racing accesses are undefined behaviour in C11, so these show HLS scheduling reorderings rather than a C11 violation. The README says Alloy verified that the paper's scheduling constraints implement C11 [self-reported]. My computation from the authors' CSVs: SC atomics took 1.42–1.87× longer than weak atomics (chaining) and 1.06–1.43× longer (bursting); mutexes took 3.1–8.2× longer. — "Hardware Synthesis of Weakly Consistent C Concurrency," FPGA 2017; artifact: https://github.com/nadeshr/weak_atomics
- Other GPU-side and HLS semantics found: MC Mutants found two WebGPU implementation bugs, one of which led to a spec change, and the official WebGPU conformance suite adopted the approach [self-reported]. — R. Levine et al., "MC Mutants," ASPLOS 2023 (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/tyler-utah/tyler-utah.github.io/main/files/mcmutants2023.pdf). The FPGA 2016 OpenCL-atomics work-stealing artifact (README only): https://github.com/nadeshr/kmeans-stealing

**What this establishes**
- **Allowed behaviours differ by target.**
  - coRR violations were seen on NVIDIA Fermi/Kepler but not on AMD or Maxwell, a behaviour no non-RMO CPU model allows (Alglave 2015).
  - PTX is not multi-copy atomic, while RISC-V is, and Armv8 is per its paper's title [secondhand] (Lustig 2019; RISC-V manual; Iorga 2021 reference list).
  - x86-TSO relaxes only store→load order (`tso.cat`).
  - CCI-P FPGAs can break same-location coherence (Iorga 2021).
  - Wasm makes data races defined, where Vulkan and CUDA C++ make them undefined (Wasm `relaxed.rst`; Vulkan appendix; CCCL memory model).
- **No single atomic ordering is available everywhere.** Vulkan forbids SequentiallyConsistent, WGSL atomics are Relaxed only, Wasm atomics are seqcst only, and SYCL leaves orders and scopes optional per device (Vulkan appendix; WGSL; Wasm threads Overview; SYCL 2020).
- **Weak behaviours are rare and depend on stress.** Measured rates range from 0 to about 38.7k per 100k runs, and some patterns appeared only under memory stress. GPUHarbor saw 35.4m weak behaviours in about 395.5b iterations, and CCI-P coherence loss was about 0.1%. Passing tests therefore does not show a behaviour is forbidden (Alglave 2015; GPUHarbor 2023; Iorga 2021).
- **The portable progress floor is weak, and spin-waiting across groups has failed in practice.** The specifications give weakly parallel (C++ `par_unseq`, SYCL), unfair (OpenCL, quoted secondhand), no stated guarantee (Vulkan), or progress only within a cluster or cooperative grid (CUDA C++).
  - Observed failures: inter-workgroup barrier deadlocks on all 8 GPUs tested, LOBE violations on Apple and Arm, an OBE violation from an Apple-supplied test, and livelock where CPUs terminate.
  - The specifications themselves mark as unsafe: spin-waits under `par`, mutexes under `par_unseq`, host spins without CUDA API calls, blocking on the web main thread, and unconstrained LR/SC without a fallback.
  - Sources: C++ draft; SYCL 2020; CCCL execution model; Wasm threads Overview; RISC-V Zalrsc; Sorensen 2016, 2018, 2021.
- **Soundness results are pairwise; none spans CPU, GPU and Wasm together.**
  - Scoped C++ to PTX: proved in Coq (Lustig 2019).
  - OpenCL to PTX: sound only for a stronger proposed model, within bounds (Wickerson 2017).
  - Remote-scope promotion: proved (Wickerson 2015).
  - C/C++ to Wasm: sketched from SC-DRF; Wasm to x86/Arm: expected but not proved (Watt 2019).
  - HLS scheduling to C11: bounded Alloy check [self-reported] (Ramanathan 2017 artifact).
  - None of the sources read claims that one model (C++, HRF, Vulkan or Wasm) is sound when compiled to CPU, GPU and Wasm together. SYCL requires one model across host and device by rule, not by proof (SYCL 2020).
